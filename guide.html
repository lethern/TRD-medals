What Is The Rising Dead?  

The Rising Dead is a team-based survival game where you control a single Marine and work with your squad to survive 15 waves of increasingly dangerous enemies. Survive long enough, and you may eventually reach evacuation.
At the beginning of the game, your squad parachutes into a zombie-infested town on a mission. Unfortunately, strong winds scatter your Marines and equipment across the map. Your first objective is therefore simple: find your squad, regroup, and recover your equipment.
From there, the team will move around the map together, searching for supplies, gathering class kits and equipment, completing missions, and eventually establishing a base. Later in the game, the squad transitions from exploring the map to defending its base until evacuation.
There are eight different classes, represented by class-kit items that can be found during the game. Some classes are much easier for new players, while others are best left to experienced players. See the Class Guides section for information on each class and how to play it.

Advice for New Players  

The most important thing to understand is that you are weak when you first start playing. Experienced players may have accumulated numerous medals that provide significant permanent buffs, while a new Marine has none of these advantages.
So don't try to be a hero.
Stay with the team. Keep up, and don't wander off or fall behind. A Marine caught alone will usually die alone. If you become separated, press N to ping your location to the team.
The Rising Dead is designed around teamwork. Play as part of the squad and experienced players will generally try to keep you alive, help you with equipment, and give you advice.
Also remember that you don't need to kill everything. Zombies can get back up, and sometimes running is far more useful than standing around shooting. Prioritize enemies fast enough to threaten the squad and keep moving with your team.
Stay with the squad, keep moving, and don't try to be a hero.
Good luck. You'll need it.

A Few Things Every New Player Should Know

Green Slime = Antivenom

If you find yourself covered in green slime, you need Antivenom to cure it.
This is one reason I recommend that new players keep an Antivenom in their inventory whenever possible.

Understanding Ammo

There is plenty of ammunition scattered around the map, and new players often make the mistake of hoarding too much of it.
A standard Marine can ingest up to 18 magazines. Once you are full, give additional ammunition to teammates who need it or bring some back to base for later.
More ammunition can generally be found by exploring and searching the map, particularly:
  • Army vehicles
  • Police vehicles
  • Caches
  • Equipment crates
Shotgun ammunition works differently. You can ingest up to 4 Shotgun Shell items, with each item containing 12 shells, for a total of 48 shotgun shells. Shotgun ammunition is more limited and is generally saved for intense situations, so avoid wasting it unnecessarily.
Don't fill your inventory with ammunition you don't need. Inventory space is valuable, particularly while the team is searching the map.

Recommended New-Player Inventory

As a new player, I generally recommend carrying:
  • Antivenom
  • Armor
  • Sidearm or ammo modifier
This leaves your remaining three inventory slots available to help carry equipment and supplies back to base.

Searching, Missions & Base

The early and middle portions of the game are spent moving around the map. The squad will generally make 2–3 trips exploring different areas, gathering equipment, searching for supplies, and completing missions.
Help carry useful equipment back to base whenever you have free inventory space.
Around Wave 10, the game transitions into the base phase. The squad will generally remain at base and defend until evacuation. Once this happens, you may no longer have a good opportunity to retrieve equipment left elsewhere on the map.
This is why gathering and transporting equipment during the earlier waves is so important: the supplies you bring back are what the team will have available for the late game.

Understanding the User Interface  

The image above provides an overview of the major elements of the user interface. You don't need to understand everything immediately, but becoming familiar with the interface will make it much easier to follow the squad and understand what is happening during the game.

A. Leader & Missions

The Leader's name appears in the top-left corner. Hover over the Leader's name to view:
  • Game version
  • Game mode, such as Survival or Grunts
  • Active game modifiers, such as Veteran or Double
  • Number of missions completed out of 10
Directly beneath the Leader's name are your Orders, which show the missions that still need to be completed.

B. Squad Members

This displays all members of the current squad and their rank. A player's name will be greyed out if they are dead.
Hover over a player's name to see their last known location, which shows the coordinates where they were last seen by another Marine.

C. Minimap, Wave & In-Game Options

The Minimap provides an overview of the map and is essential for tracking squad locations, pings, objectives, and general map awareness. The current wave is also displayed in this area.
The In-Game Options menu allows you to adjust several settings and preferences, including:
  • Add or remove Leadership
  • Display coordinates above the Minimap
  • View in-game commands
  • Toggle team colors
  • Adjust camera yaw, pitch, and zoom
Nearby buttons also allow you to team ping, clear team chat, adjust sounds, view unit information, and check squad ammunition status.
Unit Information can be used to review the medals of the currently selected Marine.

D. Marine Information

This area displays your Marine's class, level, health, energy, kills, and current buffs or debuffs.
It also provides other useful information about your Marine, including stats such as:
  • Armor
  • Movement speed
  • Attack range
  • Other relevant attributes

E. Inventory, Ammunition & Command Card

The Command Card contains your Marine's abilities and available actions.
Directly above it is your ammunition status, including how many magazines and shotgun shells you have and how much Flamethrower fuel you are carrying.
To the left of the Command Card is your six-slot inventory, which displays the items and equipment currently carried by your Marine.
You don't need to understand every part of the interface immediately. Focus first on your Marine, inventory, Command Card, Minimap, and following your squad. The rest will become familiar as you play.

Medals are permanent achievements that provide significant buffs to your Marine. Individually, many bonuses are small, but combined they make your Marine substantially stronger. If you play The Rising Dead regularly, it is worth making an effort to earn them.
That said, earning medals can become grindy. Don't let medal hunting stop you from enjoying the game. Start with the easier medals and work toward the more difficult challenges as your experience and medal bonuses increase. Having more medals will naturally make the harder medals easier to achieve.
Some medals also have prerequisites and must be earned in order. For example, if you complete a 3-man all-mission Survival game without V1 Bronze, you will only receive V1 Bronze, even though the run meets the requirements for V1 Silver. You must already have Bronze before Silver can be awarded.

Service, Battle & Tactical XP

Every game begins with 0 Service XP, Battle XP, and Tactical XP. These are accumulated throughout the game based on your actions and are used as requirements for many of the basic medals.
The exact XP gains can vary, but generally:

XP Type

How It Is Earned

Service XP

Primarily earned through using abilities and performing your role.

Battle XP

Earned through combat: approximately +1 each time you shoot and +3 for each kill.

Tactical XP

Earned through various actions such as searching vehicles, interacting with Bella's bones, and carrying items.
Simply being active throughout the game will naturally increase these values. Leaders will generally accumulate XP more quickly because of their increased activity and responsibilities.

Valor Medals

Valor medals are divided into V1–V6, with Bronze, Silver, and Gold levels.
The progression works as follows:
  • Bronze: Complete the initial requirement.
  • Silver: Have Bronze and complete either one of the two Silver requirements.
  • Gold: Have Silver and complete both requirements.
This means accomplishments can be completed in either order. However, you must have the prerequisite medal before the next level can be awarded.

Where Should I Start?

If you're new to medal hunting, start with the basic medals and work your way toward the more difficult Valor, Oak Leaf, and challenge medals. Their bonuses stack, so each medal you earn makes your Marine stronger and helps make future challenges more manageable.
Some of the advanced medals are intentionally difficult and may take many attempts. Work toward them over time, but don't let medal grinding get in the way of enjoying the game.

 

Boss Guide Back to table of contents

The Rising Dead contains a variety of bosses with different movement, attacks, and abilities. Understanding what each boss can do is important to surviving them.
Note: Some HP values below are approximate and may vary based on number of players and Survival versus Nightmare, Nightmare increases enemy HP by 10%. 

Boss

Approx. HP

Key Traits and Threats

Recommended Strategy

Abomination

7,600

Slow, but bites hard and has a 50% chance to cause a fracture. Its large model cannot pass through tight spaces. Leaves a trail of creep.

Outrun it and use narrow terrain to create distance or block its path. Use explosives / combat skills to kill it.

Lyganesh

15,500

Has extremely long melee range and can kill a Marine in approximately two hits. Attacks have a 75% chance to cause a fracture or concussion and deal 50% splash damage to nearby units. Unlike Abomination, it can pass through narrow spaces. When heavily damaged, it becomes enraged: it moves faster, destroys vehicles in its path, and causes earthquakes while moving.

Do not assume you are safe because it appears to be outside normal melee range. Avoid taking a hit whenever possible.

Etaphreven / Power Rangers

2,800

Colored boss Devourers that hit considerably harder than normal Devourers. Attacks have a 20% chance to cause venom.

Maintain distance and focus fire when they approach the squad.

Kukulkaspis

1,400

Spawns from infected trees when they are destroyed. Attacks have a 20% chance to cause venom.

Be prepared for it whenever destroying infected trees.

Fiend

3,250

Has extremely fast attack speed and can kill a Marine in approximately four to five hits. Can jump over walls. Attacks have a 45% chance to cause venom.

Do not stand near walls during the base phase. Keep your distance and kill it quickly.

Mrityunjaya (MRIT)

Up to 2,400*

HP scales with the number of surviving Marines, ranging from relatively low HP in small-team games to around 2,400 with a full team. Has an RNG-based revive and may revive up to 12 times. Its relatively low DPS makes it possible to tank. Attacks have a 10% wound chance, 15% fracture chance, and 15% venom chance.

Usually more annoying than immediately lethal. Be prepared to kill it repeatedly if it continues reviving.

Slasher

3,500

Similar to a Fiend, but cannot jump walls and may move slightly slower. Attacks have a 20% fracture chance, 22% wound chance, and 25% venom chance.

Walls and terrain are more effective against Slasher than against Fiend.

Hesiod

6,200

Extremely dangerous, with fast movement and very high attack speed. Can jump great distances over walls. Its rhythmic, coiled movement creates a stepped cadence that can make it difficult to target. Attacks have a 25% fracture chance, 27% wound chance, and 50% venom chance.

Keep your distance and account for its unusual movement when aiming. Do not rely on walls for protection.

Hathor / Queen

2,200

A slow but dangerous boss that hits hard and causes an Open Wound with every successful hit. Has an RNG-based revive and may revive up to 12 times. Leaves a trail of Creep.

Prioritize avoiding her attacks and run when possible. Eliminate her quickly when she enters the base.

Sindir

2,950

A burrowed boss that can move through walls. It can cloak or hide when attacked, reposition, and sneak back toward the team. Deals splash damage and has a 40% chance to cause a wound.

Stay alert after it disappears, as it may be repositioning for another attack.

Varuna

Up to 2,200

HP scales with the number of Marines. Essentially a larger, stronger Mutalisk with increased HP and damage. Deals heavy splash damage: 75% damage within 2.5 radius and 20% damage within 7.5 radius.

Focus it down quickly, especially when multiple Varunas spawn together.

Mutated Varuna: Devourer

1,800

Uses a long-range splash attack that can damage multiple marines. Its attacks are slow and easier to dodge at range, but become more accurate up close. May have resistance to rifle and explosive damage. The attack will push the marine.

Keep moving, keep your distance and spread out. Long-range classes such as Marksman and Radio Operator are effective. Maneuver around its attacks.

Mutated Varuna: Guardian

 3,600

A tankier, close-range variant of Varuna. Unlike the Devourer, it will move toward the squad and attack at close range. May have resistance to Rifle and Explosive damage. The attack will push the marine.

Prioritize killing with explosives.

Defiler

1,000

(100 armor)

Fast, base movement speed 3.25. Attacks have a 45% chance to cause venom. Heavily Armored (100 armor)

Expect it to close the distance quickly despite its high durability. Prioritize killing with explosives if possible.

Hellspawn

31,500

Extremely durable, deals heavy splash damage, and can potentially kill a Marine in a single hit. Has medium movement speed. Its large model cannot pass through narrow spaces.

Maintain distance and use narrow terrain to restrict its movement whenever possible.

The Rising Dead has several game modes that change the difficulty, pacing, available equipment, and enemy spawns. These can then be combined with leader modifiers to further change the game.
The easiest way to understand the system is:
Game Mode + Leader Modifier(s)
For example:
  • Survival + Vet
  • Nightmare + Triple
  • Grunt Nightmare + Vet
  • Grunt Nightmare + Double + Vet
Not every modifier can necessarily be combined with every other modifier, but this system allows for a large number of different challenges.

Mode

Key Differences

Survival

Standard game mode and baseline for normal waves and enemy spawns.

Nightmare

+10% enemy HP, +25% enemy movement speed, and approximately 20% faster game progression. Includes additional bosses and increased mission difficulty.

Grunt

Uses Survival waves, but class kits are unavailable. Marines receive increased HP, damage, movement speed, and splash damage, and more items spawn throughout the map.

Grunt Nightmare

Combines the changes from Grunt and Nightmare. No class kits, enhanced Marines, faster pacing, stronger enemies, and the additional Nightmare spawns.

Custom

Everyone plays as the same class. (Demo, Radio, Marksman, etc.)

Nightmare  

Nightmare uses the same basic wave structure as Survival but makes several important changes:
  • +10% enemy health
  • +25% enemy movement speed
  • Approximately 20% faster game progression
  • Additional bosses
This means the team has considerably less time to search, complete missions, and prepare between major waves.

Important Nightmare Differences  

Wave 6 — ~28 minutes: An additional blue Power Ranger spawns.
Wave 8 — ~38 minutes: Varuna arrives earlier due to the increased game speed. Nightmare also adds Wave 8.2, with a second Varuna approximately two minutes after the first. If the first reaches the team around 39 minutes, expect the second around 41 minutes.
Wave 10 — ~1 hour: Begins naturally around the one-hour mark.
Wave 11: An additional MRIT may spawn.
Wave 12: The Drone repair mission requires an additional module.

Inferno   

Inferno deserves its own section because it changes considerably more than simply increasing enemy numbers.

Inferno Modifiers

  • Significantly increased enemy spawns
  • Hydras introduced as a ranged zombie
  • Large numbers of highly lethal burrowing Roaches
  • +25% enemy movement speed
  • +30% ability cooldown and energy cost
  • -25% ability range
  • -3 line of sight
  • Bosses appearing during almost every wave
Inferno simultaneously gives the team more enemies, more lethal enemies, less energy, less vision, and less ability range. It requires a strong team where every player is proficient with their class. Unlike easier modes, one strong player cannot simply carry the rest of the team.
Everyone should be proficient with their role and, when possible, play their best class. Inferno is not a mode where one strong player can carry the game. Every class needs to contribute, and weak links become increasingly noticeable as the game progresses.

Inferno Strategy  

Early Game: Search Before Basing

Do not immediately run to base at the beginning of the game.
Everyone should spend the first few minutes searching crates and covering different areas of the map. Use team pings to identify where other players are searching and cover areas they are not.
Make finding the first Confidential Report a priority. It becomes considerably harder to find later.
Bring these key items to the base early:
  • Class kits
  • Energy Cells
  • Armor
  • Sidearms/Shotguns
Start moving toward base as the game approaches approximately 5 minutes.
The goal is to arrive at base with useful equipment rather than having 12 Marines immediately run to base while most of the map remains unsearched.
Leader will activate -inferno command around 5:30 (before wave 2 starts)
After base, while searching the map together, make an effort to search, extract fuel and clear/remove all vehicles. Many supplies will be needed during the game.

Inferno Team Composition  

Inferno benefits enormously from having the core classes covered and players who understand how their class contributes to the team.
An ideal full-team composition would include approximately:
  • 1–2 Demo
  • 0-2 Recon
  • 1 Radio Operator
  • 1-2 Minigunners
  • 2 Marksmen
  • 1-2 Engineers
  • 1-2 Flamethrowers
  • 2 Medics
Obviously, you cannot fill every one of these positions simultaneously. Treat this as a general indication of what the team needs rather than a rigid lineup.
The team's needs also change throughout the game. You generally do not need two Flamethrowers until later waves, and late game may only require one Engineer. A recon might only be needed for the earl-mid game. Likewise, one Minigunner may be sufficient early, while having two becomes increasingly useful during the mid and late game.
The priority is maintaining the core classes while having enough combat classes like Marksmen and Radio Operator to help deal with the enormous number of heavy units and bosses.

Leadership

Inferno benefits from splitting leadership responsibilities between the primary and secondary leader. Both positions should be filled by experienced players who understand the game and can operate independently.
If available, the primary leader should have the Oak 5 medal, which provides a team-wide buff based on the leader's class. For example, an Engineer leader provides increased energy regeneration to the entire team.

Primary Leader — Nydus Control

At the beginning of each wave, Nyduses spawn on the primary leader. The primary leader should use this to the team's advantage by moving away from the squad at the start of each wave and drawing Nydus spawns away from the team.
Because the primary leader must focus on positioning and controlling Nydus spawns, they will not always be in the best position to direct the squad.

Secondary Leader — Squad Leader

The secondary leader should effectively serve as the main squad leader, making key strategic decisions and controlling the team's movement and positioning.
Delegating leadership this way is especially useful on Inferno because there is simply too much happening for one player to manage everything effectively. Demo is often occupied with explosives and other responsibilities, while the primary leader needs to manage Nydus placement. The secondary leader can remain focused on the bigger picture and keep the squad moving together.
In practice, the roles are simple:
Primary Leader: Draw Nydus spawns away from the team and provide the Oak 5 team buff.
Secondary Leader: Lead the squad, make strategic decisions, and control movement and positioning.
This division of responsibilities keeps the team organized without forcing any one player to manage too much at once.

Class Priorities

Inferno punishes wasted abilities and poor class utilization. Every player should understand what their class needs to prioritize.

Marksman

Marksman is extremely valuable on Inferno because the team needs help dealing with the large number of dangerous heavy units.
While moving:
  • Snipe Roaches
  • Snipe Hellhounds & devourers
  • Help damage bosses
At base:
  • Help kill bosses
  • Kill Roaches
  • Kill Banelings before they reach Demo's explosives
  • Cover Demo while flaring
Combat classes need to contribute meaningful boss damage so Demo does not have to waste explosives on every heavy target. This makes Marksman and Radio Operator particularly important.

Demo

Demo's explosives are an extremely valuable and limited team resource. Other classes should help kill bosses whenever possible so Demo can conserve bombs for situations where they are actually needed.
A second Demo may be necessary, particularly to help convert explosives. CL21s are highly valuable.
Protect Demo and the team's explosives from Banelings.

Medic

Carry a shotgun.
Healing priorities should generally favor:
  1. Heavy Support
  2. Demo
  3. Flamethrower
Use Nanno grenades to slow bosses when necessary.

Heavy Support

Stay near the middle of the team. Maintain vision.
Do not rush too far ahead and do not fall behind. Heavy Support needs to remain positioned where the squad can benefit from the class.
A second Heavy Support becomes useful around Wave 7, preferably using different auras so the team can benefit from both.

Radio Operator

Radio Operator becomes extremely valuable because Inferno places enormous pressure on the team's ammunition, energy, and ability to kill bosses.
Priorities include:
  • Put 1 point into supply drop early and spam it
  • Max Motion Sensor
  • Put 4 points into the fifth skill to assist with killing bosses
  • At base, max supply Drop
  • Use Motion Sensor when anticipating boss spawns
  • Help kill bosses
This may require respeccing as the game transitions from the mobile phase to base defense.
Radio Operator may also require additional energy support or some Engineer attention to maintain the necessary ability usage.

Recon

Recon's vision becomes especially valuable because Inferno reduces everyone's line of sight.
Priorities include:
  • Provide vision in tight spaces
  • Flare corners and dangerous approaches
  • Assist with Crep
  • Assist killing bosses with Drone
  • Search areas efficiently
Reduced vision makes Recon's ability to safely reveal terrain considerably more important than in a normal Survival game.

Playing Inferno Effectively

The biggest adjustment to Inferno is recognizing that resources and abilities cannot be wasted.
You have increased cooldowns and energy costs while simultaneously facing far more enemies. Every skill should therefore have a purpose. Avoid wasting energy on unnecessary abilities, and do not force Demo to spend explosives killing bosses that the combat classes could handle.
Support your combat classes, protect Demo and the team's explosives, maintain vision, and use each class for what it contributes best.
Even a strong Inferno team will fail games. With a strong group and enough practice, however, the mode gradually becomes more manageable and stops feeling as overwhelming.
Know the important waves, use your skills efficiently, conserve your resources, support the rest of the team—and with some luck, you might actually win.

Overview
1-Man Mode is one of the most difficult achievements in The Rising Dead. Completing it awards the ability to change your Marine's color to any color in the game—the reason you occasionally see Marines running around in different colors.
Until a few years ago, completing 1-Man was considered nearly impossible. A strategy was eventually developed, but it remains an extremely difficult challenge even with a good strategy.
Before attempting it, you should already have a strong understanding of:
  • Every wave and when bosses spawn.
  • How to kill or avoid the major bosses.
  • Heavy Support, Medic, and Recon.
  • How to adapt when things go wrong.
  • How to conserve and stockpile supplies for the late game.
A great way to learn 1-Man is to watch successful 1-Man replays from the TRD Discord.
Lindesm is probably one of the best 1-Man players to learn from, and he has several replays available, including runs such as:
  • 1-Man — No Medals
  • 1-Man — All Missions
  • And other 1-Man variations
Watching these replays can help you see the strategy in action, especially movement, positioning, item management, and how to handle difficult waves.
You can attempt 1-Man at any experience level, but realistically, you will benefit from having several medals and the buffs they provide before seriously attempting it.
Expect to fail. A lot. Even with a good strategy, 1-Man often comes down to execution, timing, and a little luck.

Early Game — Waves 1–6

The priority during the early game is scouting the map and moving supplies.
For the first two waves, I generally focus almost entirely on moving useful items onto the roads where they can be collected later. Prioritize moving items from the center of the map toward the outer routes, while keeping your route as efficient as possible. (I see a magazine in a distant location, leave it not worth the delay, magazine in my path of travel, I’ll take it)
The center of the map becomes increasingly dangerous after Wave 5 as enemy density increases. Fighting enemies one at a time along the outer routes is much safer than allowing everything to converge on you in the center of the map.
Once Wave 2 is complete, begin moving supplies into your base.
Around wave 2-3 when you start taking some damage, use the -1man command, this will instantly give you the LBA.

Recon Setup

Once you find a Recon kit, equip it. You will need to carry:
  • Antivenom
  • MPZ
  • Benelli shotgun
  • Nanites
The Benelli is particularly important for the Wave 4 Devourers.
You do not need to kill the Wave 2 Abomination or Wave 4 Lyganesh during this phase. They can be dealt with later during Wave 9.

Base Location

Use the military base near the top-right of the map.
This base is only available in -1man mode.
This base is ideal for 1-Man because:
  • It provides good vision.
  • It has two entrances, making your supply drop offs easier.
  • You cannot easily become trapped inside.
  • There is a secret breakable wall at the back that provides an escape route for evacuation.

Required Supplies

Your goal is to stock the base during the first 6–7 waves.
Try to collect approximately:
  • 50+ ammunition
  • Many shotgun shells
  • 6–12 Medkits
  • 4–12 Fibrin Bandages
  • 10–20 Road Flares
  • 8–16 Antivenom
  • As many Nanites as possible
  • As many Energy Cells as possible
  • Chain Explosives
  • Satchels
  • Remote Controls
  • Nannonades
You will also need:
  • Benelli
  • First Aid Kit
  • Minigun
You don't need Claymores. Or other items. (consider bringing or carrying AP or balltip ammo)

Keep the Base Organized

Don't simply dump everything into one giant pile.
Keep similar items together:
Medkits → Fibrin → Antivenom → Ammo → Explosives → etc.
This becomes extremely important later when you are trying to switch equipment quickly while under pressure. You will not have time to organize base later.

Wave 6

Continue scouting and moving supplies between the map and your base.
Try to stay in open areas whenever possible.
When Etaphreven wave 6 boss appears, kill it using the Drone, or a combination of Drone + Flaregun + Drone Decoy, depending on what is available.
While scouting, continue carrying:
  • Antivenom
  • MPZ
  • Benelli
  • Nanites
After Wave 6, also keep a Fibrin Bandage on you.

Wave 7–8 — Transition to Heavy Support

By Wave 7, you should have most of the supplies you need for the remainder of the game.
Return to base and switch to Heavy Support.
Drop the shotgun and MPZ in the base, but keep:
  • Antivenom
  • Fibrin
  • Nanites
Continue collecting any ammunition, explosives, or other supplies you are still missing.

Wave 7 Setup

Before Wave 7 begins:
  • Level your important Minigun skills.
  • Get Level 3 Ammo Link.
  • Focus on upgrading Suppress.
  • Reload before the Wave 7 Muta rush.
  • Reload before Wave 8 starts
Mutalisks cause wounds, so use Fibrin as needed.
Time your reloads strategically and run from enemies during reloads, don’t stand and take hits.
At the end of Wave 7, time your reload carefully so you begin Wave 8 with a full Minigun.

Wave 8

Wave 8 is another major Muta/Varuna wave.
Use Suppress to melt Varuna and survive the Muta pressure.
Once the wave is finished, return to base and switch to Medic.

Wave 8–9 — Medic Preparation

Skill Medic with:
  • Medical Proficiency — Max
  • Advanced Programming — Max
  • Transfuse — Minimum 3 points
  • Remaining points into Stim and Nannonade
Keep:
  • MpZ
  • Benelli
  • Armor
You will switch between the MPZ and Benelli depending on what is attacking you.

Against Light Units and Mutas

Use the MPZ.

Against Devourers

Use the Benelli and the following technique:
Shoot → Move → Shoot → Move
You will constantly switch between the MPZ and Benelli depending on the enemy you're fighting.
At this point, stick to open areas. I typically use the forest edge, where there are usually still supplies available to collect.
Recycle unnecessary items for energy when needed and gather any final supplies you are missing.

Wave 9 — Shiva & Boss Stack

When the Shiva drop appears, Stim yourself and get to it immediately.
After obtaining Shiva, continue gathering any final supplies while allowing the Wave 9 bosses to stack behind you.
Once the bosses are stacked around forest, take the northern route back toward the military base.
If there is a hole at the top road intersection, keep the bosses following you through the northern buildings.
Use:
  • Nannonade on Lyganesh if he begins separating from the group.
  • Benelli against Devourers.
  • Your movement to keep the bosses stacked together.
Once you reach the base, use Shiva to destroy the left half of the base and the Wave 9 bosses.
The resulting radiation will continue damaging enemies for 2 waves.

Wave 10–14 — Minigun Defense

Immediately switch back to Heavy Support.
Keep:
  • Armor
  • Antivenom
  • Chain Explosives
  • Road Flares
  • Medkits
  • Fibrin
You may occasionally have to switch between medkits and nanites or other items.

Skill Setup

Immediately:
  1. Max Ammo Link.
  2. Reload.
  3. Continue leveling your Minigun skills during the reload.
  4. Max the first and last skills.
  5. Max HEI.
HEI will be used to detonate your explosives.

Setting Up the Base

Once your reload is complete:
  • Flare the necessary areas.
  • Lay your Chain Explosives.
  • Slightly stagger bomb stacks instead of placing them directly on top of each other. (This spreads them over a larger area, making it easier for HEI to detonate them.)
  • Position yourself near the center of your equipment.
  • Maintain clear lines of sight and travel paths for incoming bosses.
You don't need to flare much in this particular base.
While setting up, use Medkits as needed because you will likely take some radiation damage.
Once everything is prepared, get into position for Wave 10.

Wave 10

Detonate your bombs with HEI.
Focus on detonating the inner bomb stacks first so the remaining stacks are easier to reach and manage.
Keep your line of sight on the enemies' approach paths and avoid standing on accumulated bodies.
Level 5 Ammo Link can last roughly two waves, so I recommend reloading immediately after Wave 10 rather than risking a reload during the beginning of Wave 12.

Wave 11 — MRIT

At the beginning of Wave 11:
Reload immediately.
Retreat into the back of the base and run laps around the obstacles while your Minigun reloads. You will likely have several enemies following you. Avoid taking damage.
Once reloaded, return to your defensive position.
Kill everything necessary and deal with the any bosses. Typically a fiend and mrit.

MRIT

Focus Suppress on MRIT.
MRIT will continue respawning, so:
Suppress MRIT → Move away → Kill MRIT → Move away → Repeat
If executed correctly, MRIT should have very little opportunity to land hits on you.
Continue this pattern until MRIT stops spawning.

Wave 12 — Hesiod

Before Wave 12 begins, add another Chain Explosive to each existing stack.
Your stacks should now contain 3 Chain Explosives each.
Flare the dark areas around the bombs and prepare for the wave. (flare the left side first, as there is a close-by spawn location)
Three bombs should be enough to kill Hesiod.
Detonate your bombs using HEI.
Hesiod is one of the biggest threats in 1-Man Mode and is responsible for many failed attempts.
Once Wave 12 is complete, reload and prepare for Wave 13.

Wave 13 — Final Defense

Wave 13 is primarily an air wave.
You will likely be leaving the base during the end of this wave / early wave 14.
Use your medical supplies as needed.
You may need to juggle equipment depending on what you need at the moment:
Drop Medkit → Pick up Nanites → Drop Nanites → Pick up Nannonade → etc.
Continue defending your position until Lyga arrives, wave 14 begins or you need to reload. You will not kill Lyga.
While defending, begin planning your evacuation.
You need to determine:
  • What equipment to leave behind.
  • What equipment to carry.
  • Where your evacuation supplies are located.
  • When you can safely leave the base.

Evacuation Loadout

Before leaving the base:
  • Drop the Minigun.
  • Pick up the First Aid Kit.
  • Carry the Shotgun.
  • Carry Antivenom.
  • Carry Fibrin.
  • Carry Satchels.
  • Carry Remote Controls.
  • Consider Carrying Energy Cells over fibrin or av if energy is an issue.
Ideally, time your final transition when no bosses are nearby or freshly downed mrit.

Evacuation

It is now probably early wave 14.
Break the back wall of the military base and escape through the rear.
Switch to your First Aid Kit and skill:
  • Advanced Programming — Max
  • Medical Proficiency — Max
  • Stim — Max
  • Nannonade — Max
  • Remaining points into Transfuse
Keep yourself Stimmed continuously during this phase of the game.
You will be around Wave 14 at this point, so you may encounter:
  • MRIT
  • Queen
  • Lyga
  • Nydus
  • Devourers
  • Mutas
  • Other light units
At this point, the Nydus spawns are much less important because you are no longer defending a base.
Head toward the docks, then turn toward the middle of the map.
I prefer to begin Wave 15 near the roundabout in the center, which statistically keeps you reasonably close to the Vahana drop.

Wave 15 — Vahana & Final Evacuation

When Wave 15 begins, a random Vahana drops somewhere on the map.
Head toward it using the most open route available.
Vision is everything.
Watch for:
  • Sinder
  • Fiends
  • Slashers
  • Devourers
  • Mutalisks
  • Hesiod
If something spawns directly ahead of you, change your route rather than running directly into it. Your goal is to keep enemies behind you.

Satchels

Use 2 Satchels to kill:
  • Fiends
  • Slashers
  • Sinder
Detonate them with the Remote Control.
If you have a surplus of satchels, use them on groups of devourers.

Hesiod

If Hesiod is approaching:
  1. Throw a Level 5 Nannonade.
  2. Lay 3 Satchels.
  3. Detonate with Remote Control.
The Nannonade slows Hesiod enough to give you time to get away and execute the attack. Don’t let Hesiod get near you, it will jump attack.

Devourers & Mutalisks

Use the same:
Shoot → Run → Shoot
technique.
Your movement speed should allow you to limit the damage they can inflict. Stay Stimmed and do your best to outrun enemies, it may be preferred to only turn and shoot mutas if there are 2 or more, you will likely take wounds, Fibrin Bandages are very helpful here.
At this point, you will consume a significant amount of energy to stay alive, so use Energy Cells or Recycle as necessary.

The Finish

Get the Vahana, then loiter around a high vision area until Hellspawn approaches.
Kill Hellspawn.
Then sprint for evacuation.
If the timing, positioning, spawns, and your execution all line up, you can make it.

There is more than 1 way to play 1-man

There are a lot of small details and variations that you will eventually develop and adapt to yourself. The strategy above is simply what worked best for me.
One example is how I organize my evacuation supplies.
I like placing my evacuation supplies outside the back wall of the base. When it's time to leave, I can:
  1. Drop the Minigun.
  2. Grenade the wall.
  3. Run outside.
  4. Grab the First Aid Kit.
  5. Grab Satchels and Remotes.
  6. Grab any available Energy Cells.
  7. Continue toward evacuation.
There are also different ways to manage your reloads. For example, you can reload at the start of Wave 11 or Wave 12 and potentially avoid needing another reload until the end.
Experiment with the strategy and find what works for you.

The Most Important 1-Man Trick

Probably the single most important tool available to you in 1-Man Mode is game speed.
StarCraft II allows you to adjust game speed during a solo game through the gameplay options. You can even assign game-speed adjustment to a hotkey and use the mouse scroll feature to toggle it.
Slow the game down during difficult moments.
This is particularly useful when:
  • Dropping or picking up items.
  • Switching between classes.
  • Switching between MPZ and Benelli.
  • Detonating explosives.
  • Managing medical items.
  • Setting up your defenses.
  • Reacting to bosses.
Slowing the game gives you significantly more time to make precise decisions and execute complicated actions.
In my opinion, most players who have successfully completed 1-Man have probably used this feature at some point. After watching Bronzemaster's 1-man replay, however, given the disorganized base, I suspect he may have actually completed his run at normal speed which is incredibly impressive. I’m not certain though, someone should ask him.
Even with the ability to slow the game down, 1-Man is extremely difficult, and you should still expect to fail many attempts.

Don't Give Up

There will probably be times when you become incredibly frustrated with 1-Man.
You may have a perfect run going and then lose everything because of one bad spawn, one missed attack, or one poorly timed decision.
Keep trying.
With enough practice, good execution, and a little luck, you'll eventually get it.
It may take 50+ attempts.
When it finally works, it'll be worth it.

Strategy Guides 

Basic Small-Team Strategy

Small-team games require a little more planning and coordination than a standard game. Before the game starts, discuss your strategy in the lobby and assign roles. Everyone should know what they are responsible for before the first wave. The exact strategy will vary between game modes, but the fundamentals below apply to all small-team challenges.

Base Selection

Choose a large base that is not too close to the middle of the map.
A larger base gives the team more room to organize equipment and maneuver during the late game. Avoid bases positioned too close to the center, as the middle of the map becomes increasingly dangerous as the game progresses and can make it easier for the swarm to converge on the base simultaneously.

Early Game — Split the Map

At the start of the game, split the map between the players and begin searching independently.
For 2-Man, divide the map into two sections. For 3-Man, divide it into roughly thirds, using the main roads and roundabout as natural boundaries.
The goal is to efficiently search your assigned area and move valuable equipment toward the roads and back to base.
Prioritize:
  • Miniguns
  • First Aid Kits
  • Recon equipment
  • Explosives
  • Ammunition
  • Medical supplies
  • Road Flares
  • AP ammo
  • RBA
  • Energy Cells
  • M29
  • Shotguns
Don't waste time making long trips for low-value equipment. If something useful is along your route, grab it. Otherwise, keep moving.

Search the Middle Early

The middle area of the map should be prioritized during the early game. It is much safer to collect equipment from the middle during the opening waves than it is later in the game.
Once the mid-to-late game begins, avoid unnecessary trips through the middle. Enemy density increases significantly, and the middle can become dangerous from multiple directions. The outer roads are generally much easier to defend and allow the team to control where enemies approach from.
The goal is simple:
Clear the valuable equipment from the middle early so you don't have to go back later.
Once the important equipment has been moved out, shift your attention toward the outer routes.

Short Search Routes

Don't make your route so long that you end up having to leave important equipment behind, particularly ammunition, Miniguns, First Aid Kits, and other valuable supplies. It's better to make several shorter trips and gradually move the important equipment into base than to make one long search and leave critical supplies sitting somewhere on the map.

Regrouping

When it is time to return to base, arrive together.
This is particularly important in small-team games. Arriving individually can easily result in one player becoming trapped at the base entrance as the swarm begins flowing through the door while the other players are still outside.
Use N team ping to coordinate your return and make sure everyone arrives at approximately the same time.

2-Man Mode

2-Man is one of the hardest challenges in The Rising Dead. While easier than 1-Man, it requires two highly experienced players who understand the game, their classes, and the strategy before the game even begins.
This guide is specifically for 2-Man Survival and is not intended to cover completing every mission. In fact, missions are generally not worth the risk. Skip crep and avoid searching vehicles unless you desperately need a specific item, ammo, explosives, or medical supplies.

Early Game — Split the Map

Choose a large base. There will be more than enough equipment scattered across the map, so your priority is to search as much of the map as possible, quickly and efficiently.
Before starting, decide who takes which side:
"You take left, I'll take right."
  • Search buildings quickly.
  • Prioritize high-value items.
  • Move useful equipment onto the roads you will eventually use to transport it to base.
  • Don't waste time picking up individual magazines or low-value items in distant corners.
  • Keep moving.

High-Priority Items

Prioritize:
  • Ammo boxes
  • Medkits
  • Nannonades
  • Fibrin bandages
  • Nanites
  • Energy cells
  • Remote controls
  • Satchels
  • Road Flares
  • RB armor
  • First Aid Kits
  • AP ammo
  • Heavy Support kits
  • Recon kits
If you find a Recon kit, equip it. Keep an MPZ, Benelli, antivenom, and nanites if available. Bring Heavy Support kits and First Aid Kits back to base. Stack any early-game E-cells you find.
If both players find Recon kits and have an MPZ + Benelli, both players can remain Recon and continue searching/moving items into base well into the mid-game efficiently.

Wave 3 — Regroup

By Wave 3, you should have finished searching your respective halves and begin moving equipment toward base.
At this point, choose between:
2 Recon
  • Better carrying capacity
  • Disguise allows safer base drops
  • More efficient equipment gathering
Recon + Medic
  • More sustain
  • First Aid Kit + Recon allows you to save medical supplies for later
  • Traditionally my preferred setup
Regardless of your choice, arrive at base together.
Small-team players are extremely vulnerable when entering the base. The single entrance can trap you while swarms pour in.
Use -N team ping to coordinate your arrival and enter together. You may need shotguns to clear the entrance.
If running two Recons, meet outside the base, activate disguise, enter together, drop equipment, and leave together.

Organizing the Base

Do not dump equipment around the entrance.
Keep the base organized. You will have very little time to clean it up later.
Use Q to quickly drop items from your inventory.

2-Man Shopping List

By the late game, you want approximately:
  • 2 Minigun kits
  • 2 First Aid Kits
  • As many medical supplies as possible
  • Medkits
  • Nannonades
  • Fibrin bandages
  • Nanites
  • Antivenom
  • ~30 Road Flares
  • Remote controls
  • Chain explosives
  • Satchels
  • As much ammunition as possible
  • 2 Shotguns
  • Shotgun shells
  • 2 Ammo Modifiers — preferably AP or Balltip as a backup
You do not need Claymores, Stims, or unrelated classes/items for the late game. Don’t waste inventory space bringing demo or radio operator to add clutter to the base.

Waves 3–8 — Equipment Runs

After your initial base drop, continue searching the map and ferrying equipment back to base.
If running 2 Recon:
  • Both carry MPZ/M29 + shotgun
  • Armor
  • Antivenom
  • One player focuses on explosives
  • One focuses on medical supplies (carry nanites)
  • Carry an ammo modifier
If running Medic + Recon:
  • Both carry sidearms
  • Shotgun/MPZ
  • Armor
  • Ammo buff
  • Use the remaining inventory for equipment runs
The goal is simple: make several short trips rather than trying to carry everything at once.

Prioritize the Center First

Clear equipment from the central areas of the map early.
As the game progresses, enemy density becomes much heavier toward the center. You want to deal with enemies in smaller groups around the outer map rather than having everything converge on you simultaneously in the center.
This becomes especially important around Wave 5+.

Wave 2 / abomination

Kill the Wave 2 Abomination with:
Claymores + Drone
Use the same general strategy for Lyganesh.

Wave 6 — Eta

Get into an open area and use Recon abilities to kill Eta:
  • Flaregun + Drone
  • Decoy + Drone
Use the same general approach for Wave 8 Varuna.

Wave 9 — Shiva

Gather the Shiva and stack the Wave 9 bosses.
Use Shiva when the bosses are near the base. The goal is not only to kill Wave 9, but to leave the radiation cloud around the entrance, creating a defensive trap for incoming zombies.
Once Wave 9 is dealt with:
Both players switch to Heavy Support. Yes, you need two Miniguns.

Heavy Support Setup

Think of the two Minigunners as:
Minigun Demo + Minigun Medic

Explosive Minigunner

  • No more than 3 points in Ammo Link
  • Max HEI
  • Carries explosives
  • Sets bombs
  • Handles boss detonations

Medical Minigunner

  • Max Ammo Link
  • Max Suppress
  • Carries medical supplies
  • Keeps the team alive
Both players should carry:
  • Antivenom
  • Fibrin bandages nearby or in inventory
Never reload both Miniguns at the same time. The Medical Minigunner should make sure reloads are staggered.
You may sometimes need to juggle your inventory during the defense:
Drop road flares → pick up fibrin → drop fibrin → pick up nannonade.
This is why keeping the base organized is so important.

Waves 10–15

The strategy is straightforward:
Explosive Minigunner
  • Sets bombs
  • Flares the entrance
  • Uses HEI to detonate explosives
  • Saves most remote controls and satchels for evacuation
Medical Minigunner
  • Uses Medkits and Nanites as needed
  • Keeps the team alive
  • Maintains staggered reloads
You are not tanking bosses.
The goal is to kill everything with Miniguns and explosives before it reaches you.

Wave 11 — MRIT

When MRIT arrives:
Suppress/kill → move away → kill → move away → repeat.
MRIT is very weak on small team games, his HP is balanced to the number of alive players.
Do not allow him to close the distance.
Before Wave 12, prepare a 3-bomb stack near the entrance for Hesiod. Consider using a Satchel + Remote Control for a more reliable detonation.

Wave 12 — Drone

One Minigunner temporarily switches to Medic to complete the Drone mission.
Return to base and both players switch back to Minigun.

Wave 13

Prepare a Lyga stack at the beginning of the wave.
A Satchel + Remote Control can be used for a reliable kill.

Wave 14

Use HEI on Nydus to prevent corpses from overwhelming the base.
Once the Nydus spawning stops:
  1. Set a 3-bomb Hesiod stack.
  2. Set a 16-bomb Hellspawn stack for the single Hellspawn.
  3. Use a Remote Control to accurately detonate both.
Once these bosses are dead, evacuation begins.

Evacuation

This is the final—and often most dangerous—part of the run.
There are two approaches I've used successfully.

Option 1 — Two Medics

Both players switch to Medic and carry:
  • Shotgun
  • Antivenom
  • Fibrin
  • Satchels + Remotes
  • Additional medical supplies
Both players should have:
  • Max Medical Proficiency
  • Max Advanced Programming
  • Max Stim
  • Max Nannonade
  • Remaining points in Transfuse
Exit the base and keep both players Stimmed.
Use Nannonades to slow groups of bosses and elite units. Use Remotes and Satchels on groups of Devourers or bosses. Use shotguns to turn and fire into pursuing Mutalisks and other enemies.
Sprint to evac.
This gives you a high chance of getting both players out, but the transition itself is risky. Exiting the base can be difficult, especially if MRIT or another enemy is waiting at the entrance, and quickly changing classes and reorganizing inventories isn't always clean.

Option 2 — Minigun + Medic

If you aren't confident making the transition to two Medics, keep:
1 Minigunner + 1 Medic
The Medic keeps the Minigunner Stimmed throughout evacuation and uses Nannonades and explosives against heavy units.
The Minigunner can suppress enemies, but you'll be slower and can become bogged down.
If things completely spiral out of control:
The Minigunner holds the line while the Medic Stims themselves and runs for evac.
Both strategies are essentially hail-Mary plays. Choose whichever fits the situation and your team's strengths.
At this point, you're not trying to make the run pretty. You're trying to desperately get to the evac.

3-Man Mode

  1. Early Game
  2. Strategy 1 Traditional / Flame thrower Strategy
  3. Strategy 2 - Double minigun
  4. Final Thoughts
3-Man is probably the easiest and most fun of the small-team challenges. It is still challenging, particularly if you're leading for the first time, but you don't need three exceptional players to complete it. A competent leader and three players who understand the basics should be enough to reach evac.
Completing all missions is also feasible in 3-Man, and doing so awards the V1 Silver medal. If you're going for all missions, however, everyone needs to contribute proactively rather than relying on one player to carry the run.
There are two strategies I commonly use:
  1. Flamethrower / Traditional
  2. Double Minigun
Both work well. 3-Man is forgiving enough that there is plenty of room to experiment with other setups as well.

Early Game — Split the Map

Before the game starts, discuss your strategy and assign roles in the lobby. You don't need to overcomplicate it, but everyone should know what they are responsible for before the first wave.
At the start, divide the map into roughly thirds, using the main roads as natural boundaries:
  • Right side
  • Left side
  • Bottom side
Each player takes one section and begins searching.
If all three players are experienced Recons, you can continue searching independently until around Wave 3. Otherwise, regroup around Wave 2. There is little reason to push your luck unnecessarily, especially with Mutalisks beginning to spawn.

Regrouping

Arrive at the base together.
This is particularly important in small-team games. Entering the base individually can easily result in one player getting trapped inside while the swarm starts pouring through the entrance.
Use N team ping to coordinate your arrival.
The goal during these early waves is simple: search efficiently, move valuable equipment toward the roads, and get it back to base. Prioritize valuable equipment, and avoid wasted time on low-value items.

Leading 3-Man

3-Man gives the Leader a little more freedom, but there are a few things worth keeping in mind:
  • Keep your search routes efficient. Rather than making one long trip through your assigned area, use 3–4 smaller loops. This lets you return to the roads more frequently and reduces the amount of time spent exposed away from the team.
  • Respect the Mutalisk rushes. The Wave 5 and Wave 7 Mutalisk waves are considerably less forgiving in small-team games. You have fewer guns shooting, so don't get caught too far out or separated from the squad.
  • Wave 11 MRIT is easier in 3-Man. His HP naturally scales with the number of Marines alive, so the reduced player count makes him considerably more manageable.
  • Don't hoard explosives. You only need to kill one Hellspawn at Wave 15. There are generally plenty of explosives throughout the map, so use them rather than worrying about conserving every satchel and bomb for the endgame. You usually won’t have to convert any explosives so make use of your claymores.

Strategy 1 — Flamethrower / Traditional  

The traditional strategy revolves around collecting enough fuel for a Flamethrower to defend the base entrance during the late game.

Roles

1 Flamethrower / Engineer
  • Focuses on collecting fuel.
  • Stays primarily on the road.
  • Handles the Flamethrower defense later.
  • Opportunistically charges e-cells if running engineer early
1 Medic
  • Covers the Flamethrower while they refuel.
  • Provides healing.
  • Recycles items to maintain the squad's energy and health.
  • Carries sidearm to kill air (benelli &/or m29 preferred+ ammo mod)
1 Recon
  • Searches the sides of the map.
  • Collects explosives.
  • Takes care of most boss mechanics.
  • Focus on missions
This strategy puts a disproportionate amount of work on the Recon player. It can therefore be a good choice if one player is significantly stronger than the others, or if you simply want to challenge yourself.
Generally, put the weaker player on the Flamethrower.
The Medic is deceptively demanding in 3-Man. They need to constantly manage energy and recycle items to keep the squad healthy, so I would not recommend putting a newer player into the role.

Waves 6–9

The Recon handles most of the important boss mechanics:
  • Wave 6: Drone Eta.
  • Wave 8: Drone Varuna.
  • Wave 8: If the base is well supplied, Recon can switch to Minigun and Suppress Varuna instead.
  • Wave 9: Use Shiva to destroy the Wave 9 boss stack.

Wave 10+

At Wave 10, the Recon player transitions into Demolition.
After Wave 12 bosses are dealt with, they can switch to Minigun for the remaining Drone missions and wave 13 (you could also just stay Minigun until Evac)
If staying as minigun til evac: Use HEI to detonate bomb stacks. Keep a few Remote Controls in the base for Lyganesh and Hellspawn, as remote detonation is preferable for these bosses and avoids problems with HEI locking onto large targets.
At Wave 15, the Minigunner transitions back to Demolition, unless they are comfortable playing a Minigun/Demo hybrid.
There is only one Hellspawn stack. Once it is dead, go to evac.
The main weakness of this strategy is that it becomes something of a one-man show. The Recon/Demo/Minigun player handles an enormous amount of the late-game workload while the Medic spends much of the defense maintaining the squad.

Medic Sidearms

Make good use of the Medic's sidearm slot:
  • M29 when the squad benefits from the attack-speed support or air coverage.
  • Shotgun when additional crowd control is more valuable. If flamer and minigun are operating, stick to shotgun preferrably m15.

Strategy 2 — Double Minigun

This is my preferred strategy for a more coordinated team.
The biggest advantage is that you don't need to spend the early game collecting fuel. This gives the team considerably more freedom to search the map and work toward completing missions.
The early and mid-game classes are less important, but for a safe playthrough I would generally recommend:
  • 1 Recon
  • 1 Medic
  • 1 Minigun
The Leader should play Recon and handle:
  • Explosives
  • Bosses
  • Side searching
  • Focus on missions
The Medic and Minigunner primarily search the roads and help carry equipment to base.
Ideally, most of the equipment should already have been moved onto the roads by this point.

Waves 6–9

The Recon handles the important boss mechanics:
  • Wave 6: Drone Eta.
  • Wave 8: Drone Varuna.
  • Wave 9: Stack the bosses and use Shiva.

Wave 10 — Two Miniguns

At Wave 10, the Leader/Recon switches to Minigun.
You now have:
Minigun + Minigun + Medic
The Medic effectively acts as the tank, although they shouldn't have to absorb much damage if the Miniguns are doing their job.
Both Miniguns should optimize for DPS and can run their individual Aura buffs. AP ammo is also useful.
The Leader becomes the Minigun/Demo player and should invest heavily into HEI for bomb detonations.
Both Minigunners should always carry Antivenom.

Minigun/Demo Setup

The Demo Minigunner should keep Ammo Link below Level 3 to preserve movement speed. (be sure to reload at the start of each wave)
The Medic can Stim the Minigunner while they are laying bombs and flares.
Most importantly:
Never reload both Miniguns at the same time.
Stagger the reloads so one Minigun is always available.
Save several Remote Controls for Lyganesh and Hellspawn. Remote detonation is more reliable for these bosses, and HEI can occasionally lock onto large targets.

Late Game

Once the two Miniguns are established, the basic plan is straightforward:
Kill everything before it reaches the base.
The Leader/Demo handles bomb placement and HEI detonations while the second Minigunner focuses on DPS. The Medic keeps everyone healthy and supports the Minigunners while they set up explosives.
You should have enough firepower that very little should actually reach the squad.
Prepare your late-game explosives ahead of time and keep the base organized enough that players can quickly swap equipment when necessary.
For the major late-game bosses, use Remote Controls when possible, particularly for Lyganesh and Hellspawn.
Once the single Hellspawn stack is dead:
Go to evac.

Final Thoughts

Both strategies are highly effective.
The Flamethrower strategy is more dependent on one strong player and requires considerable fuel collection, but it can work very well with an inexperienced teammate.
The Double Minigun strategy requires more coordination but gives the team much more freedom to search the map, complete missions, and build up supplies.
Of all the small-team modes, 3-Man is probably the most forgiving, so don't be afraid to experiment. These are simply two proven strategies, not the only ways to play the mode.
Figure out what works for your team and have fun with it.

3-Man Nightmare

  1. Early-Mid Game
  2. Wave 10
  3. Wave 12
  4. Wave 13
  5. Wave 14
  6. Wave 15
  7. Evac
  8. Leading 3-man nightmare
3-Man Nightmare is one of the most difficult game modes to beat. Unlike regular 3-Man, you cannot rely on one or two strong players to carry the team. All three players need to understand the fundamentals, be proficient in their roles, and operate efficiently.
Completing all missions is possible, but requires a highly effective team and a fair amount of luck.
Nightmare makes the usual 3-Man strategy considerably less forgiving:
  • +10% enemy health
  • +25% enemy movement speed
  • Less time per wave
There is very little room for wasted time, unnecessary damage, or poor positioning. This is a sweaty game mode. Play efficiently and stick to the plan.

Early Game — Split the Map

The opening is similar to regular 3-Man. Discuss roles and strategy in the lobby, then divide the map into roughly thirds at the roundabout:
  • One player takes the right section.
  • One takes the bottom section.
  • One takes the left section.
Search independently, moving valuable equipment toward the roads. Bring any first aid kit, recon or minigun to base. With a strong team, you can remain separated slightly longer than usual, but don't push your luck. Regroup around Wave 2–3 and, most importantly, arrive at base together.
When dropping items in base, keep the base organized. Don’t drop items near the entrance. One effective strategy can be to use shiva on the entrance of your base at wave 9 or wave 13.
Use N team ping to coordinate your return.
During the early game, the recommended setup is:
1 Recon + 1 Medic + 1 Minigun
The Recon searches the sides and handles explosives, while the Medic and Minigun cover the roads and help move equipment.
Be especially careful during the Wave 5 and Wave 7 Mutalisk rushes. With only three players and Nightmare's increased movement speed, getting caught off guard or separated from the team is much more dangerous.

Waves 6–9

The basic boss progression remains the same:
  • Wave 6: Recon kills Etaphreven with Drone.
  • Wave 8: Recon/HS kills Varuna
  • Wave 9: Stack the bosses and destroy them with Shiva.
One effective option is to use Shiva at the entrance of your base on Wave 9, allowing the radiation cloud to help kill enemies during the early base phase. The radiation lasts for approximately two waves, but it will also damage your team, so use this strategy cautiously and avoid unnecessary exposure. Alternatively, Shiva can be saved and used at the base entrance on Wave 13.
Do not overcomplicate these waves. Your goal is to reach Wave 10 with the team alive, the base organized, and enough supplies to transition into the late game.

Wave 10 — Two Miniguns + Demolition  

This is where 3-Man Nightmare differs from the standard 3-Man strategy.
At Wave 10:
  • Medic → Minigun
  • Recon → Demolition
  • Existing Minigun remains Minigun
You now have:
2 Miniguns + 1 Demolition
The two Miniguns provide the DPS needed to compensate for Nightmare's increased enemy health and speed, while the Demolition player handles the bosses and explosives.
Both Minigunners should be properly equipped with:
  • RBA
  • Anti-Venom
  • AP ammunition
  • Medkits
  • Nanites
  • Fibrin
Normally, players may avoid carrying RBA because of its movement penalty. Don't do that here. The additional survivability is extremely valuable on Nightmare, and both minigun players should make an effort to use it.
Both Miniguns should be optimized for DPS and must never reload simultaneously. One Minigun should always be available while the other reloads.
Both minigunners need to toggle Suppress when the team appears to be getting overwhelmed. Do not spam suppress, finesse it as needed, try to prevent the team from taking damage while monitoring your energy usage.
The Demo player should have the necessary explosives ready and focus on boss damage while the Miniguns provide sustained DPS.

Wave 10–11 — Bosses & MRIT

The Demolition player handles the bosses while the two Miniguns provide the majority of the damage.
Do not unnecessarily tank MRIT.
Kite him while the Miniguns suppress:
Move left → kill → move right → kill → move left → kill
There is no reason to stand and absorb damage when two Miniguns can burn him down quickly. On Nightmare, unnecessary damage is particularly punishing, you will have limited medkit items.
Once the Wave 10 bosses are dealt with, the Demolition player switches to Medic for Wave 11.
The Medic can handle the Fiend using Flaregun or Remote Controls if available. If the necessary equipment isn't available, simply remain Demo rather than forcing an awkward class transition.

Wave 12 — Demo → Medic

Before Wave 12 starts:
Switch back to Demo
Medic → Demolition
The Demo handles the bosses and explosives.
Once the bosses are dead:
Demolition → Medic
The Medic and two Minigunners then move out together to complete the Drone mission.
Once back at base, stay Medic.
The next priority is Wave 13.

Wave 13 — Parasites

Wave 13 is an important resource-management point.
The Medic should focus on absorbing as many Parasites as possible while keeping the Minigunners healthy and combat-effective. All three players should have Anti-Venom and Fibrin available.
The Minigunners should continue prioritizing DPS rather than trying to absorb unnecessary damage. Keep ammunition flowing and maintain enough medical supplies to recover from the wave.
At this stage, do not start worrying about having a massive stockpile of every item imaginable. You should already have collected what you need during the early game. The priority now is using those resources efficiently and surviving the remaining waves.
The Leader should prepare a lyga stack (9 bombs) at the entrance of base. Lyga will likely arrive during wave 13 - beginning of 14.

Wave 14 — Maintain DPS

Wave 14 is primarily about killing Nyduses while maintaining the two-Minigun setup and preparing for Wave 15.
Keep the base organized, One minigun can focus on killing the Nydus’ with HEI while the other continues providing covering fire.
At this point, the Medic player has a choice:

Stay Medic

Staying Medic is perfectly viable if you are comfortable detonating the wave 15 bombs yourself with Remote Controls and Flaregun. (good to stay medic if low on medical items)
This keeps the squad's healing available throughout the final wave and allows the two Miniguns to focus entirely on DPS.

Switch to Demolition

If you are not comfortable handling the explosives as Medic, switch back to Demolition.
There is no reason to force the Medic strategy if doing so makes bomb management unreliable. Dedicated Demo provides much more straightforward explosive control.
The team should now be thinking about wave 15 boss setup and Hellspawn towards the end of 14.

Wave 15 — Hellspawn

Before the final wave, you should have already prepared the necessary explosives.
For the final wave, you will need:
  • Multiple outer explosive stacks for fiends/slashers/sindir
  • Two Inner 4-stack explosive for Hesiods
  • Two 18-stacks for Hellspawn
If the confidential report mission was completed, use 1 18 stack and 1 vahana to kill hellspawns
Remote Controls and Flaregun can be used to detonate the explosives, If you need to remain as medic, but switching to demolition is preferred.
Once the Hellspawn are dead, switch back to Medic for evac.

Evac

Once the Hellspawn are dead, move out together.
The two Miniguns should stay close enough to provide overlapping suppression while the Medic keeps the squad healthy.
The final base phase can actually be easier than expected with two properly equipped Miniguns. Their combined DPS is extremely effective against Nightmare's increased health and movement speed.
Don't split up.
Move together, suppress effectively, and evac.

Leading 3-Man Nightmare

The biggest mistake a Leader can make in Nightmare is trying to do too much.
You don't need every item on the map. You need the right items, collected efficiently, and the team needs to stay alive.
  • Split the map efficiently and use short search routes.
  • Bring First Aid Kits, Recon equipment, and Miniguns back to base.
  • Coordinate everyone's return to base.
  • Respect the Wave 5 and Wave 7 Mutalisk rushes.
  • Keep the base organized throughout the game. Don’t leave items near the entrance!
  • Make sure the Minigunners have RBA, AP ammunition, Anti-Venom, and medical supplies.
  • Keep Fibrin and Antivenom readily accessible during base phase.
  • Never allow both Miniguns to reload simultaneously.
  • Kite MRIT rather than unnecessarily tanking him.
  • Don't waste time collecting low-value items.
  • Don't take unnecessary risks.
  • Stick to the strategy.
3-Man Nightmare rewards discipline more than creativity. Once the team is properly equipped, the two-Minigun setup gives you enough DPS to make the late game much more manageable than it initially appears. The challenge is getting there without wasting health, time, or resources along the way.

  • First Aid Kits
  • Demolition kits

Recommended Team Setup  

The most traditional 4-Man Nightmare setup is:
1 Minigun + 1 Flamethrower + 1 Medic + 1 Demolition
This is the most vanilla strategy and probably the simplest to execute, but there is plenty of room for variation.
The Demo and Flamethrower players do not necessarily need to play those classes for the entire game. Both can use other classes during the early game before transitioning into their late-game roles.

Demolition / Recon

The Demo player should generally play Recon during the early and mid-game, switching to Demolition later as needed, or they may equip Radio Operator for a time to kill trees.
Recon helps considerably during the search phase by providing additional carrying capacity and allows the team to conserve explosives by using Drone on bosses.
The Recon should also carry a shotgun and help collect and manage explosives throughout the early game.
Around Wave 8–9, Recon should transition into Demolition and begin converting bombs as needed for the late game.

Flamethrower / Engineer

The Flamethrower player can use Engineer during the early game.
This allows them to help build E-cells, conserve Flamethrower fuel, and contribute additional DPS using SWL.
If possible, use the early game to charge a Level 5 E-cell for the Medic. This can make the Medic's energy management considerably easier later.
If you are using the traditional Flamethrower strategy, ideally have the Flamethrower operational before Wave 9 so the squad is ready for the transition into base phase and to assist with killing large numbers of ground units.

Medic

The Medic should generally remain Medic for the entire game.
Small-team games are notoriously strenuous on Medic energy, and the Medic needs to be proactive about managing it. Consistently recycle items for energy and stay on top of it. Don't wait until your energy is almost gone before you start recycling.
Hopefully the Flamethrower / Engineer can provide a Level 5 E-cell during the early game, but the Medic should be capable of managing their energy without depending on it.
For weapons, the Medic should carry M29 + AP and/or Benelli, depending on what the squad needs.
The Medic's job is simple in concept but demanding in practice: keep the squad alive and keep your energy under control.

Minigun

The Minigunner is the team's primary source of sustained DPS and should remain near the middle of the squad, maintaining vision and constantly covering the team.
The Minigunner should always carry:
  • AP ammo
  • Armor
  • Anti-Venom
During the early game, keep approximately 15 magazines on you.
During the mid-to-late game, maintain a minimum of approximately 25 magazines after base drops.
Upgrade Ammo Link to Level 3 during the pre-base phase so you can carry the ammunition needed for the later waves and maintain movement speed.
Use Solo Aura and toggle Suppress as needed during heavy waves. An effective Minigunner prevents the team from taking damage by killing enemies before they can overwhelm the squad.
Most importantly:
Don't lag behind and don't interact with anything.
A Minigunner doing something other than shooting isn't doing their job. The rest of the team should handle searching while the Minigunner stays with the squad and provides the DPS necessary to keep everyone alive.
This becomes even more important on Nightmare because enemies move 25% faster. If the Minigunner falls behind or becomes distracted searching equipment, the rest of the squad can quickly become overwhelmed.

Moving as a Team  

Once everyone has regrouped, stay together while searching the remaining map and completing missions.
The rest of the team should carry armor whenever possible, preferably RBA. The Medic should have M29 + AP and/or Benelli, while Recon should also carry a shotgun.
Make short trips back to base rather than staying out so long that valuable equipment has to be abandoned.
As equipment accumulates, keep the base organized.
Don't dump ammunition, medical supplies, explosives, or other important equipment near the front of the base. Keep the entrance clear and make sure important supplies remain accessible during the base phase.

Waves 2–9  

The early bosses can generally be handled normally:
  • Wave 2: Kill the abomination.
  • Wave 4: Kill the lyganesh.
  • Wave 6: kill the Etaphreven with the Drone.
  • Wave 8: Kill the Varuna with the Drone and/or HS Suppress.
By Wave 8–9, Recon should transition into Demolition and convert some bombs as needed for the late game.
If you are using the Flamethrower strategy, the Flamethrower should ideally be operational by this point as well.
At Wave 9, stack the bosses, retrieve Shiva, and use it on the stack.
One effective option is to use Shiva at the entrance of your base on Wave 9, allowing the radiation cloud to help kill enemies during the early base phase. The radiation lasts for approximately two waves, but it will also damage your team, so use this strategy cautiously and avoid unnecessary exposure. Alternatively, Shiva can be saved and used at the base entrance on Wave 13.

Wave 10+ — Base Phase  

Once you enter Wave 10, the game transitions into a more traditional style of base defense:
1 Flamethrower/ tank + 1 Minigun + 1 Medic + 1 Demolition
At this point, everyone's responsibilities become straightforward.

Flamethrower

Hold the entrance.
The Flamethrower provides the primary defense at the doorway and prevents the swarm from freely entering the base.

Minigun

Provide sustained DPS.
Stay behind the group and focus on anything getting through or around the main defense. Prioritize air units and burrowing enemies coming over the walls, and use Suppress when the shit hits the fan.
Don't become distracted by equipment or other jobs. Keep shooting.

Demolition

Handle bombs and boss detonations.
The Demo should already have prepared the explosives needed for the late game and should focus on efficiently dealing with the major threats as they appear.

Medic

Keep everyone alive.
Continue recycling aggressively to maintain energy and keep the squad healthy throughout the defense.
Help to organize the base as needed.
With everyone performing their role correctly, the base phase becomes relatively straightforward despite the Nightmare modifiers.
At wave 12 complete the drone mission

Waves 13–15  

At Wave 13, you will need to deal with Lyganesh.
Continue with the normal base defense.
At Wave 15, you will need to kill:
  • Sindir, varuna, fiends and slashers
  • 2 Hesiods
  • 2 Hellspawns
Bosses can generally be handled normally.
The Flamethrower continues holding the entrance, the Minigunner provides DPS and suppresses when necessary, the Demo handles the explosives, and the Medic keeps everyone alive.
Once the final bosses are dead, move out together and evac.

Variations & Final Thoughts  

The traditional Flamethrower + Minigun + Medic + Demo setup is reliable, but it is not the only way to complete 4-Man Nightmare.
There is plenty of room to adapt the strategy around your squad. For example, you can run:
2 Miniguns + Medic + Demo
and eliminate the Flamethrower entirely.
Regardless of the strategy, I would still recommend keeping at least one Medic and one Minigun throughout most of the game. Those two roles provide the healing and sustained DPS that keep the squad functioning, while the remaining two players can adapt around what the team needs.
One weakness of 4-Man Nightmare is that you don't have many backup plans if somebody dies. You can attempt to transition into a 3-Man Nightmare strategy, but making that transition in the middle of the game can be chaotic and may require pausing the game to reorganize classes, equipment, and responsibilities.
Once you find your rhythm, however, 4-Man Nightmare provides an excellent balance between difficulty and an enjoyable game. It is minimalistic enough that every player's role matters, but still gives you enough flexibility to adjust the strategy and experiment with different team compositions.
Have fun with it.

4-Man Grunt Nightmare is one of the most unique small-team modes in The Rising Dead. It combines the speed and difficulty of Nightmare with a completely different style of play created by the Grunt modifier.
You need to account for both sets of modifiers:

Nightmare

  • +10% enemy health
  • +25% enemy movement speed
  • Less time per wave

Grunt

  • All class kits are replaced by other items
  • More items spawn throughout the map
  • Marines have increased HP, damage, movement speed, and splash damage
There are no class kits, enemies are stronger and faster, and the game moves quickly. However, there are also considerably more supplies available and your Marines themselves are much stronger.
The biggest implication is simple:
Inventory management is everything.
You only have six inventory slots per player, and without traditional classes, the squad needs to recreate those roles through equipment and assigned responsibilities. You will need to make smaller, more frequent trips back to base, carefully distribute supplies between players, and avoid carrying anything you don't actually need or that someone else is already carrying.

Early Game — Split the Map

The game begins similarly to the other small-team modes.
Before starting, discuss your strategy, assign roles, choose a large base away from the center, and divide the map into roughly four sections.
Search independently during the opening waves. Move valuable equipment toward the roads and plan to regroup at base around Wave 2–3.
Prioritize:
  • Nanites
  • Anti-Venom
  • Medical Items
  • Armor
  • Sidearms
  • Energy Cells
  • Ammunition
  • Shotgun shells
  • Explosives
  • REMOTE CONTROLS
  • Ammo modifiers
  • Other useful medical supplies
When it is time to regroup, arrive at base together.
Once together, begin making shorter equipment runs as a squad. Grunt Nightmare has plenty of supplies, but your limited inventory space means you cannot carry everything at once. Make frequent trips back to base rather than leaving valuable supplies behind.

Nanites — Do Not Waste Them

Nanites are one of the most valuable resources in Grunt Nightmare.
Bring every Nanite you find back to base.
Their primary purpose should be treating fractures during the late game, when your tanks and other players may desperately need them.
DO NOT WASTE NANITES.
Do not routinely use Nanites to remove Parasites. Morphine through Parasites whenever possible and allow them to heal naturally over time.
Only consider using a Nanite on a Parasite if the Marine is in immediate danger of dying and cannot use Morphine because of insufficient energy.
The same general principle applies to medical supplies throughout the early and mid-game: avoid using them unnecessarily. Maximize Morphine and use your Marine's natural durability whenever practical.
Save your medical supplies for when the game actually becomes dangerous.

Assign Grunt Roles for the Early Game

Everyone may technically be a Grunt, but that does not mean everyone has the same job.
Assign roles before the game begins. These roles will evolve as you progress into the late game
A good setup is:
1 Demo Grunt + 1 Medic Grunt + 1 M29 Grunt + 1 Utility Grunt

Demo Grunt

Responsible for:
  • Carrying explosives.
  • Preparing boss stacks.
  • Detonating explosives during base phase.

Medic Grunt

Responsible for carrying the team's primary medical supplies:
  • Nanites
  • Anti-Venom
  • Other medical items as needed.
There is no reason for everyone to carry the same medical supplies. The Medic Grunt exists specifically to prevent that waste of inventory space. (when leaving base only 1 marine should carry nanites)

M29 Grunt

Acts as the team's equivalent of a Minigunner and provides sustained DPS.
Ideally, this player should have the Able Body medal.
They should eventually carry (mid - late game):
  • M29
  • AP ammo
  • Ammunition
  • Medical supplies as needed

Utility Grunt

The fourth player is essentially the squad's mule during the early and mid-game.
Their inventory should remain flexible so they can carry whatever the squad currently needs moved back to base.

Inventory Management

Inventory optimization is probably the single most important principle during the early and mid-game.
Everyone only has six inventory slots.
If everyone carries Anti-Venom, you waste inventory space.
If multiple players leave base carrying Nanites, you waste inventory space.
If everyone carries 2 medkits, you waste inventory space.
Everyone needs to understand who is stacking what.
Realistically, only the Medic Grunt and perhaps one additional player need to carry Anti-Venom while searching.
Only one player generally needs to carry Nanites away from base.
Everyone should eventually have armor, and during the mid-to-late game everyone should consider carrying an ammo buff. During the early game, four upgraded Grunts with Grenade Launchers can simply gun down most enemies without needing many sidearms. Think of using m29 as your suppress option when you need a bosot in dps
Remember that armor and a sidearm already consume two of your six slots.
Don't fill the remaining four with redundant equipment.

After Base Drops

Everyone should be diligent about dropping ammunition and equipment into base.
After a base drop, your inventory should be relatively empty so you have room to continue gathering supplies.
When leaving base, keep at least:
  • 4 magazines during the early game.
  • Around 7 magazines for an average Grunt during the mid-to-late game.
  • Around 10 magazines for the M29 Grunt.
The point is to stock as much ammo in base as possible

Clearing the Map

You generally aren't attempting to complete every mission in Grunt Nightmare, so searching vehicles isn't particularly important unless you actually need something.
However, the squad should actively destroy vehicles and other visual obstacles when doing so improves visibility and movement around important areas.
Everyone has a Grenade Launcher, so use it.
Before firing:
Use N team ping.
Four Grunts throwing grenades around without communicating is an excellent way to accidentally kill each other.
Clear visual obstacles as you search so the map becomes easier to navigate and defend later.

Early Bosses

Use your available explosives aggressively during the early game.

Wave 2 — Abomination

Kill the Abomination with Claymores.

Wave 4 — Lyganesh

Kill Lyganesh with Claymores.
Claymores have limited late-game value without a Demolition class available to convert them, so use them during the early game rather than hoarding them.

Wave 6 — Eta

Etaphreven can generally be gunned down with the squad's weapons and Grenade Launchers.

Wave 8

Use the same general strategy. Concentrated Grunt fire and Grenade Launchers are usually enough to handle the boss.
Continue searching and clearing the map between waves while making frequent trips back to base.

Upgrading Your Grunt

Your primary upgrades should generally be:
  • Lightweight Proficiency
  • Combat Proficiency
  • Morphine
Put approximately 1–2 points into Grenade Launcher, then consider investing in Link Ammunition.
Grenade Launcher is an excellent ability, but there is actually an advantage to keeping its level relatively low.
A smaller blast radius gives you more control during the late game, particularly when using grenades to clear bodies around explosive stacks and important equipment.
You want enough Grenade Launcher to be useful without creating an enormous blast radius that becomes difficult to safely control inside the base.

Wave 9 — Shiva

At Wave 9, retrieve Shiva and stack the bosses.
Kill the stack with Shiva or Claymores depending on what is available and what you want to conserve.
Another option is to save Shiva for Wave 13 and use it near the base entrance. The radiation cloud can help kill incoming enemies and conserve ammunition during one of the heavier late-game waves.
Remember that the radiation will also damage your team, so position and use it cautiously.
Once Wave 9 is complete, prepare for the base phase.

Wave 10+ — Base Phase

During the base phase, the squad effectively reorganizes into three functional roles:
1 Demo + 2 Tanks + 1 M29 Gunner
This is where Grunt Nightmare becomes significantly different from a traditional game.

Demo Grunt

The Demo Grunt effectively performs the traditional Demolition role without actually having access to the class.
Recommended equipment includes:
  • Explosives
  • Remote Controls
  • Flaregun
  • Fibrin Bandage
  • Armor
  • Anti-Venom
You obviously cannot carry all of this simultaneously. Juggle equipment from the base as needed.
Your primary job is setting and detonating explosives.
Without Demolition abilities, your effective detonation range is much more limited. Because of this, don't build overly complicated defensive layers.
Generally use:
  • 1 inner explosive stack
  • 1 outer explosive stack
on each major path of travel.
Use your Grenade Launcher as the primary detonation method, with Remote Controls and the Flaregun available when you need a backup for detonation during GL CD.

Two-Tank Strategy

Ideally, use two Grunts to tank the entrance.
This is one of the most important differences in Grunt Nightmare and should be discussed before the game begins or during a pause.
The tanks should carry equipment such as:
  • Shotguns
  • Heavy armor, preferably RBA
  • Fibrin Bandages
  • Anti-Venom
  • Medkits
  • Nanites
  • Knife
Again, inventory space is limited, so these items will need to be divided between the two players and swapped from the base as needed.
The tanks should stand as far forward as reasonably possible and hold the entrance.
This is important because the Demo Grunt has limited range for detonating explosives and needs the enemies kept near the front of the base.

Juggling Bosses

When a heavy enemy gets through the explosives, the tanks should juggle the damage between themselves.
For example:
Tank 1 takes damage → uses Morphine → falls back → Tank 2 takes over → Tank 1 heals → Tank 1 steps forward while tank 2 falls back / repeat the process.
Don't allow one player to stand in front of MRIT and absorb everything until they run out of Fibrin and medical supplies.
Rotate.
Juggle and kite MRIT between the two tanks while healing each other.
By distributing the damage between two Marines, you effectively increase the amount of punishment the frontline can absorb while reducing the medical burden on any single player.

M29 Gunner

The M29 Grunt functions as the squad's primary sustained-DPS player.
They should carry:
  • M29
  • AP ammo
  • Armor
  • Medical supplies as needed
The M29 Gunner should remain behind the tanks, provide DPS, help keep the base organized, and move forward to assist with healing when necessary.
Let the tanks hold the entrance while you keep the damage flowing.

Juggling Inventory During Base Phase

You need to become comfortable constantly changing what you are carrying.
For example:
Demo drops Remote Control → picks up Fibrin → uses Fibrin → drops it → picks Remote Control back up.
Or:
M29 Gunner drops Nanites → picks up Nannonade → uses it → switches back.
Use the Q menu to quickly drop equipment.
This is another reason keeping the base organized is so important. If ammunition, medical supplies, explosives, and equipment are scattered randomly throughout the base, these quick inventory changes become much harder under pressure.
Everything should have a place.

Clearing Bodies

During the base phase, clear bodies whenever you have spare energy.
Bodies will pile up around the entrance and explosive stacks. Eventually, zombies can reanimate from these bodies, forcing the team to use more ammunition and making the explosives harder to manage.
Use the Grenade Launcher to clear piles whenever it is safe.
This is another reason a lower-level Grenade Launcher can actually be preferable. The smaller blast radius makes it easier to clear bodies between bomb stacks and around important equipment without accidentally detonating explosives or destroying something you need.
Do this proactively rather than waiting until the entrance is completely buried.
Ping before you use grenade as a warming.

Wave 12 — Hesiod & Drone

Before Hesiod arrives, increase the appropriate explosive stack to approximately 3-4 bombs.
Consider placing an additional inner stack specifically for Hesiod near the base entrance.
When Hesiod approaches, the tanks should move back away from the stack so Demo has room to detonate it safely.
Because several bosses may be arriving, consider using a combination of:
  • Grenade Launcher
  • Remote Control
  • Flaregun
for reliable detonations.
If necessary, ask one of the tanks to help detonate a stack with their Grenade Launcher.
Complete the Drone mission during Wave 12.
If ammunition and explosives are healthy, the medical supply drop is generally a strong choice for the Wave 12 reward.

Wave 13

Wave 13 is a major DPS check.
Ideally, everyone should temporarily carry an ammo modifier, even if they don't normally carry one. The goal is to maximize the squad's damage for the wave.
Use medical supplies as necessary rather than being overly conservative at this point.
The Demo Grunt will also need to prepare for Lyganesh.
Use approximately:
9 bombs for Lyganesh.
If Shiva was saved from Wave 9, this is also an effective time to use it. The radiation can help reduce the ammunition required to survive the wave, but remember that it damages your team as well.

Wave 14 — Nydus

Wave 14 is particularly difficult.
Nydus spawns will flood the base with zombies, and the sheer number of corpses can quickly become a problem.
Clear body piles with Grenade Launchers whenever possible, but be careful around explosives and important equipment.
This is where good base organization and disciplined Grenade Launcher use become extremely important.
Don't allow the front of the base to become so cluttered that Demo can no longer reliably detonate the explosive stacks.

Wave 15 — Final Bosses

Wave 15 leaves very little room for error.
You will need to deal with:
  • 2 Hesiods
  • 2 Hellspawns
  • Fiends
  • Slashers
  • Sindir
  • Other late-game threats
Demo needs to have the explosives prepared ahead of time.
Lay additional bombs for the Hesiods if supplies allow.
Continue using the two tanks to control anything that survives the explosives while the M29 Gunner provides sustained DPS.
If you have the Vahana tactical nuke, use it on one of the Hellspawns
Once both Hellspawns are dead:
Move to evac immediately.
Don't stay in the base longer than necessary.

Most Important Supplies

Two resources deserve special attention throughout the entire game:
Nanites and Shotgun Shells.
Avoid wasting either during the early and mid-game.
Nanites become extremely valuable for fractures and emergency medical situations during the late game, while Shotgun Shells become increasingly important once the tanks are holding the entrance.
There may appear to be an abundance of supplies early because of the Grunt modifier, but that doesn't mean the supplies you actually need will remain abundant.
Conserve the important ones.

Final Thoughts

4-Man Grunt Nightmare completely turns the traditional playstyle on its head.
You don't have a Medic, Demolition, or Heavy Support class to rely on. Instead, four Grunts have to recreate those roles through equipment, positioning, teamwork, and inventory management.
The most important principles are:
  • Optimize every inventory slot.
  • Assign roles before the game begins.
  • Make frequent base drops.
  • Do not waste Nanites.
  • Conserve Shotgun Shells.
  • Use Morphine instead of unnecessarily consuming medical supplies.
  • Keep the base organized.
  • Juggle damage between the two tanks.
  • Clear bodies before they become a problem.
  • Communicate before using Grenade Launchers.
This playstyle can take some getting used to, especially for players accustomed to relying on traditional classes. Everyone needs to understand their role, what they are responsible for carrying, and when they need to adapt.
Plan it out ahead of time, optimize your inventory, work together, and you will prevail.

Missions are optional objectives scattered throughout The Rising Dead. Completing them can provide equipment, ammunition, skill points, upgrade bosses, or other benefits.
Some leaders will need to complete the missions for achievements and medals. The goal should always be to complete all the missions!
Not every mission needs to be completed immediately. Some are safe to complete early, while others can trigger dangerous enemies or events. Pay attention to the squad's current situation before starting a mission.

Ambulance or Police Car Mission

Search ambulances or police cars throughout the map to complete this mission.
Completing the mission allows respawned Mini Marines to eventually graduate back into regular Marines. A Mini Marine generally needs to survive approximately 2 waves before being promoted.
This mission becomes particularly valuable when the squad has several experienced players stuck as Mini Marines.
If several experienced players are waiting to be promoted, it may be worth changing your route to reach an ambulance or police car sooner.

Abomination Bone Sample

Search the remains of the Wave 2 Abomination or Wave 4 Lyganesh to begin this mission.
Searching the sample triggers a random event, which can include:
  • A large zombie/Nydus wave.
  • A Queen spawning from an egg.
Because the outcome can be dangerous, consider the squad's current strength before searching the sample.
It is helpful to have at least one of the following ready:
  • Firebat
  • Minigun
  • Several Marines with shotguns
  • Radio Operator with Napalm available

Positioning

Don't trigger the event while the squad is weak or spread across the area.
If possible, move the squad away from the bones before searching them. This gives the resulting enemies fewer directions from which to approach and makes the event easier to manage.
Bonus pro tip: let recon disguise and search the bone while the rest of the squad moves safely away from the area.
Don't trigger the bone sample blindly. Make sure the squad is ready for whatever comes out.

Bella's Bone Marrow Mission

There are 3 bone marrows randomly placed around the map.
First, find the dog Bella and interact with her. Bella will then follow the player who she bonded with initially. She will help locate the buried bone marrows and caches.

Finding the Bones

Travel around the map with Bella and watch for her reaction.
When Bella gets within range of a bone marrow or buried cache, she will:
  • Bark
  • Point her nose toward the item
  • Bella will always Prioritize bone marrows if both a marrow and cache are nearby
Follow the direction Bella is pointing to narrow down the location. If trees, buildings, or other obstacles block your view, use a Flaregun to illuminate the area.
Once you locate a bone marrow, search it and continue searching until all 3 marrows are recovered.

Keep Bella Alive

Bella can be accidentally killed, especially when she is standing near enemies or explosives.
On Veteran modus, killing Bella also triggers Bella's Revenge, causing a future random boss to spawn with double HP.
If Bella is found late in the game, finding the remaining marrows can become much more difficult. A player with good mobility and vision can be particularly useful for searching the map with her. (reconnaissance class)

What Happens If You Ignore Bella?

If Bella is completely ignored, she can eventually reach the edge of the map, transform into a strong armored dog boss, and attack the squad later.

Infected Tree Mission

There are 8 randomly spawned infected trees on the map.
Destroying an infected tree has a random chance to spawn a Kuku boss. Because of this, destroying trees too early can create unnecessary problems, especially when the squad is weak or spread out in the early game.
A good general rule is:
Destroy trees that are blocking the squad or causing problems, but don't go hunting every tree immediately.

Tree Scaling

Infected trees become stronger as the waves progress:
  • Their HP increases.
  • Their surrounding creep increases.
  • Destroying them becomes increasingly important.
Eventually, all 8 infected trees need to be destroyed.

100%
Because of this, don't blindly destroy the final trees one at a time.
Ideally, locate the remaining trees first and coordinate the final kills so they can be destroyed quickly. This is an ideal task for the Radio Operator to complete using global Mk45 skill.
For example, triggering an unlimited Kuku spawn while the squad is already preparing for several bosses during Wave 6 Veteran can create a much more difficult situation than necessary.

Mission Reward

Once all 8 infected trees are destroyed, an ammunition drop will appear at a random location on the map. (the amount of ammo will depend on the size of the squad)
Try to complete the mission before Wave 9 whenever practical so the squad has a better opportunity to collect and use the ammunition.

Confidential Reports — Crep

There are approximately 20 crates scattered around the map. A crate is different from a cache found by bella. A crate looks like a sack of equipment. They spawn randomly, at the start of each game. One contains the first Confidential Report (Crep).
Once the first report is found, it provides the location of the next report. Follow the chain until all 3 reports are recovered.

How CREP Works

  1. Search crates until you find the first CREP.
  2. Decode the report.
  3. It provides coordinates for the next report.
  4. Travel to those coordinates.
  5. Find and decode the next report.
  6. Repeat until all 3 reports are recovered.
You can manually enter the coordinates into chat (ex. -p 114 208), or use type:
-p crep
to ping the next location for the squad. (as long as the crep is in your inventory)
You can also enable coordinates through the in-game options menu. Once enabled, clicking on the map will display the coordinates of the selected location.

Rewards

Completing the mission rewards the squad with:
  • Liquid Body Armor
  • Vahana Tactical Nuke on Nightmare
This makes the Crep mission one of the more valuable missions in the game.

Important

If the first Crep has not been found, don't travel past an unsearched crate. The first report can be inside any of them.
On Veteran, approaching a Crep can also cause a Nydus to spawn nearby, so don't blindly run toward the objective.
Find the report, decode it, follow the coordinates, and repeat.

Dogtag Mission

The Dogtag mission activates when Marines die.
Dead Marines leave behind dogtags that can be recovered by other players.
To help locate a dead Marine, hover over their name at the top of the screen. This shows their last known coordinates—the last location where another Marine had vision of them.
Use those coordinates, along with the Marine's likely route, to work out where their dogtag may be located.

Why Collect Dogtags?

Dogtags provide a passive damage buff.
Because of this, collected dogtags can be particularly useful for Heavy Support to carry when several have been recovered.
If someone dies, their dogtag isn't just a memorial. Go get it.

Guts Mission

When the Wave 6 Etaphreven (eta) boss dies, it leaves behind guts.
Etaphreven is the large purple boss dog (aka power rangers).
Searching the guts triggers the future spawn of an armored dog boss called Devi.

Important Difference by Difficulty

  • Regular Survival: You receive an audio cue when Devi spawns.
  • Veteran: There is no audio warning.
Be prepared for Devi after completing the mission, particularly on Veteran where there is no warning.

Varuna Hatchling Mission

When the Wave 8 Varuna dies, it releases several hatchlings called Varulets.
To complete the mission, the squad must interact with at least one Varulet. This can be surprisingly difficult because teammates may accidentally kill them, the squad may still be fighting the wave, and the Varulets will eventually wander off.
Don't kill the Varulets yet—someone needs to interact with one.

Mission Reward

Successfully interacting with a Varulet:
  • Grants skill points.
  • Causes the next Varuna spawn to become a Mutated Varuna.

Dealing With the Remaining Varulets

Completing the mission does not mean you can ignore the remaining Varulets. Either interact with them or kill them before they escape.
Any Varulet that reaches the edge of the map will eventually become another Mutated Varuna. Each escaping Varulet becomes its own Mutated Varuna, so on Triple, allowing 12 Varulets to escape could mean dealing with 12 Mutated Varunas.
Varulets generally wander around the map before eventually making their way toward the edge. You may need to hunt them down, which is a great job for the Marksman.
If no Varulets are interacted with and the mission is not completed, a Mutated Varuna will spawn at a random time.

Mission Complete: When Does the Mutated Varuna Spawn?

When the mission is successfully completed, the timing depends on the game mode:
  • Survival: The Mutated Varuna spawns directly after the Wave 12 Drone mission, where the Drone will naturally kill it. This makes completing the mission generally beneficial—the squad receives the skill points while the Drone handles the resulting Mutated Varuna.
  • Nightmare: If the mission is completed during the first Wave 8 Varuna, the Mutated Varuna will likely appear as the second Wave 8 Varuna (Wave 8.2).
  • Generally: the next normal Varuna spawn will be mutated.

Mutated Varuna Types

Each Mutated Varuna has a 50% chance of being either:
  • Devourer — Uses a long-range splash attack.
  • Guardian — A much tankier variant with significantly more HP.
The important thing is to interact with at least one Varulet to complete the mission, then don't let the others escape.

Drone Mission

After Wave 12 spawns are winding down and bosses are dead, the squad can repair the crashed Drone.
The mission begins by interacting with the Drone mission objective that appears in the base during Wave 12.
Once activated, Drone Modules are scattered around the crashed Drone. Find all of the modules and return them to the Drone to complete the repair.

Drone Supply Drop

Once the Drone is repaired, the Leader receives a notification to choose a supply drop:
  • Ammunition
  • Medical Supplies
  • Explosives
Choose whichever resource the squad needs most. (it’s usually ammo)

When to Call the Drop

The supply drop lands near the center of the squad.
Because of this, it is usually best to wait until the squad is close to returning to base before calling the drop.
Calling it too early can cause the supplies to land far from the base or in an inconvenient location.

Mutated Varuna

The repaired Drone also automatically kills the Mutated Varuna associated with the Wave 8 Hatchling mission.

Failure to Complete the Drone Mission

The Drone was originally sent to deal with a massive wave of Mutalisks and Varunas arriving during Wave 13. If the squad fails to complete the Drone mission, expect to face several additional Varunas during Wave 13.

Mission Reward

Completing the Drone mission also gives the squad an opportunity to replenish supplies. Choose the reward based on what the team needs most at that point in the game.
Double / Triple
With Double or Triple, you will get additional Varulets, but each Varulet that morphs will become one Mutated Varuna.

1. Leadership Overview

Leadership is about keeping the squad moving, making decisions, and adapting to whatever the game throws at you.
You control the squad's general route, pace, priorities, and defensive strategy, but you don't have to do everything yourself. A good Leader uses the strengths of the entire squad to get things done.
You will constantly face decisions where there isn't an obvious answer:
The Confidential Report is on the other side of the map, the last ambulance is somewhere else, and Wave 8 is almost over. Do we have time to get both? Which one matters more? Where should we go?
Leadership is about making the best decision with the information you have, then adapting when things don't go according to plan.
Don't be intimidated by Leadership. You will make mistakes, and that's part of learning. Once you understand the fundamentals, leadership becomes one of the most engaging roles in the game—you are constantly solving problems, helping your squad, and finding ways to turn bad situations around.
You don't have to do everything yourself. You just need to make sure the important things get done.
The Leader is identified by the red holographic icon around their Marine
Once you understand the fundamentals, Leadership becomes one of the most engaging roles in the game. You are constantly making decisions, adapting to the squad, and solving problems—and there is nothing more satisfying than recovering from a situation that looked completely lost.

2. Why Should You Lead?

There are several good reasons to learn Leadership:
  • Leadership medals provide powerful buffs that make your Marine faster, stronger, and more capable.
  • Leadership teaches you the map, missions, waves, and overall flow of the game, which also makes you a better player.
  • Leadership gives you the opportunity to teach new players and help grow the community.
  • There are medals specifically tied to leading new players through Wave 9 and to evacuation.
  • Most importantly, Leadership allows you to directly influence the outcome of the game.

3. Setting Up Leadership

Before trying to become Leader, open the Options menu in the bottom-left corner near the minimap.
There are two important settings:

Leadership Selection

Enabling Leadership Selection puts you into the pool of players who can become Leader when command changes.
If the current Leader dies, becomes AFK, or the squad distrusts them, eligible players are considered based on experience. Among players with Leadership enabled, the player with the highest XP is normally selected.
If a Sub-Leader has been assigned, they take priority in the chain of command.

Officer Candidate School

Officer Candidate School prioritizes you for Leadership when command changes.
Among eligible Officer Candidates, the player with the lowest XP is selected. It requires at least 4,000 XP and one evacuation.
This is particularly useful for newer players who want to earn Leadership medals, because it allows lower-ranking players to take priority over more experienced players.
If you want to learn Leadership, turn on both Leadership Selection and Officer Candidate School.

4. Leader Commands

The Leader has several commands that help organize and control the squad.

Set Designated Location — XR

Sets the squad's designated location, represented by the white dot on the map.
This is one of your most important tools. Use it frequently to tell the squad where they are going next.

Explosive Permission — XB

Allows you to give or remove explosive permission from individual squad members.
This is primarily a safety tool.
For example, if a Radio Operator repeatedly ignores requests to stop dropping explosives near the squad, you can remove their explosive permission.
You may also need to decide whether an inexperienced player should receive explosive permission. I generally don't mind giving new players permission if they have experience with similar games, while experienced players who happen to spawn as a Private because of a damaged or missing bank should obviously still be trusted.

Assign Sub-Leader — XM

Assigns a Sub-Leader who will take priority in the chain of command if Leadership changes.
This is particularly useful when you have a trusted experienced player in the squad.

Change Leader — XT

Allows the Leader to transfer command.

Kick / Votekick

The -kick command starts a vote for the squad to decide whether a player should be removed from the game.
This should generally be reserved for serious situations:
  • Chronically AFK with marines waiting to respawn
  • Toxic or abusive behavior
  • Sabotage
  • Team killing
  • Purposefully Destroying equipment
  • Repeatedly disrupting the squad or violating standard video game code of conduct
Don't kick someone simply because they don't understand something.
When I was new, I once carried an M15 while playing Firebat. Experienced players repeatedly told me to drop it, but I didn't understand that I couldn't use it. Instead of explaining why, the Leader kicked me. I stopped playing the game for a long time afterward.
Eventually, another Leader took the time to explain the game to me, and that experience is a big part of why I stayed with The Rising Dead.
Teach before you punish.
Don't be a tyrant. Use the command when it benefits the squad, not simply because you can.

Teamkill Decision

If a player kills another player, the Leader will receive a notification asking whether to swap the Marine who caused the kill with the Marine who was killed.
This allows the Leader to decide how to handle the situation and helps keep things fair when a teamkill is accidental or the result of reckless behavior.
Use your judgment. Not every teamkill deserves the same response.

Undsertanding the Distrust Leader function — X+F

If the Leader becomes unresponsive or is clearly incapable of leading, squad members can use the Distrust Leader command. Multiple players must use it until the game's threshold is reached, at which point Leadership transfers to the next eligible player.

5. Choosing Your Base

At the beginning of the game, several randomly generated bases will appear on the map.
As Leader, use XR to designate the base you want the squad to use.
Consider:
  • Size
  • Distance from the center
  • Visibility
  • Defensive layout
I generally prefer a large base away from both the center and the outer edge of the map. Bases too close to the center can have late-game bosses arriving from multiple directions at once, making them difficult to defend. Bases too close to the edge have the opposite problem: bosses can reach the team very quickly, sometimes while Demo is still setting up defenses.
Once you've selected a base, set the designated location and have everyone regroup there.

6. Starting as a New Leader

Don't overthink it.
Many experienced players actually enjoy playing with new Leaders because it creates a different experience and forces everyone to adapt to unique situations.
If you want to start leading:
  1. Enable Leadership Selection.
  2. Enable Officer Candidate School.
  3. Speak up and let the lobby know you're willing to lead.
  4. If selected, choose a base.
  5. Set the designated location.
  6. Regroup the squad.
  7. Choose your class.
  8. Start moving.

What Class Should You Lead?

Demolition is one of the strongest classes to lead with because you can control troop movement around heavy enemies and elites, prepare defenses, and personally handle important combat situations.
However, you don't need to start with Demolition.
If you're new to Leadership, Medic can be an easier class to learn with while you become comfortable directing the squad. There is also an achievement for leading with all classes, so don't feel obligated to always lead as Demolition.

7. Leading the Squad

Once everyone reaches the base, have the squad drop off equipment and regroup.
If you intend to add a game modifier (-vet, -double, -triple, -inferno…) you will need to type the command before wave 2 at around 5:40ish. Its best to wait until the last few seconds before initiating the command to allow squad some time to regroup.
Then start moving.
Don't feel overwhelmed by the missions. Your primary objective during the early game is to search the map and gather enough equipment to survive until evacuation.
If the squad searches the map effectively, most missions will naturally get completed along the way.
You do not need to personally solve every mission.
For example:
  • Recon can search the sides of the map, work on Confidential Reports, and help find Bella.
  • Radio Operator can destroy infected trees.
  • Everyone can search ambulances and police cars.
  • Everyone can help search for Bella's bones.
  • Players can bring useful equipment back to the base.
Your job is to coordinate this activity, not personally do all of it.

8. Choosing a Route

Every Leader develops their own routing style.
Some Leaders use a predictable pattern such as:
Inner → Outer → middle area
Others might run:
Top Inner → Bottom Outer → Top Outer → Bottom Inner → Middle area
You generally have enough time for two or three major excursions with the squad and can cover most or all of the map.
You can also route around specific missions, such as ambulances or Confidential Reports, but this is often unnecessary if you have an effective Recon player.
The goal isn't to find the perfect route. The goal is to efficiently cover the map while keeping the squad together.

9. Use the Designated Location

Set the designated location frequently.
When the squad arrives at the current location, promptly place the next one.
This creates a rhythm:
Move → search → regroup → new location → move again.
Over time, the squad learns to anticipate your movement and becomes much more efficient.
Keep the squad on route, but allow enough time for players to:
  • Search buildings
  • Collect fuel
  • Complete missions
  • Pick up useful equipment
Don't rush the squad so aggressively that everyone leaves useful supplies behind.

Be Consistent

A consistent route is especially important for Recon.
Recon often operates far away from the squad. If they know roughly where the squad is going, they can judge how far they can safely travel and where they will eventually intersect with the squad.
If you suddenly change directions without warning, you can leave a Recon stranded—especially on difficult modes.
A predictable Leader makes Recon's job much easier.

10. Understanding Missions

You don't need to personally complete every mission. You need to understand which missions matter and when they are worth prioritizing.

Ambulance & Police Car Mission

Search the ambulances and police cars for reports.
Once completed, respawned Mini Marines can eventually be graduated back into regular Marines. (they need to survive approximately 2 waves before promotion)
This mission becomes especially important if the squad has several experienced respawned Mini Marines.
If you have several experienced players stuck as Mini Marines, I will often alter my route to reach the ambulances or police cars and restore them as soon as possible.

Abomination Bone Sample

The squad must search the remains of the Wave 2 Abomination or Wave 4 Lyganesh.
Searching the sample triggers a random event that can lead to a large zombie/Nydus wave or a Queen spawning from an egg.
Consider the squad's current capabilities before triggering it.
It is helpful to have:
  • Firebat ready or…
  • Minigun ready or…
  • Several Marines with shotguns
  • Radio Operator's Napalm available
Don't trigger this blindly when the squad is weak or separated. Try to move the squad away from the searched bones before triggering, allowing the wave to come from one direction instead of coming from all directions.

Bella's Marrow Mission

There are three bone marrows randomly placed around the map.
First, the squad must find Bella. Once someone interacts with her, she follows that player and can locate the buried bones and caches.
Bella will bark and point toward what she detects, she will prioritize bone marrows when both a marrow and cache are nearby.
This mission can be difficult because Bella can be accidentally killed—especially around explosives.
If Bella is found late in the game, a proactive Recon can be extremely useful for searching the remaining areas with her.
If you completely ignore Bella, she can eventually reach the edge of the map, transform into a strong armored dog boss, and attack the squad later.
On Veteran, killing Bella also triggers Bella's Revenge, causing a future random boss to spawn with double HP.

Infected Tree Mission

There are eight randomly spawned infected trees.
Each destroyed tree has a chance to spawn a Kuku boss, so don't mindlessly destroy trees early in the game when the squad is weak and spread out.
My general approach is:
Destroy trees that are blocking the squad or causing a problem, but don't go hunting every tree immediately.
The trees become stronger as the waves progress, so eventually they need to be cleared.
Once all the trees are destroyed, the squad receives a large ammunition drop somewhere on the map.
Be aware that when only five trees remain, there is the chance of an unlimited Kuku spawn that will increase with each tree killed. Kill the last 5 trees together, as quickly as possible.

Confidential Reports

There are 20 crates scattered around the map, and one contains the first Confidential Report.
Once found, the report provides the location of the next report. Continue this process until all three reports are recovered.
You can manually enter the coordinates or use:
-p crep
to ping the location for the entire squad.
Completing the mission rewards Liquid Body Armor, on nightmare it will also drop a vahana.
This is one of the missions I generally prioritize, and it is particularly well suited to Recon.
If the Confidential Report has not been found, don't travel past an unsearched crate. The squad needs to search it.

Dogtag Mission

This optional mission activates when Marines die.
Dead Marines leave behind dogtags that must be recovered.
You can hover over a fallen Marine's name at the top of the screen to see their last known coordinates. This is the last location that another marine had vision of the dead marine. With some detective work, you can often figure out the route they were taking and locate their tag.
Dogtags also provide a passive damage buff, making them useful for Heavy Support to carry when several have been collected.

Wave 6 — Guts Mission

When the Wave 6 Etaphreven (eta.) boss dies, it leaves guts behind. Eta.. is the purple power ranger (big boss dog)
Searching the guts triggers a randomly spawned armored dog called Devi at a later time during the game.
On regular Survival, you will receive an audio cue when Devi spawns. On Veteran, there is no audio warning.

Wave 8 — Varuna Hatchling Mission

When the Wave 8 Varuna dies, it releases several hatchlings called Varulets. The squad must interact with at least one Varulet to complete the mission and receive the skill points.
This can be surprisingly difficult because teammates may accidentally kill them, the Varulets immediately try to escape, and the squad may still be dealing with the wave.
Don't accidentally shoot the Varulets. Make sure someone is responsible for interacting with one.
Once the mission is complete, interact with the remaining Varulets or kill them. Any Varulet that reaches the edge of the map will escape and eventually become an additional Mutated Varuna.
Completing the mission also causes the next Varuna spawn to become a Mutated Varuna. There is a 50% chance it will be either a Devourer or Guardian.
On Survival, the Mutated Varuna spawns directly after the Wave 12 Drone mission, where the Drone will naturally kill it. On Nightmare, it will likely be the second Wave 8 Varuna (Wave 8.2).
If the mission is not completed and the Varulets escape, a Mutated Varuna will eventually spawn at a random time.

Wave 12 — Drone Mission

After Wave 12, the squad must locate and repair the Drone.
The Drone mission requires the squad to initiate the mission by searching the drone interaction feature that will spawn in the base during wave 12.
Next you will need to find Drone Modules scattered around the crashed Raven/Drone. Once all are found, bring them to the Drone to repair it.
This is a good mission for Recon, Marksman, or Radio Operator to help with, given their skills to provide vision and scouting.
Once the Drone is repaired, the Leader will get a notification to choose a supply drop:
  • Ammunition
  • Medical supplies
  • Explosives
Choose the drop based on what the squad needs most.
Wait until the squad is close to returning to base before calling the supply drop. The drop lands near the center of the squad, so calling it too early can cause it to land far away from the base or in an inconvenient location.
The Drone mission is not just a mission to complete—it's an opportunity for the Leader to replenish whatever the squad is running low on.

11. Wave 9 — The Leader's Biggest Test

Wave 9 is where Leadership really matters.
At the beginning of Wave 9:
  • A large number of Abominations spawn around the edges of the map.
  • Lyganesh also spawns.
  • A Shiva drops at a random location.
  • Evil Marines move toward the Shiva.
The squad is effectively surrounded from the beginning.
Every second spent wandering around the center of the map gives the surrounding Abominations and Lyganesh more time to close in, gradually tightening the noose around the team.
Your goal is to get through the encircling line of Abominations and Lyganesh, get them behind the squad, and then deal with them on your terms.

Common Escape Strategies

Market Wall
Have team wait inside the market wall near the northeast area of the map once the bosses are near, break the wall. This allows squad to casually move through the hole and past the encircling wave of Abominations, effectively putting them behind you. (once you go through the wall, its preferred to head east, there may still be some bosses that spawned in the Northwest heading along the northern route to your position.
Docks
The docks are particularly useful because enemies cannot spawn over the water, creating a natural gap in the surrounding spawn line.
Start to the left of the roundabout and wait until the Abominations begin closing in before moving toward the docks. This position also helps lure bosses that spawn in the lower docks parking lot up the middle lane instead of directly from the docks.
A boss may still spawn near your route, but this can usually be handled by Demolition. 
Terrain Gaps
Some narrow gaps or holes in the terrain are too small for Abominations to pass through. You can use these to let the bosses move around you before crossing through.
Vehicles
Vehicles can temporarily block the road and therefore block enemy movement. If you have vehicles blocking a road, you can wait for the bosses to move around them, then destroy the obstruction and move through.
Fight Through
If there is no safe route around the Abominations, you can simply fight through them.
Demolition should be toward the front of the squad and ready to destroy anything blocking the path.

The Important Part

Get through the boss line.
Once the Abominations are behind you, move along the outer edge of the map and avoid immediately circling back through the center.
Open areas such as the forest, docks, and top-right portion of the map are generally easier places to maneuver.
Avoid the bottom-left quadrant during early phases of wave 9—"Ambush Alley."
This area has a long history of trapping and wiping squads during Wave 9 due to narrow streets and low visibility.

After the Bosses Are Behind You

Once the bosses are safely behind the squad, you can:
  • Complete remaining missions.
  • Retrieve the Shiva.
  • Gather equipment.
  • Prepare to kill the Wave 9 bosses.
You can stack and kill them in an open area such as the forest.
Be especially careful around the 57-minute mark, when additional Evil Marines spawn along the main roads. Running back toward the center while Wave 9 bosses are behind you can result in a devastating ambush if the center has poor visibility.

Don't Kill the Bosses Too Early

Damaging Abominations and Lyganesh causes them to move faster.
Also remember:
Killing the Wave 9 bosses starts Wave 10.
Wave 10 will eventually begin naturally around 1:05–1:10 (on survival mode), whether Wave 9's bosses are alive or not.
If you are not ready for Wave 10, don't rush the final kills. Get close to base first and then kill.

12. Retrieving and Using Shiva

At the beginning of Wave 9, a Shiva drops somewhere randomly.
You want to recover it, but don't blindly run toward it while the squad is surrounded.
Usually you will want to: get the Wave 9 bosses behind you, then retrieve Shiva.

Safest Use: Wave 9

WARNING: The displayed targeting radius is not an accurate representation of the actual blast radius. The explosion can extend significantly beyond the indicator and easily cause TKs. Always give yourself extra margin when firing near teammates. 
Using Shiva on the Wave 9 bosses is the simplest and safest option.
This is particularly useful if:
  • The squad is low on explosives.
  • Radio Operator is inexperienced.
  • The squad is running out of time.
  • You simply want the safest solution.
But Shiva isn't mandatory. Radio Operator, Recon, Marksman, and Demolition can often kill the bosses through their combined abilities.

Wave 13

Shiva can be fired toward the center of the map to soften incoming Mutalisks and potentially damage or kill a Varuna.

Wave 13 Base Entrance Strategy

A more specialized strategy is to fire Shiva directly at the front entrance of the base.
The radiation cloud can kill many enemies attempting to enter the base, saving the squad a large amount of ammunition.
If using this strategy:
  • Fire it before Wave 13 starts if possible
  • If fired before w13 starts, The radiation should expire before Wave 15 starts.
  • Don't flare the entrance. (prevents squad from shooting enemies killed by the Rad)
  • Shoot enemies coming over the walls instead.
  • Use Radio Operator Motion Sensor to detect unexpected bosses and Lyganesh.
  • Avoid standing in the radiation.
  • Demolition may need to very briefly enter the radiation to set bombs.
The goal is to have the radiation disappear before Wave 15 so Demolition can safely prepare the wave 15 base defense bombs and Hellspawn explosives.

Wave 15 Hellspawn

Shiva can also kill a Hellspawn if the squad is low on explosives.
This is not ideal because the radiation creates an unsafe area around the base. If you need to use it this way, finding the Hellspawn early with Radio Operator or Marksman allows you to fire Shiva at it before it reaches the base.

14. Base Phase — Waves 10–15

Once Wave 9 is finished, the squad enters the base-defense phase.
There is considerably less for the Leader to actively direct. Your job is primarily to make sure the squad is prepared for each wave.

Wave 12

Leave the base together for the Drone mission.
Maintain vision with:
  • Road Flares
  • Flaregun
  • Marksman Scope
  • Radio Surveillance
Stay together, complete the mission quickly, and return to base.
Once the Drone is repaired, the Leader chooses the supply drop:
  • Ammo
  • Medical
  • Explosives
Choose based on what your squad actually needs. Usually it’s ammo.
Wait until the squad is almost back at base before calling the drop. The supply drop lands near the center of the squad, so calling it too early can cause it to land somewhere inconvenient.

Wave 13

Make sure you have a competent Minigunner with a fresh drum of ammunition.
The Minigunner's performance is especially important during the massive air wave.

Waves 14–15

Wave 14 brings the massive Nydus wave.
Wave 15 brings the major final boss fight, including Sindir and two Hellspawns.
Once the Hellspawns are dead, the defensive phase is effectively over.
Get out of the base.

15. Evacuation

The first evacuation is usually the safest and simplest choice.
As soon as the Hellspawn and other major bosses are dead:
Move the squad in front of the base immediately.
Don't let everyone start reorganizing their inventories or wandering around looking for last-minute items.
Have the squad:
  1. Move in front of the base.
  2. Wait together.
  3. Prepare the Minigunner for reload.
  4. Wait for the evacuation location.
  5. Move as a group to the evac location.
  6. Follow the pace of the Minigunner.
  7. Don't leave slower players behind, unsupported.
  8. Keep calm and carry on.
  9. Move as one
When moving toward the ground evacuation, approach from the sides when possible rather than taking the center lanes, which can be heavily obstructed by spines and creep.
Everyone must be directly on the evacuation vehicle for it to trigger the end of the game.

Second Evacuation

The second evacuation is more dangerous and is useful if you're pursuing the associated all second evac achievements.
Expect:
  • Random tactical nukes
  • Additional bosses
  • Burrowing Abominations on Veteran
Watch the minimap constantly and keep the squad together.
If teammates start wandering toward a tactical nuke, pause and redirect them rather than allowing them to walk into it.
On Veteran, avoid killing the burrowing Abominations unless necessary—they can respawn after being killed.

16. Herding the squad

Eventually, something will go wrong.
An Abomination appears in front of the squad.
Someone runs the wrong direction.
Half the team gets separated.
Your Minigunner dies.
Demolition misses the bombs.
Your carefully planned route suddenly isn't safe anymore.
This is where Leadership matters most.
When the squad is panicking, think about where most of the players are actually going.
You have two options:

Go With the Squad

If the direction they're moving is safe, simply move the designated location to match them.
I wanted them to go left, but everyone went right. Fine—let's go right.

Stop and Redirect

If the direction they're moving is dangerous, stop and clearly communicate where you want everyone to go.
Don't stubbornly leave the dot in the original location while the entire squad runs somewhere else.
The designated location is a tool to organize the squad, not a commandment.
Adapt to what is actually happening.

17. Leading New Players

Leading new players can be frustrating for some or an opportunity for others.
They may:
  • Move slowly.
  • Fall behind.
  • Take unnecessary equipment.
  • Stay at base too long.
  • Wander away.
  • Not understand your instructions.
  • Die in an obscure location while carrying a ridiculous amount of ammunition.
Don't get frustrated.
New players are slower partly because they don't have the medals that increase the movement speed and effectiveness of experienced Marines.

Choosing Your Leader Class

When leading a lobby with several new players, I would consider leading as Medic or Recon.
Medic is a good choice when you expect the squad to need a lot of help staying alive. You can keep inexperienced players alive while carrying explosives and remote controls to deal with the bosses. Once in the base phase you can switch to minigun or demolition, and attempt to delegate someone else to play medic.
Recon is a good choice when you have a somewhat competent medic in the group, it allows you to carry extra supplies and ammo to base and gives you the best chance at winning a survival game with new players. Use a combination of explosives with flaregun and drone to deal with heavy units. Once in the base phase you can switch to minigun or demolition.
Both classes allow you to contribute heavily to the squad while still focusing your attention on leading.

Set Expectations Early

I recommend explaining the basics in the lobby when possible, or pausing at the beginning of the game.
Something as simple as:
We're going to regroup and set up a base.
 Gather useful items on your way there and drop them at base.
 We'll then move out together to complete missions and gather equipment.
 Don't wander off or fall behind—you will probably die alone.
 If you get separated, use N to team ping.
 If you're covered in green slime, use Anti-Venom.
 Running is often better than shooting.
 Don't be the last person in the squad.
Giving new players these basic instructions can make a huge difference.
Don’t stress too much about teaching. Give players the basic information they need and let them figure out the rest as they play. If they follow along, great; if they don’t read the chat or there’s a language barrier, don’t beat a dead horse trying to communicate—move on and focus on leading the team.

Be Realistic

If half the squad is new, you can usually still win, but you may need to move slower.
If you have a lobby of almost entirely new players, don't expect a perfect game.
A reasonable goal might simply be:
Get as many of them as possible to Wave 10.
If you somehow reach the base phase, do your best with what you have. Leading a lobby full of new players can actually be its own kind of fun challenge. Don't worry about late-game problems like having a perfectly stocked base when you're still teaching people how to stay alive.
Let new players figure things out while keeping them alive when possible.
If someone dies early, they will generally respawn next to the Leader each wave, which gives them another opportunity to learn. Late-game deaths are less forgiving.
But there is a limit.
If someone repeatedly refuses to move, stays in base, or stands behind shooting an Abomination while the rest of the squad leaves, don't sacrifice the entire squad's movement for one player. Sometimes you have to leave them behind so they learn.
Be understanding, but don't let one player hold the entire squad back.
And most importantly:
Don't get frustrated. Have fun with it.
Helping new players survive long enough to understand the game is one of the best ways to grow the community. We were all new once.

18. Leading Experienced Players

You might be surprised that this has it’s own section, but it is important to note that sometimes leading a squad of highly experienced players feels like you’re just barely holding onto the reigns. Hang on and keep up.
Leading experienced players is usually much easier.
Experienced players generally already know:
  • Where to go.
  • What to do.
  • Which missions matter.
  • What bosses are coming.
  • When important events are happening.
They may even remind you about something you forgot.
Ultimately, you are still the Leader, so use your own judgment, but don't be afraid to let an experienced squad help you.
Experienced players will often move ahead of your designated location because they already understand where the squad needs to go.
If you have a fast, experienced squad moving efficiently, sometimes the best thing you can do is simply go with the flow.
Don't force the squad in a different direction unless you have a reason.

19. Squad Composition

Squad composition becomes less important when you have a lobby full of experienced players. Experienced players can compensate for missing classes and adapt their playstyles.
With newer players, however, you want to pay more attention to having the important roles covered.

Core Classes

I generally want:
  • Medic
  • Engineer
  • Minigun
  • Demolition
  • Firebat
Medic is the most important of these. You can survive without some of the others, but having the core classes covered makes the game significantly easier.
Also consider who is playing the class.
For example, I would much rather have:
1 experienced Medic + 1 new Medic
than:
2 new Medics
The ideal composition changes depending on the game mode, difficulty, and experience of the players.
There is no single "correct" squad composition.
A strong default squad might look something like:
  • 1 Demolition
  • 2 Medics
  • 1 Minigun
  • 1 Firebat
  • 2 Marksmen
  • 2 Recon
  • 1 Radio Operator
  • 2 Engineers
But this is only a starting point, not a requirement.

20. Leadership on Difficult Modes

The fundamentals don't change on difficult modes, but the consequences of mistakes become much greater.
Veteran introduces additional bosses and more dangerous events, while Nightmare adds further complications such as additional Wave 6 and Wave 8 threats and another Hellspawn at Wave 15.
This makes the Leader's ability to:
  • Ensure adequate squad composition
  • Know the upcoming waves.
  • Maintain a consistent route.
  • Keep Recon from getting stranded.
  • Preserve resources.
  • Prepare the squad before major events.
  • Adapt when important classes die.
even more important.

21. Final Thoughts

There will be moments when Leadership feels hopeless.
The Minigunner dies at the beginning of Wave 13.
Your Firebat or tank dies during Wave 12.
Half the squad gets wiped during Wave 8.
You look at what remains and think:
There's no way we're getting through this.
Don't let that thought discourage you.
Some of the best games are the ones where everything goes wrong. You lose several excellent Marines, your original plan falls apart, and somehow the squad adapts and keeps going.
You are the Leader, so you will sometimes feel responsible when things go badly. But remember that you don't have to do everything yourself. On a good squad, your teammates know their classes, understand the game, and will help you adapt.
Your job is to provide direction when direction is needed.
Adapt. Keep moving. Never surrender. Never give up.
That's what makes Leadership so satisfying—and why, even after years of playing The Rising Dead, experienced players keep coming back.

  1. Overview
Medic is arguably the most important class in The Rising Dead. Every squad needs one, and a good Medic can be the difference between a team surviving a difficult wave or collapsing.
Medic's primary job is to:
  • Heal injured teammates.
  • Cure debuffs (venom, wounds, fractures, and parasites).
  • Remove dangerous environmental hazards.
  • Stim teammates when they need to move or fight.
  • Anticipate injuries before they happen.
Medic is also one of the easiest classes to learn. It is an excellent class for new players because you can spend much of the early game learning the map and mechanics while still contributing simply by keeping yourself and your teammates alive.
You will also have a green aura around your feet, making Medic easy for the squad to identify.
  1. Skills and Abilities
Medic has five primary skills:
  1. Blood Transfusion — E
  2. Stimulants — D
  3. Nano Grenade — V
  4. Medical Proficiency — Passive
  5. Advanced Programming — Passive
The passive skills unlock additional abilities as they are leveled.

3. How to Build

There are several viable builds, this build recommendation may be controversial to some, and I must reiterate you can build your medic class to your style, as long as you are able to cure debuffs, it doesnt really matter, but this is the general progression I use:

Early game

Put 1 point into each primary skill so you have access to everything.
Then prioritize:
  1. Medical Proficiency (level 1)— enough to unlock Anti-Venom
  2. Advanced Programming (level 4)— enough to unlock Mend
  3. 2 points in Blood Transfusion
  4. 1 point in Stim
  5. 1 point in Nano Grenade
Getting your debuff cures online early is extremely important. Eventually someone will run into you with a broken arm or leg, and you don't want to be standing there without Mend.

Mid game

After getting your basic abilities:
  • Max Medical Proficiency
  • Max Advanced Programming
  • Total of 3 points into Blood Transfusion
  • Max Stim
Medical Proficiency eventually gives you Auto Injectors and Healing Beam.
Advanced Programming improves your utility and eventually gives you Symbiosis, which helps keep you alive (auto-cures parasites and fractures).

Late game

After the important utility is unlocked:
  • Put remaining points into Nano Grenade
  • Blood Transfusion can be leveled as needed (I usually dont go over level 3)
  • You generally don't need to heavily invest in every skill if you don’t want
The exact build isn't important as long as you prioritize debuff removal and variety early.

Medical Proficiency

Medical Proficiency is one of your most important passive skills.

Level 1 — Anti-Venom & Cauterize

Improves your Anti-Venom and Cauterize abilities.

Level 2 — Blood Transfusion

Improves the healing and duration of Blood Transfusion.

Level 3 — Auto Injectors

Automatically heals you when you are about to die.
This makes Medic surprisingly durable and is one reason Medic can function as an emergency tank if the flame-thrower falls.

Level 4 — Stim Upgrade

Stim provides a skill cool down reduction (same as the movement improvement %)
This makes high-level Stim extremely useful on combat classes. (think of wave 9 Radio Ooperator, or Demo converting Bombs on difficult modes)

Level 5 — Healing Beam

Unlocks the ability to use Healing Beam to cure Open Wounds and Septic Wounds and significantly improves the efficiency of the beam.

Advanced Programming  

Advanced Programming provides several important utility upgrades.

Level 1 — Medical Status

Displays the medical status of your squad on the right side of the screen.
This is extremely important.
Watch the status panel for:
  • Low health
  • Venom
  • Open Wounds
  • Fractures
  • Parasites
  • Other medical problems
Don't rely entirely on teammates asking for help. A good Medic notices the problem before they have to ask.

Level 2 — Nano Grenade Upgrade

Nano Grenade can destroy corpses.

Level 3 — Recycle Upgrade

Greatly increases the energy gained from Recycling.
This is extremely important for Medic's energy economy.

Level 4 — Mend Upgrade

Improves Mend's cooldown and energy cost.

Level 5 — Symbiosis

Improves your health regeneration and automatically removes Fractures and Parasites from yourself.

5. What Causes What?

A few useful things to remember:
  • Worms: 100% chance of Venom
  • Devil Dogs / Dogs / Devourers: High chance of Venom
  • Regular zombies: Small chance of Fracture
  • Lyganesh: High chance of Fracture and/or Concussion
  • Mutalisks: Moderate chance of Wounds
  • Queens: 100% chance of Wounds
  • Infestor Queens: Can inflict Parasites

Green Gas Zombies

If you see a zombie surrounded by green gas:
Don't kill it in the middle of the squad's path.
If you can, lure it away from the team first so the gas is released somewhere harmless.
This is a small thing, but it prevents the Medic from having to waste Nano Grenades cleaning up unnecessary gas.

6. How to Use Your Abilities

Blood Transfusion — E

Your primary healing ability.
Use it to:
  • Heal significant damage.
  • Keep injured teammates alive.
  • Stabilize someone during combat.
Prioritize teammates who are actively taking damage or are critically injured. Or critical classes ex. Minigun during a chaotic wave 8

Stim — D

Stim temporarily improves movement and, with the appropriate upgrade, skill cool down reduction. Psychoactive substances, yay!
Use it to:
  • Help someone with a leg fracture move.
  • Reduce Skill Cool Down Speed.
  • Temporarily compensate for an arm fracture.
  • Increase combat effectiveness.
  • Help teammates reposition if they are slow (minigun).
  • Help players escape dangerous situations.

Good targets

Early/mid game:
  • Keep the Minigunner Stimmed when practical. As their ammo-link skill will slow them
  • Stim Recon when they stop by before heading out again.
  • Demolition if converting alot of bombs in a short time.
Base phase:
Stim Demolition before the wave starts.
This is especially useful because Demolition may immediately run into danger and can benefit greatly from the extra mobility and combat performance.
Wave 12 drone mission:
When the team is carrying drone modules keep the module carriers stimmed.

Nano Grenade — V

Nano Grenade is one of Medic's most versatile abilities.
Use it to:
  • Remove poison gas.
  • Removes Creep
  • Slow enemies.
  • Destroy corpses after the appropriate upgrade.
  • Help stack bosses.
  • Control dangerous enemies.

Boss stacking

Nano Grenade can be extremely useful when the squad is trying to stack bosses.
For example, during Wave 9, throwing a Nano Grenade toward the faster-moving boss can help keep the group together for the kill zone.
It can also help slow bosses such as Varuna and other dangerous enemies.

Be careful

Don't automatically throw Nano Grenade whenever you see enemies.
The slow can interfere with other players' plans.
For example:
Demolition is setting up a Claymore and needs a boss to hit a specific location.
If you throw a Nano Grenade and slow the boss, it may miss the intended position and cause the bomb setup to fail.
Learn to recognize when the team wants an enemy slowed and when they need it to keep moving.

Healing Beam — X-S

Healing Beam is powerful but extremely energy hungry.
Do not leave it running unnecessarily.
Its primary uses should be:
  • Curing Open Wounds.
  • Curing Septic Wounds.
  • Emergency healing.
  • Saving a teammate who is actively tanking a dangerous enemy.
For example:
If Hesiod gets through the bombs and starts attacking the Flamethrower, immediately put Healing Beam on the Flamethrower to help keep him alive.

Important

Healing Beam can be set to autocast.
Be careful.
If you leave it running unchecked, it can rapidly drain your energy and leave you unable to perform your other medical duties.
Healing Beam should not be used like a garden hose.

7. Energy Management

Energy is one of the Medic's most important resources. Without energy, you can't heal, cure, Stim, or use Nano Grenades effectively. A good Medic should always maintain enough energy to respond to an emergency.

Recycle Regularly

Use Recycle to turn unnecessary items into energy. Don't wait until you're completely out of energy—look for junk and excess equipment throughout the game and recycle it proactively.
Good things to recycle:
  • Excess class kits
  • Extra Kevlar
  • Defuse kits
  • Excess Ballistic Ammo
  • Excess Uranium Ammo
  • Other equipment the squad does not need
  • Duplicate or unnecessary supplies
You generally don't need four Recon kits, four Medic kits, or multiple Engineer kits sitting in base.
Before recycling Engineer or Flamethrower kits, make sure the Engineer/Flamethrower has extracted the fuel they need.
The Advanced Programming Recycle upgrade makes recycling significantly more effective and allows Medic to contribute heavily to the squad's overall energy economy.
Recycle junk. Don't recycle the squad's future.
This becomes especially important in 2- and 3-player modes, where the Medic has to provide a much larger percentage of the team's healing, curing, and energy management.
Don't sit at zero energy waiting for an emergency. Recycle useful junk before you need the energy.

8. Supporting the Squad

A good Medic doesn't just react to injuries.
Anticipate them.
Watch what your teammates are doing and prepare for the damage they are about to take.

Demolition

Before Demolition runs out to place bombs:
Stim them.
Especially during dangerous waves where a fracture could leave them stranded.

Flamethrower

Stay aware of where the Flamethrower is operating.
If they are tanking a boss and start taking heavy damage:
Heal them immediately.

Minigunner

During the early and mid game, keeping the Minigunner Stimmed can significantly improve their effectiveness.

Recon

When Recon returns to the squad before heading back out:
Stim them before they leave.
The movement speed can make a big difference when they are traveling around the map.

9. Who do I prioritize healing?

Triaging is an essential part of playing Medic. You will often have several injured teammates at once, and you need to decide who needs your attention first.

General priority

  1. Anyone who is about to die
  2. Minigunner
  3. Demolition
  4. Flamethrower
  5. Everyone else
The first rule always overrides everything else: if someone is about to die, save them first.

During the Base Phase

The Minigunner should normally be positioned safely in the center of the base, so they are less likely to take immediate damage. This means your attention should generally shift toward the Flamethrower and Demolition, who are more likely to be taking damage from outside the base.
Save Blood Transfusion for the Flamethrower when possible. Use Medkits on other players to conserve your energy and keep your Transfusion available for emergencies.
Don't waste energy trying to keep everyone at 100% health. Prioritize players who are actively taking damage or whose health is low enough to put them in danger.

With Two Medics

With two Medics, triage becomes much easier. You have more healing and curing resources available, so you can divide the workload and respond to injuries more aggressively.
Good Medic isn't about keeping everyone at full health — it's about making sure the right person gets healed at the right time.

10. Medic Inventory

I generally recommend carrying:
  • Armor
  • Shotgun
  • Remaining slots for transporting items to base

Why a shotgun?

Medic frequently has to run toward injured teammates.
A shotgun gives you useful emergency firepower while you're moving between teammates.
It is also useful when Medic is positioned near the Flamethrower during base defense.

Medic as a Mule

Medic has a lot of freedom in their inventory.
Use your spare slots to transport useful items back to base during the early game.
Once the squad is established at base, carry additional:
  • Medkits
  • Nanites
  • Medical supplies
  • Nano Grenades
Don't be afraid to use your medical supplies in the late game.
After Wave 13, the squad is going to take serious damage. This is what you saved them for.

11. Important Waves  

Wave 9 — Boss Stacking

Wave 9 is one of the most energy-intensive waves in the game, and a lot can go wrong if the squad isn't prepared.
Before the wave:
  • Build up your energy reserves by recycling unnecessary items.
  • Coordinate with the Engineer for additional energy when needed.
  • Keep slower classes, especially Minigunner, Stimmed so they don't fall behind.

During Boss Stacking

Once the bosses are being stacked, a Flamethrower or Medic might attempt luring and kiting them into a favorable “stacked” position. (nice and clumped up together)
Keep an eye on the player doing the luring. Getting hit by Lyganesh's Smash can cause a Concussion and potentially a Fracture.
Keep the boss lurer Stimmed while they are stacking.
When the squad gives the order to destroy the Wave 9 bosses, your priorities change.
Switch your Stim to Demolition.
Demolition may need to run ahead of the bosses to place bombs, making them especially vulnerable to Fractures and other injuries.

Nano Grenade

Use Nano Grenades to support the stack when appropriate:
  • Slow bosses that are moving too quickly.
  • Help keep the bosses grouped together.
  • Remove Creep from the squad's path.
Be careful with the timing. If Demolition is setting up a bomb that requires a boss to reach a specific location, slowing the boss at the wrong time can cause the setup to fail.
During Wave 9, your job isn't just to heal damage — keep the people doing dangerous jobs alive long enough to finish them.

Wave 13 — Muta Rush

Wave 13 is one of the biggest tests for the Medic.
The Muta rush can cause:
  • Heavy damage
  • Multiple Open Wounds
  • Large numbers of Parasites
At this point, stop saving medical supplies for later. There isn't much later.
Use your:
  • Medkits
  • Nanites
  • Nano Grenades
  • Fibrin Bandages
  • Other medical supplies
  • Use energy cells if needed

Absorbing Parasites

One useful strategy is to position yourself near the center-facing edge of the base and help absorb incoming Parasites.
With Mend, Auto Injectors, and Symbiosis, Medic can deal with Parasites more effectively than most classes. Taking the Parasites yourself can prevent several teammates from becoming infected.
If you are doing this:
Carry Fibrin Bandages and don't be afraid to use them.
Your goal is to survive and keep the squad functional.
Don't die trying to save resources. A dead Medic can't heal anyone.

Waves 14–16 — End Game & Evacuation

This is the time to use everything you've been saving.
The squad will take significant damage during the final waves and evacuation. Don't be afraid to burn through your medical supplies to keep everyone alive.

During Evacuation

  • Use Medkits liberally.
  • Cure critical debuffs immediately.
  • Use Nanites for Fractures and Parasites.
  • Use Nano Grenades to control enemies and clear hazards.
  • Use Healing Beam to heal when your energy reserves are strong, but remember that while channeling it, you are not shooting or contributing DPS.
If you have a full E-Cell, strong energy reserves, or a reliable Engineer supplying energy, you can afford to use Healing Beam more aggressively during evacuation.

Your Priority

When multiple players are taking damage, focus on keeping the squad's most important combat roles alive:
  1. Minigunner
  2. Flamethrower
  3. Demolition
  4. Medic
  5. Everyone else
Of course, someone who is about to die always takes priority, regardless of class.
If you can keep the Minigunner, Flamethrower, Demolition, and yourself alive through the final waves, your squad has a much better chance of making it through evacuation.
The end game is not the time to conserve resources. Use everything you have to get the squad out.

12. Medic as a Tank

This is one of the more overlooked aspects of the class.
With Advanced Programming and Auto Injectors, Medic can be surprisingly durable.
If the squad doesn't have a Flamethrower available and someone needs to tank:
Medic can do it.
Auto Injectors provide additional survivability, while your medical abilities allow you to recover from damage and debuffs.
        It’s like having an extra LBA
Medic isn't normally the first choice for tanking, but it is an extremely useful emergency option.

13. When Things Go Wrong

Medic is also one of the best classes at recovering from a disaster.
If the squad starts collapsing:
  1. Keep the minigun alive.
  2. Cure critical debuffs.
  3. Stim teammates who need to escape.
  4. Heal players who can still survive.
  5. Use Nano Grenade to control pursuing enemies.

The Last Medic Standing

A high-level Medic with:
  • Level 5 Stim
  • Level 5 Nano Grenade
  • Shotgun
  • KCA or LBA
  • Sprint
can be surprisingly difficult to kill.
You can often outrun many enemies and use Nano Grenades to slow dangerous pursuers.
In extreme situations, Medic can potentially make it to evacuation alone.
This is particularly relevant in 1-man and 2-man modes, where surviving to the evacuation is sometimes the entire strategy.
But in a normal squad:
Don't plan on being the last survivor.
Your job is to keep the squad alive, not escape without them. Stand with your squad, do your job, and if the line falls, fall with it. 

14. How Many Medics?

For a 12-player squad, 2 Medics is a good target.
Three Medics can certainly work, but usually isn't necessary.
On easier difficulties, a highly experienced Medic can potentially support the entire squad alone.
One excellent Medic is better than three Medics who don't manage their energy or understand debuffs.

15. Medic Quick Reference

Keep the squad alive. Don't just react to injuries—watch what your teammates are doing and anticipate when they are about to need you.
Your priorities are:
  1. Save anyone who is about to die.
  2. Cure dangerous debuffs.
  3. Keep key classes healthy and functional. (minigun, demo, flamer)
  4. Stim teammates before dangerous tasks. (demo, recon)
  5. Manage your energy so you are ready for emergencies.

Remember

Don't wait for someone to ask for medical attention. Watch the squad status and anticipate who is about to get hurt.
Don't waste energy keeping everyone at 100% health.
Don't spam Healing Beam.
And most importantly: a Medic with no energy is just a guy with a first aid kit.

1. Demolition Overview

Demolition is an advanced class and one of the most difficult classes to master. It is also one of the most important.
The Demolition's primary responsibilities are:
  • Killing bosses and heavy units
  • Managing the squad's explosives
  • Clearing corpses
  • Converting explosives into more powerful/ useful explosives
  • Controlling dangerous enemy encounters through positioning and bomb placement
A good Demolition can make difficult waves look easy. A bad Demolition can waste the squad's explosives, destroy important items, kill teammates, or turn a manageable situation into a wipe.
Demolition has a high skill ceiling, but the fundamentals are relatively simple, and there is no single correct way to play the class. The strategies below cover those fundamentals along with some of the most important advanced concepts.

Before playing Demolition, set up your inventory hotkeys.
From the StarCraft II menu:

Set inventory slots to 1–6.
I typically use:

  • 2 — Satchels

3, 3, 3, 2 → Run → Remote Detonate
This is much faster than manually selecting each explosive.

Claymores, Chain Explosives, and Satchels generally deal the same damage, and most of the damage is near the center of the bomb, (the outter edge of the bomb circle = little damage done) The other main difference is how they are triggered.

Claymore

Claymores detonate automatically when an enemy enters their red circle.
They can also be triggered by:
  • Flaregun
  • D8-Beam
  • Other explosives
  • Satchel + Remote Control
Claymores are generally best used as material for converting into other explosives.

Chain Explosive

Chain Explosives do not detonate on their own.
They can be triggered by:
  • Flaregun
  • D8-Beam
  • Another explosive
  • A Satchel + Remote Control

Satchel

Satchels function like Chain Explosives but can also be detonated directly with Remote Control.
This makes them extremely useful for defensive bomb stacks.

CL-21

CL-21 deals approximately 3,000 damage, compared with ~2,000 from normal explosives.
That means:
2 CL-21 = 3 normal explosives
CL-21s also have a larger effective damage area when compared with other explosives. They will do more damage if a boss unit is off-center on the bomb.
They require significant energy to manufacture, so they are generally only worth making if you are short on explosives or on higher game mode difficulties.

4. Bomb Damage & Placement

Bomb damage is strongest at the center of the explosion and decreases toward the edge.
This matters when fighting bosses.
An Abomination standing directly on top of a bomb will take considerably more damage than one standing on the outer edge.

Bomb Stacking

For many bosses, you will stack multiple explosives together and detonate them simultaneously.

5. Primary Demolition Skills

Remote Control — E

Remote Control detonates Satchels or CL-21s within range.
Upgrading it provides:
  • Greater range
  • Lower energy cost
  • Shorter cooldown
Remote Control is primarily used to quickly detonate your defensive bomb stacks.

RPG — D

RPG is one of the most powerful direct-damage abilities in the game.
It deals a lot of damage when upgraded with a decent area and cast range.
Use RPG to:
  • Finish bosses after bomb stacks
  • Kill enemies when conserving explosives
  • Destroy Nyduses
  • Destroy high-concentration corpse areas (bodies on the ground)
  • Damage bosses that are already partially injured
  • Provide emergency damage
Do not fire an RPG unless you are confident in your shot.
A missed RPG can kill a teammate or destroy something important.
If you aren't sure, don't shoot.

Cluster — V

Cluster deploys a group of small proximity mines around the target area.
It is particularly effective against larger targets such as:
  • Abominations
  • Devourers
  • Roaches
  • ETA
  • Queen
Cluster is especially useful for saving explosives.
For example, a well-placed Cluster may heavily damage an Abomination, allowing you to finish it with an RPG instead of using four bombs.

Important:

Avoid deploying Cluster Mines in front of your squad.
The mines are randomly placed and can block your team's path.
Use them behind or to the side of the team, where they won't trap the squad.
Cluster is also poor against Lyganesh because of its small model size.

Re-arm — F

Re-arm refreshes all of your abilities, including secondary abilities.
It consumes significant energy.
Use it primarily for emergencies or important boss encounters rather than constantly refreshing your abilities. Some hard game modes that require alot of explosives ex. triple or inferno, you may need to skill this to make additional CL-21s

Demolition Proficiency — Passive

This passive unlocks your secondary Demolition abilities.

Disarm — X+E

You can disarm:
  • Claymores
  • Chain Explosives
  • Satchels

Useful when you need additional remotely detonated explosives for your defensive stacks.

7. Preferred build

As stated earlier, there is no perfect way to build demolition class and you will see significant variance amongst how experienced players play demolition. However, that being said, here is a strong basic Build recommendation to ensure you have what you need when it counts most.
  1. First priority is getting Demo Proficiency to level 3 (to convert claymores)
  2. Get 2-3 Upgrades in cluster to deal with abominations (level 3 can kill an abom)
  3. Get 1 point in Remote control
  4. Get 1 point in re-arm for emergencies
  5. Next focus on getting RPG to level 4 (level 4 rpg can kill a Nydus)
  6. Then focus on maxing remote control skill (level 5 RC before wave 10)

  • A Second demo might also help.
        Double, triple, inferno (more bosses):
  • I would spend more points in re-arm early and get demo proficiency to level 4 as soon as possible so you can start making CL-21 ASAP and saving energy on re-arming.
  • A second Demo might also help

9. When Should You Kill Bosses? 

Most of the time:
Kill dangerous bosses as soon as possible.
This is particularly important with inexperienced squads.
If an Abomination appears in front of the squad, don't let everyone panic and start shooting it while the squad splits in different directions.
A good Demolition should be near the front of the squad and ready to immediately deal with dangerous bosses.
Kill the threat, clear the path, and keep moving.

The exception: Resource conservation

On difficult modes such as Triple or Inferno, you may need to conserve explosives.
Use your other classes and abilities whenever possible.
For example:
  • Radio Operator uses 30mm on Lyganesh → you may only need ~5 bombs afterward.
  • Recon uses Drone on an Abomination → finish it with an RPG or a single Satchel.
  • Cluster heavily damages an Abomination → use RPG rather than four bombs.
  • If marksman is effectively sniping fiends during base phase → save the explosives
Don't use four bombs on something that can be killed with 1 bomb or Cluster or RPG.
Resource conservation is one of the biggest differences between a novice and an expert Demolition.

11. Wave 9

Wave 9 is one of the most important waves for Demolition to understand.
Multiple bosses spawn around the outer edge of the map, meaning the squad will begin the wave surrounded.
The biggest mistake is allowing the squad to panic, split up, and start backtracking.

The objective

Get the bosses behind the squad.
Once they are behind you, you can control the engagement instead of fighting through an ambush.

Supermarket Strategy

One of the easiest strategies is to begin Wave 9 near the Supermarket near the destructible wall.
  1. Wait for the bosses to approach.
  2. Break through the wall.
  3. Move around the approaching bosses.
  4. Continue along the outer edge of the map.
  5. Get the bosses safely behind the squad.
  6. Find an open area to stack them.
  7. Kill them near base.

Other terrain strategies

You can also use terrain such as:
  • Roads with holes
  • Narrow passages
  • The docks
Be aware that Abominations cannot cross some gaps that Lyganesh can, so don't assume terrain will stop every boss.

If a boss blocks the squad

The Demolition should generally be toward the front of the squad.
Kill whatever is preventing the squad from moving forward.
Don't hesitate to use bombs on an immediate threat if necessary. Getting trapped and wiped is much more expensive than using a few explosives.
Once the bosses are safely behind the squad, stop killing them unnecessarily.
Injured Abominations and Lyganesh move faster, and killing too many bosses too early can cause Wave 10 to begin before you are ready.
The goal is to stack the bosses and kill them efficiently near base, ideally before Wave 10 begins naturally.

12. Base Defense

Once the squad reaches base, Demolition's job changes from killing individual bosses to building a defensive bomb network.
A typical defensive stack is:
1 Satchel + 1 Chain Explosive
Try your best to have the center of the bomb (max damage) detonate directly on the heavy units/ bosses.
You can use larger stacks when necessary or conserve explosives by using fewer bombs and allowing the Firebat or squad to finish enemies.
When setting up your stacks, as long as the center of 1 stack isn’t touching the edge of another they will not trigger each other. You want your bomb stacks close together but never overlapping.

Inner vs. Outer Stacks

Your inner stacks are the primary defensive stacks. (inner = closer to base entrance)
Bosses must pass through these before reaching the squad.
When an inner stack is detonated:
Replace it immediately.
Outer stacks are your backup in case multiple bosses approach from the same direction and you don't have time to replace the inner stack.
Always detonate the inner stack first when possible. This minimizes the distance you need to travel into danger to replace it.

Flares & Vision

Your bomb defenses are useless if you can't see them.
Use Road Flares around your bomb stacks.
A good routine is:
Flare at the beginning of each wave.
Flares should provide enough vision to see incoming enemies and your bomb stacks.
Always maintain enough vision that:
  • You can see approaching bosses.
  • The squad can see you.
  • You can safely replace bombs.
  • You can identify threats before they reach your defenses.

Protect yourself

Many Demolition deaths occur while placing outer bombs and flares.
Before leaving base:
Flare in front of where you are going first.
The squad needs to be able to see and support you.
Don't build an enormous defensive perimeter if you don't have enough vision to safely maintain it.
More bombs are not better if you can't safely use them.

13. Vet Mode & Difficult Modifiers

Veteran Modus significantly increases the workload for Demolition.
You may encounter:
  • More Abominations
  • Random bosses every odd wave (usually fiend or slasher, sometimes Hesiod)
  • Banelings
  • Large boss waves on Waves 6 and 8
  • Additional Lyganesh on Wave 9
  • Additional threats during evacuation

Banelings

Banelings are particularly dangerous because they can detonate your bombs.
If you're playing Veteran:
Always flare before leaving base.
If you see a Baneling approaching your bomb stacks, back away.
A good Marksman can eliminate Banelings before they reach your defenses, so coordinate with them whenever possible.

Wave 6

Veteran Wave 6 produces multiple ETA/"Power Rangers."
Set up a defensive position with:
  • Satchels
  • Road flares
  • Good vision
A location with a single entrance is ideal. Otherwise an open area with good vision is also good. Sometimes you will have to make due with wherever you end up, just do your best.  

Wave 8

Wave 8 produces multiple Varunas.
An open area with good vision is preferred.
The docks are particularly useful because nothing spawns from the water, reducing the number of directions you need to defend.
Defending near the edge of the map is preferred as bosses will arrive sporadically (if you are near the center of the map they are more likely to arrive at the same time).
Setting up satchels facing the center of the map may be needed for multiple varuna spawns, especially if Heavy Support is unavailable. (HS can suppress and do heavy damage to Varuna)

Base Phase and Evac

You will contend with additional abomination spawns each wave, banelings and generally more boss spawns. Plan accordingly.
During the evacuation phase, you may encounter an additional hellspawn and/or a Lyga.
During wave 16 (second evac) there will be more tactical nukes dropping and burrowing abominations and infinite random boss spawns. With Burrowing abominations, avoid killing them unless they are in your path, they will respawn around team when you kill them, its better to have them safely behind you.

14. Wave 15 — Hellspawn

Hellspawn are among the most dangerous enemies in the game and require substantial explosives to kill.
Baseline requirements:
  • Regular: ~16 normal explosives (no change in vet mode)
  • Nightmare: ~18 normal explosives
Higher difficulties can add additional Hellspawn. (ex. -triple = 6 hellspawns!)

Hellspawn Stacks

Prepare dedicated Hellstacks before they arrive.
A Hellstack should contain enough explosives to kill the Hellspawn without relying on perfect follow-up damage.
Keep enough space around the stacks for the squad to position safely.
This is particularly important with inexperienced players because poor positioning can cause someone to accidentally pull the Hellspawn away from the bomb stack.
If you have extra bombs, don’t be afraid to add a few extra, just in case you miscount :)

Plan for multiple directions

Don't assume every Hellspawn will approach from the same direction.
Prepare your hellstacks as soon as wave 14 is ending (no more nyduses are spawning)
The important part is having a plan before the Hellspawn arrive.
A good Demolition doesn't panic when something goes wrong because they already have a backup plan.

15. Evacuation

Killing the Hellspawn does not mean your job is finished.
During evacuation, continue watching for:
  • Fiends
  • Slashers
  • MRIT
  • Additional bosses
Use RPG when appropriate to quickly eliminate threats before they reach teammates.
For the first evacuation, you may need to continue killing MRIT to keep the evacuation route clear and prevent the squad from losing time.
You can also place defensive bombs behind your route and flare them so you can see incoming threats.

16. Demolition Safety

This is perhaps the most important section of the entire guide.

Never leave Cluster Mines lying around.

Pick them up or disarm them when possible.
Even experienced players can accidentally walk into them.

Don't take unnecessary RPG shots.

If you aren't confident in your aim:
Don't shoot.
It is better to let an enemy deal some damage to the squad than accidentally kill a teammate or destroy an important item.

Watch your bomb placement.

Don't place bombs where:
  • The squad needs to walk.
  • They can accidentally chain-detonate.
  • You cannot see them.
  • You cannot safely replace them.

Respect Banelings.

A Baneling reaching your bomb stack can destroy your entire defensive setup.

1. Engineer Overview

The Engineer is the squad's energy resource manager. Your primary job is to move energy from players who don't currently need it to players who do.
You are also responsible for fuel, Energy Cells, battlefield visibility, and emergency support.
The Engineer becomes increasingly valuable as difficulty increases. On easier difficulties you can sometimes get away without one, but on Nightmare, Inferno, and high-spawn modes such as -double, Engineer is essential and a good Engineer can make a major difference.
Engineers Skills:
        Primary:
  • SWL  E
  • Electric Discharge D
  • Power Link F (Take energy)
  • Engineering V (Give energy)
  • Faraday Armor B
        Secondary (found in actions sub menu):
  • Transfer Fuel X+X
  • Extrac Fuel X+C
  • DB Charge X+E
  • Charge Energy cell X+D
  • Generator X+S
Your priorities are simple:
  1. Manage squad energy.
  2. Keep the Medic and Demolition energized.
  3. Verify the Firebat is supplied with fuel.
  4. Charge Energy Cells early.
  5. Clear important visual obstructions.
  6. Use stun/Faraday for emergencies.
  7. Use SWL for extra DPS when the squad can afford the energy or is low on ammo.
The Engineer isn't difficult because the mechanics are complicated. The difficult part is knowing when to transfer, when to save, and when to spend.

2. Energy Management

PowerLink is your most important skill.
Your goal is not to make everyone's energy bars equal. Your goal is to prevent energy from sitting unused while another player needs it.
Generally, take energy from lower-demand classes such as Firebat, Heavy Support/Minigun, Grunt, and Marksman and move it toward Medic, Demolition, Radio Operator, or whoever needs it most.

Medic comes first

Keep the Medic supplied whenever possible.
A high-ranking Medic may have a much larger energy pool, so don't blindly judge them by percentage. A high-ranking medic at 70–80% may be completely healthy, while a low-rank medic at 50% during a difficult fight may be in serious trouble.
A proactive Medic may also ask you to take excess energy from them. That's fine—they can recycle unwanted items to recover energy.
Don't ask "Who has the lowest energy?" Ask "Where will this energy be most useful?"

Don't drain people too far

3. Fuel & Generator

The Engineer can extract fuel from vehicles and fuel-barrel bombs and transfer it to the Firebat or another Engineer, or convert it into energy with Generator.

Firebat fuel target

A good general target is:
5,000–10,000 Firebat fuel by Wave 10
Harder difficulties and higher-spawn modes such as -double may require considerably more.
Don't automatically turn your fuel into energy just because someone is low.
Before using Generator, check:
  • Is there stagnant energy I can transfer instead?
  • Does the Firebat have enough fuel?
  • Is the squad actually running low?
  • Are we entering a high-energy-demand wave?
Transfer existing energy first. Generate new energy when the squad actually needs it.
Generator becomes particularly valuable on Waves 9, 14, and 15, when multiple players are spending energy at the same time. During these waves, it's often better to run Generator and use your stored Energy Cells rather than aggressively draining teammates who are already using their energy.
Don't be greedy with fuel. If the squad is running out of energy and the Firebat has an adequate reserve, use Generator.

4. Energy Cells

Charge Energy Cells early, while the squad can afford the energy.

Goal: 3 fully charged Level-5 Energy Cells by approximately Wave 10.

A Level-5 Energy Cell provides passive energy regeneration (multiple E-cells will not stack the regen boost) and is particularly valuable for:
  • Medic
  • Radio Operator
  • Heavy Support, especially with frequent Suppress
  • Marksman, when heavily using Scope/Recoil/Canister
  • Demolition, if they have the inventory space
  • Engineer, where the regeneration helps you clutch the transfer of energy
Don't wait until the squad is struggling to start charging Cells. Build the reserve early.
After roughly Wave 9, charging cells should generally stop being a priority unless the squad has plenty of spare energy. Later in the game, use the Cells you've built when the squad needs them.

5. Battlefield Utility

Don't destroy everything just because you can.
Use D8 Charge/D8-Beam to remove objects that actually affect the squad:
  • Block important defensive positions.
  • Block routes.
  • Hide enemy approaches.
  • Create dangerous blind spots.
  • Interfere with evacuation.
Large vehicles are particularly important because they can create huge blind spots. The last thing you want during an evacuation is a Fiend appearing from behind a firetruck that nobody could see.
When possible, extract vehicle fuel before destroying it. However, don't waste time recovering fuel from a vehicle if removing the obstruction is more important.
Also remember that burning fuel from a destroyed vehicle can damage teammates for several seconds.
D8-Beam can also:
  • Open Bella caches.
  • Destroy infected trees.
  • Detonate explosives when necessary.
Check with the Squad Leader or Radio Operator before destroying infected trees.

6. Combat Abilities

SWL — Hotkey E

SWL uses energy instead of ammunition and provides a decent attack speed with splash damage.
Use it when:
  • The squad needs additional DPS.
  • You're running low on ammunition.
  • A high-priority target needs to die quickly.
  • The squad's energy balance is healthy.
Examples include the Mutalisks Rush on Wave 7 and Varuna on Wave 8.
Don't continuously spam SWL. Every shot is energy that could otherwise be transferred to another player.
Use SWL when the squad can afford the energy—not simply because you can fire it.

Electric Discharge — Hotkey D

AOE stun.
This is primarily an emergency tool. Use it to save teammates about to get pounded or stop dangerous enemies/bosses from reaching them.
Tip: type -d in chat to switch from instant use or targeted use
Don't hesitate to use it when someone is about to get overwhelmed.
Be advised! This skill will use some energy from your squad members within its radius. If a teammate has no energy, it will also stun them!

Faraday Armor

Provides an energy-based defensive shield.
Use it to protect teammates taking heavy damage, particularly during dangerous boss encounters.
This will also use some of the targeted squad members’ energy.

7. Important Waves

You don't need to memorize a complicated Engineer strategy for every wave. Just remember these:

Wave 7 — Mutalisks

Use SWL for additional DPS if the squad can afford the energy.

Wave 8 — Varuna

Keep energy available for emergency abilities. SWL can provide additional DPS if needed.

Wave 9 — Major Energy Wave

This is one of the most important waves for Engineer play.
  • Don't aggressively drain teammates beforehand.
  • Keep Radio Operator, Demolition and Medics well supplied.
  • Expect heavy energy usage across the squad.
  • Have your Energy Cells ready at this point and distributed to the squad.
  • Use Generator rather than draining players who are already spending their energy.
  • I will typically ensure our squads’ energy is topped off before wave 9 starts
Radio Operator may need substantial energy for Motion Sensor, artillery, air strike, 30mm, and other abilities.

Wave 10+

The game enters the later/turtle phase.
  • Stop aggressively draining Firebat.
  • Generally keep Firebat above ~50% energy. (especially if using Stun or farraday)
  • Start using the resources you've accumulated. (no point in saving ecells past wave 14, use them!)
  • Transfer fuel to flame thrower if they need it.
  • Generator becomes increasingly important.

Waves 13–15

Expect another major increase in energy demand.
Use Generator and your stored Energy Cells when necessary. Don't conserve resources at the expense of squad survival.

8. Build

Prioritize PowerLink and Engineering early. These are the foundation of the Engineer's resource-management role.
After that, specialize according to what the squad needs:
  • SWL → Additional DPS
  • Stun → emergency crowd control
  • Faraday/Engineer shield → defensive support
A general-purpose Engineer should prioritize energy management first, then specialize.
My preferred Engineer Build:
  1. Upgrade Engineering Link to level 5
  2. Upgrade Power Link to level 5
  3. Upgrade 1 Point in Faraday Armor
  4. Upgrade SWL to level 5
  5. As game progresses evenly distribute remaining points in Faraday and Electrical discharge
This build allows me to manage the squads energy, charge ecells ASAP, remove visual obstacles, collect fuel and provide DPS support in case the team needs extra firepower.

9. Common Engineer Mistakes

❌ Draining everyone as low as possible
 Leave players enough energy to do their jobs.
❌ Letting the Medic or Demolition sit at low energy while someone else is full
 Move the excess.
❌ Using Generator while the squad has stagnant energy
 Transfer existing energy first.
❌ Waiting until late waves to charge Energy Cells
 Build them early.
❌ Burning all your fuel on Generator
 Check the Firebat's fuel first.
❌ Spamming SWL
 That energy may be more valuable elsewhere.
❌ Forgetting your own energy
 You need enough to sprint and respond to emergencies.

Quick Reference

If you only remember one section, remember this:
Keep the Medic and Demolition energized.
 Move stagnant energy.
 Don't drain teammates below what they need.
 Verify Firebat has around 5–10k fuel by Wave 10.
 Build  Level-5 Energy Cells early. (Goal of 3 level 5 e-cells)

 Use Generator and Energy Cells during heavy energy needs.
 Help Keep important defensive positions and evacuation routes clear.

1. Radio Operator Overview

The Radio Operator is an advanced support/combat class. It is not a core class—the game is winnable without one—but a good Radio Operator can make difficult games significantly easier.
The class provides global vision, enemy movement information, supply drops, corpse clearing, obstacle removal, and powerful long-range attacks against bosses. It is also one of the most energy-intensive classes, so good energy management and knowing when to reskill are important.

Radio Operator Skills

Primary:
  • Supply Drop — V
  • Mark 45 — E
  • Motion Sensor — F
  • Satellite Surveillance — D
  • Advanced Communication
Secondary — Actions Menu:
  • Designator — X+C
  • Napalm Strike — X+F
  • 30mm Board Cannon — X+E
  • Air Strike — X+D
  • Artillery — X+A
The Radio Operator can fill several different roles during a game. You may focus on supply drops, boss damage, tree destruction, vision, or enemy movement, depending on what the squad needs.
Because of this, don't be afraid to reskill. I will often reskill the Radio Operator three or more times in a game as the squad's needs change. Simply remove and re-equip the kit to change your build.
Don't lock yourself into one build. The best Radio Operator is the one who adapts to what the squad needs.

2. Primary Skills  

Supply Drop — V

Supply Drop is the Radio Operator's primary source of additional supplies and can be called starting on Wave 3. Each Skill point will decrease the radius of the random drop location, at level 5 the drop location is small enough to fit inside base with team standing in the middle.  
There are three types of supply planes and they cycle randomly:

Plane

Supplies

Falcon

Ammunition / flaregun charge

Eagle

Medical supplies/ E-Cells

Buzzard

Explosives / road flares
Choose the drop based on what the squad actually needs. Don't automatically call explosives every time.
Supply drops have a significant delay. If you call something that the team doesn’t need and then a new plane starts circling you may miss the one you want, so if you’re desperate for amm\unition wait until an ammunition drop is available.
When you call a drop:
  1. Call it somewhere your squad can reach.
  2. Tell the squad what type of drop you called.
  3. Watch the minimap and make sure someone collects it.
Calling the drop while you are ahead of the squad is generally a good practice so the squad doesn't accidentally leave it behind.

Mark 45 — E

Mark 45 is a global-range explosive ability.
It is particularly useful for:
  • Destroying infected trees.
  • Clearing vehicles and barriers.
  • Destroying large piles of bodies.
  • Clearing hordes.
  • Removing visual obstructions.
It is not an efficient boss-damage ability. Even when upgraded, its damage against bosses is relatively low.

Most important rule:

Never fire Mark 45 blindly.
Mark 45 can:
  • Team kill.
  • Destroy important items.
  • Destroy things outside what you may expect from the visual indicator.
Scan the area first with Satellite Surveillance and make sure there are no teammates or important items in the area before firing.
Because it has global range, Mark 45 is especially useful for clearing obstacles or destroying infected trees without having to physically travel to them.

Motion Sensor — F

Motion Sensor is one of the Radio Operator's most valuable support abilities.
It provides the squad with information about:
  • Enemy movement.
  • The direction enemies are approaching from.
  • Teammate locations in areas with poor visibility.
  • Boss movement.
This is particularly valuable during Wave 9, when large numbers of Abominations are approaching from different directions.
Motion Sensor also helps Demolition, Reconnaissance, and Marksman locate incoming bosses and position their attacks.
A good Radio Operator should use Motion Sensor when anticipating boss spawns, but remember that it requires energy, so its usage may depend on the Engineer feeding you energy.

Satellite Surveillance — D

Satellite Surveillance provides global vision in a set area.
Use it to:
  • Locate infected trees.
  • Check boss locations.
  • Find supply drops.
  • Confirm Shiva drops.
  • Locate LBA drops.
  • Find confidential reports.
  • Check an area before using Mark 45.
  • Provide temporary vision anywhere on the map.
Satellite Surveillance is particularly important because it allows the Radio Operator to see before shooting.
Scan first. Shoot second.

Advanced Communication — Passive

Advanced Communication is the Radio Operator's passive skill and unlocks the secondary abilities:
  • Designator
  • Napalm Strike
  • 30mm Board Cannon
  • Air Strike
  • Artillery
Some of these abilities have long cooldowns (artillery & airstrike) and should generally be treated as limited resources for important targets, particularly bosses. Some of these abilities have short cooldowns (Designator, Napalm & 30mm) and you can spam them as your energy permits.

3. Secondary Skills

Designator — X+C

Designator can be used on enemies to provide your teammates with vision of the target for several seconds.
It can also be used to stun the target for a very short time.
This makes Designator useful both offensively and as an emergency tool.

Napalm Strike — X+F

Napalm provides area damage and can help:
  • Damage bosses.
  • Clear hordes.
  • Destroy piles of bodies.
Napalm can injure and kill teammates.
Don't use it near the squad unless you are certain the area is safe.

30mm Board Cannon — X+E

The 30mm Board Cannon is one of the Radio Operator's best boss-killing abilities.
It is a global-range ability and deals close to 7,000 damage over 20 seconds.
Most importantly:
30mm does not team kill.
This makes it safe to use near teammates.
It is particularly effective against slower ground bosses. A 30mm can almost kill an Abomination and deal almost half the health of a Lyga.
Because of its effectiveness, use it on meaningful targets rather than wasting it on ordinary enemies.

Air Strike — X+D

Air Strike has a large range and area of effect and substantial explosive damage, but it is not global.
There is approximately a 10-second delay before the strike lands.

How to aim it

Don't aim directly at a moving boss.
Instead, place the target circle slightly ahead of the boss along its path and let the boss walk into the strike.
This works particularly well against slower targets such as:
  • Abominations
  • Lyga
It is much harder to reliably hit fast-moving enemies such as:
  • Devourers
  • Fiends
  • Slashers
  • Hesiods
  • Varunas
Air Strike is explosive and can team kill. Never use it near teammates or the base.

Artillery — X+A

Artillery requires you to first place an artillery beacon, then move away from the danger area and use the same hotkey X+A to channel for approximately 2 seconds to call the strike.
The artillery lands around the beacon and deals massive area damage.
It is particularly useful for Wave 9, where a well-placed strike can potentially kill the Abominations when they are stacked together.

Good Artillery requires preparation

Before placing the beacon:
  1. Use Motion Sensor/Satellite Surveillance to locate the bosses.
  2. Determine where they are moving.
  3. Get the squad to stack the bosses together.
  4. Place the beacon where the bosses will be.
  5. Make sure the squad is safely outside the strike area.
  6. Call the artillery.
Artillery is highly unpredictable, so never place the beacon around teammates, valuable items, or the base.

4. Infected Trees

The Radio Operator is particularly useful for destroying infected trees because Mark 45 can be used at global range.
There are 8 infected trees on the map.
As the game progresses each wave:
  • Trees gain HP.
  • Their surrounding creep increases.
  • Destroying trees becomes increasingly dangerous.

Kuku Spawn Mechanic

When you kill a tree, randomly a Kuku boss can spawn, so avoid killing them early game.

Generally, once you have located the trees, begin working toward destroying them, but pay attention to the current wave and the squad's situation.
You also want to destroy them before Wave 9 whenever possible.
Once all trees are destroyed, an ammo drop will randomly appear on the map. Waiting too long can prevent the squad from getting useful ammunition from this drop.

5. Wave 9 — Radio Operator's Biggest Combat Role

If the squad isn't using the Shiva tactical nuke to destroy wave 9 bosses, Radio Operator and Demolition become extremely important for dealing with the Wave 9 Abominations and Lyga.
A good squad leader will generally route the squad through an open area and have the bosses stack together.
Once the bosses are grouped:

Recommended sequence

1. Motion Sensor
 Track the approaching bosses and help the squad maintain awareness.
2. Artillery
 Place the beacon and call artillery once the bosses are properly grouped.
3. 30mm
 Use it on any remaining bosses.
4. Air Strike
 Target the path of the bosses.
5. Mark 45 / Napalm
 Use these for additional damage and cleanup.
6. Designator
 Use it to provide vision or temporarily stun dangerous enemies when appropriate.
Demolition can simultaneously contribute Cluster Mines, RPGs, and other explosives.
If executed correctly, the squad can kill the Wave 9 bosses near the base while spending some energy to save Shiva for later and it’s alot of fun.

Prepare before Wave 9

Don't show up to Wave 9 with a build that can't perform the job.
Before Wave 9:
  • Hold a level 5 Energy Cell if available.
  • Ensure the Engineer knows you will be using significant energy.
  • Have the appropriate skills upgraded.
  • Make sure you have enough points in Advanced Communication for the abilities you intend to use.
  • Keep Motion Sensor available.
  • Re-Skill the kit if needed

6. Reskilling & Recommended Builds

There is no single perfect Radio Operator build. Radio operator might have the most variance in its build, there are alot of ways to build it.
Your build should change as the squad's needs change.

Basic Build (no need to Reskill)

If Re-skilling the class sounds like too much work, stick to this buld to maximize effectiveness.
  1.  Focus on getting Advanced Communication to level 5 before wave 9
  2. 1 point in Supply Drop
  3. 1 point in motion sensor
  4. Progressively Increase Surveillance until trees are found (then max before w12)
  5. Progressively increase Mk45 until infected trees are destroyed
  6. Distribute remaining between supply drop and motion once trees are dead

Early Game — Tree / Utility Build

My typical early-game priority is:
  1. 4 points in Advanced Communication for boss/secondary abilities.
  2. 1 point Supply Drop
  3. 1 point Motion Sensor
  4. Max Satellite Surveillance
  5. Upgrade Mark 45 enough to kill the trees for the current wave.
Your exact Mark 45 level depends on when you are destroying the trees.

After the Trees Are Destroyed

Once the tree objective is finished, reskill toward combat/support:
  1. Max Advanced Communication
  2. 2-3 points in Supply Drop
  3. 1-2 points in Mark 45 for clearing obstacles
  4. Distribute remaining points between Motion Sensor and Satellite Surveillance
  5. Have Satellite Surveillance maximized by Wave 12 (drone mission)

Wave 9+

Reskill again if necessary to ensure you have the abilities required for the Wave 9 boss fight.
Don't be the Radio Operator who says:
"I can't use artillery because I didn't upgrade it."
Reskill the class.
A pro Radio Operator should expect to change your build as the game progresses rather than committing to one build from Wave 1.

7. Late Game

Once the squad reaches the base and the major tree/boss objectives are finished, the Radio Operator's role changes.
At Wave 12, Satellite Surveillance is also particularly useful for locating Drone Module parts during the drone repair objective.
After approximately Wave 13, your most useful tools are generally:
  • Supply Drops
  • Motion Sensor
  • Satellite Surveillance for Temporary vision
  • Mark 45 for clearing evacuation obstacles
  • 30mm against bosses
  • Napalm/airstrike when at appropriate distance

Waves 13–14

Radio Operator can help locate and, when safe, damage Lyga using a combination of:
30mm + Air Strike + Mark 45 + Napalm
This can save valuable Demolition explosives.

8. Radio Operator Safety

This deserves its own section because a careless Radio Operator can do more harm than good.

Never fire explosives blindly.

Mark 45, Air Strike, Artillery, and Napalm can cause friendly damage and/or destroy important items.
Before using an explosive:
  • Check the area.
  • Use Satellite Surveillance when necessary.
  • Make sure teammates are clear.
  • Make sure valuable items are clear.
  • Consider where the squad will be moving.
  • Never assume the visual targeting circle represents the exact damage radius.
The actual damage area can be larger than the targeting indicator, and there is some randomness to the explosive damage.

Never use Air Strike or Mark 45 near the base or squad.

The potential damage is simply not worth the risk.
30mm is the exception—it does not team kill and is safe to use near items/ equipment.

9. Quick Reference

If you only remember a few things:
Adapt your build to what the squad needs.
 Reskill throughout the game.
 Use Motion Sensor frequently, especially around Wave 9.
 Use Satellite Surveillance to find trees, bosses, drops, and safe targets.
 Destroy infected trees before Wave 9 when practical.
 Coordinate tree destruction with the Squad Leader.
 Have the tools ready for the Wave 9 boss stack.
 Use 30mm on important bosses.
 Lead moving bosses into Air Strikes rather than aiming directly at them.
 Use Mark 45 primarily for trees, corpses, obstacles, and cleanup—not bosses.
 Tell the squad when and where you called a Supply Drop.
 Never fire explosives blindly.
 Always scan before firing if you aren't certain the area is clear.

The Radio Operator's job is simple:

See the battlefield. Tell the squad what is coming. Give the squad what it needs. Kill the things that need killing—and don't kill your teammates while doing it.

1. Reconnaissance Overview

Reconnaissance is probably the most difficult and dangerous class in The Rising Dead therefor this class guide will likely be the longest and most in-depth.
It is an advanced class that requires a strong understanding of:
  • The map
  • Enemy movement
  • Spawn timing
  • Missions
  • Squad Leader routing
  • Item priorities
  • Your own movement and abilities
Recon is not a necessary class. A squad should have its important classes covered first. If the team needs a Medic, Engineer, Demolition, Firebat, or Heavy Support, taking Recon instead is usually a mistake. Radio Operator and Marksman are also generally more valuable once the basic squad is established.
Recon becomes valuable when the squad is already functioning and you can use your mobility to accomplish things the rest of the squad cannot.

What does Recon actually do?

A good Recon will:
  • Search areas the squad is unlikely to enter.
  • Find and retrieve important equipment.
  • Complete missions independently.
  • Search crates for Confidential Reports (Crep).
  • Search police cars and ambulances for mission objectives.
  • Find and mark infected trees.
  • Help with Bella Bone Mission
  • Find missing mission items.
  • Recover respawned mini Marines.
  • Move explosives and other important items onto the squad's route.
  • Provide vision with Road Flares and Flaregun.
  • Recover unique items such as M15, Shiva, LBA, Vahana, and RBA.
  • Use the Drone to scout or kill dangerous enemies.
  • Help the Squad Leader anticipate what is ahead.
The Recon is essentially a one-man wolf pack.
A good Recon makes the Leader's job easier because the Leader doesn't have to personally search the entire map, complete every mission, or worry about every item sitting in a building.
This is why Recon is difficult to teach. There isn't always a specific button you need to press. You need to constantly ask:
What does the squad need right now, and what can I accomplish independently while they are moving?

Recon is usually away from the squad

A common mistake is having three or four players choose Recon, then all run around with the squad.
That's not reconnaissance.
If you're playing Recon, you should generally be:
  • Ahead of the squad.
  • On the side of the route.
  • Searching buildings.
  • Searching dark areas.
  • Checking dead ends.
  • Completing missions.
  • Recovering items.
  • Bringing important items toward the squad.
2–3 proactive Recons can effectively complete most of the game's missions.
However, Recon should not wander around aimlessly.
Your job is not:
"Go as far away from the team as possible."
Your job is:
"Go somewhere useful, accomplish something, and remain capable of getting back to the squad."

2. Skills & Abilities

Recon has several ways to build, and there is no single perfect build. However, understanding what each skill actually does—and how the abilities work together—is essential.

Primary Skills

These are the core Recon skills and abilities:

1. Under-Barrel Shotgun — E

The Under-Barrel Shotgun gives Recon an additional close-range weapon and is one of your best emergency tools.
Use it when:
  • Sprint is on cooldown
  • A fast enemy is closing on you.
  • You need to quickly kill a Dog.
  • You need to finish a Devourer.
  • Multiple Mutalisks are approaching.
  • You need immediate burst damage to escape.
The shotgun uses energy to load/fire ammunition, so don't waste it unnecessarily.
Advanced technique: Combine its use with a Benelli for extremely high burst damage, (double shotgun blast)
This can quickly kill or heavily damage dangerous enemies such as Devourers.

2. Decoy — D

Decoy is one of Recon's most versatile utility abilities. Decoy can be healed with healing ray and Nannonade, but cannot receive other buffs.
It can:
  • Draw enemy aggro.
  • Distract enemies while you activate Disguise.
  • Give you time to search for something.
  • Tank a Baneling.
  • Help control enemy movement.
  • Hold a boss's attention temporarily.
  • Be combined with Drone Strike or explosives.
Use Decoys deliberately.
A Decoy doesn't simply distract an enemy—it can influence where the enemy moves.
This is especially important during boss fights. A Decoy placed on the wrong side of a boss can cause the boss to move toward an undesirable entrance or position.
Before placing one, ask:
"Where will the enemy go after it attacks the Decoy?"

3. Endurance — Passive

Endurance improves your mobility and interaction efficiency, including:
  • Movement speed.
  • Sprint performance.
  • Sprint cooldown/duration.
  • Decreased Interact speed.
Movement speed is effectively one of Recon's primary defensive tools.
If you can outrun the enemy, you don't have to fight it.
The interaction improvement is also extremely valuable. Spending less time standing still means spending less time exposed while searching objectives, crates, vehicles, and other locations.
General recommendation: Prioritize Endurance heavily and eventually max it.

4. Flaregun — F

The Flaregun provides additional vision. It does not improve much with upgrades, and I wouldn’t recommend putting more than 1 point in it.
Use it to:
  • Check around corners.
  • Mark infected trees.
  • Provide vision for teammates.
  • Reveal dangerous enemies.
  • Stun ground targets and groups of air units
The Flaregun can stun enemies, making it particularly useful for creating enough distance to escape or target fast moving enemies with drone strike.

These abilities provide much of Recon's team utility and scouting capability.

Disguise — X+D

Disguise allows Recon to move through dangerous areas without immediately attracting normal enemy attention.
It is particularly useful for:
  • Searching mission objectives.
  • Searching buildings.
  • Recovering distant items.
  • Moving through enemy concentrations.
  • Escaping situations where you would otherwise be surrounded.

Disguise is not immunity

Disguise can be compromised by things such as:
  • Taking damage.
  • Attacking.
  • Certain environmental hazards, such as gas.
  • Bosses can detect you.

Also, Disguise requires an activation/channel period. Don't wait until an enemy is already standing on top of you.
Create distance first, then activate Disguise.

Drone — X+X

The Drone is one of Recon's strongest late-game abilities. It has 4 missiles available to fire.
It can:
  • Scout dangerous areas.
  • Detect large enemies.
  • Provide overwatch for the squad.
  • Attack enemies using Drone Strike.
  • Damage or kill bosses.

Drone overwatch

The Drone isn't static or limited to the location where it was initially deployed.
You can reposition it to provide air support somewhere else.
You can move the drone using Hotkey X.
This allows you to cover squad or team mates while on the move or you can send it across map to provide overwatch of team mates in trouble or newly spawned mini marines.

Single Drone Strike — X+A

The Single Drone Strike fires one drone missile at the designated target.
It is useful when you need controlled damage against a specific enemy without committing the Drone's entire missile payload.
Use it for:
  • Dangerous individual enemies.
  • Boss damage.
  • Infected trees if kuku spawns on it
  • Finishing weakened targets.
  • Destroying nydus (2 hits)
Because you're only firing one missile, it is generally easier to control than the full Drone Strike.

Drone Strike — X+S

Drone Strike fires all (4) available drone missiles simultaneously. Aiming is done via autotarget.
This is Recon's major burst-damage option and can deal enormous damage when used correctly.
It is particularly effective against:
  • Bosses.
  • High-priority targets.
  • Infected trees.

WARNING: Friendly Fire

This is one of the easiest ways for Recon to accidentally teamkill the squad.
The Drone's targeting can change. You may initially designate a boss, only for the Drone to switch targets to another enemy positioned directly beside your teammates.
Before firing, always check:
  1. What is currently targeted?
  2. Where is the target?
  3. Where is the squad?
  4. Where will the missiles land?
Never casually use Drone Strike around your teammates.
It is much safer when you are operating independently and the target is well away from the squad.

Recon's Core Philosophy

Recon isn't primarily about standing with the squad and trading damage. The class excels at mobility, information, infiltration, and creating opportunities for the rest of the team.
A good Recon should be able to:
Move quickly → scout → mark → infiltrate → complete objectives → escape → provide overwatch → strike high-value targets.

3. What to Carry

Recon has the best inventory (+4 backpack slots) in the game, but that doesn't mean you should fill every slot with junk.
Your basic survival kit should be:

Minimum equipment

  • Side Weapon
  • Antivenom
  • Armor
Ideally:
  • Benelli/shotgun
  • MP5/MPZ or M29
  • Antivenom
  • KCA
  • Fibrin Bandages
  • Medical supplies
Whenever possible, I prefer to find these items on the map rather than take them from base.

Antivenom is mandatory

Always carry Antivenom.
Venom can:
  • Reduce movement speed.
  • Reduce vision.
  • Cause you to miss shots.
  • Severely impair your ability to escape.
For a Recon, losing movement speed can be fatal.
A Recon without Antivenom is gambling with their life.

Armor

KCA is an excellent general-purpose option because it provides protection without heavily compromising your movement speed.
Remember:
Movement speed is part of your defense.
Don't choose armor purely because it provides more damage reduction.

Weapons

MP5 / MPZ

Excellent against:
  • Light enemies
  • Mutalisks
  • Dogs and hellhounds
  • General ghouls, worms and zombies
Poor against (armored units):
  • Roaches
  • Devourers
  • Heavy enemies

Benelli / Shotgun

The shotgun is your emergency weapon.
Use it for:
  • Dogs
  • Devourers
  • Multiple Mutalisks
  • Fast enemies
  • Enemies that are about to catch you
Don't waste shotgun ammunition on slow enemies you can simply outrun.

4. How to Recon

This is the most important section of the guide.
Recon is not simply:
"Run around and pick up items."
You should constantly be evaluating:
  1. Where is the squad going?
  2. What areas have already been searched?
  3. What areas are unlikely to be searched by the squad?
  4. What missions are currently active?
  5. What wave is coming?
  6. What enemies could spawn?
  7. What does the squad currently need?
  8. How quickly can I get back if something goes wrong?

Your basic loop

A good Recon's gameplay often looks like this:
Run ahead → search side area → collect useful items → move important items toward the squad → complete missions → continue ahead → return when necessary.
Don't spend 30 seconds searching a building that the squad is about to enter anyway.
Instead:
Search the building the squad probably won't enter.
That is the value you provide.

A good rule for Recon

Never travel somewhere without a reason.
Before entering an area, know what you're trying to accomplish.
Examples:
  • "There are two buildings here the squad won't search."
  • "There are unsearched crates in this area, and no crep"
  • "There is an ambulance or police car here."
  • "I need to find the missing class."
  • "Bella is in this area."
  • "The squad is heading this direction, so I'll search ahead and put useful items on their route."
If you cannot answer why you're going somewhere, you're probably wasting time.

5. Scouting & Routing

Every Squad Leader has a slightly different route.
Learn your Leader's habits.
If your Leader consistently:
Outer route → Inner route → Base
then you can predict where the squad will be several minutes from now.
That allows you to operate ahead of them without constantly checking the minimap.

Search the sides

Your priority should generally be:
Squad route → side areas → buildings → forests → dead ends → difficult areas
The squad will naturally search the main road.
Your job is to search the places they won't.

Move items onto the road

If you find something useful but cannot carry it:
Move it to the road where the squad will pass.
This is particularly useful for:
  • Explosives
  • Energy Cells
  • Class equipment
  • Armor
  • Weapons
  • Medical supplies
Don't leave an important item hidden inside a building simply because you can't carry it.

Don't over-search an area

Recon is about coverage, not completely looting one location.
If an area is becoming dangerous:
Leave.
You can always return later.
A Recon who searches every corner of the forest until they are surrounded isn't doing a better job.
They are about to die.

6. Missions

Mission knowledge is one of the biggest differences between an average Recon and an excellent Recon.

Confidential Reports — Crep

This should be one of your highest priorities.

How Crep works

There are approximately 20 crates (different from cache’s, crates look like little equipment sacks) scattered around the map at the start of the game.
One of those 20 crates contains the first Confidential Report.
During the early game, the entire squad should prioritize searching crates until the first Crep is found.
Bonus Tip: you can include coordinates above your minimap via the in game options menu in bottom left. Once active, if you click anywhere on the map it will show the coordinates of the selected location.
Once you find the crep:
  1. Click the Crep to decode it.
  2. It gives coordinates for the next report.
  3. Use the minimap coordinates or the modern -p crep command.
  4. Travel to the location.
  5. Find and decode the next crep.
  6. Repeat for the remaining reports.
  7. Complete the mission.
Once completed, the mission rewards the squad with a Liquid Body Armor drop.
On Nightmare, the reward also includes a Vahana Tactical Nuke.

Why Recon should do crep

The reports are randomly located.
It makes little sense to drag the entire squad across the map to collect one.
A Recon can simply:
Leave the squad → retrieve the crep → return or intercept the squad.
This is exactly the type of job Recon exists to perform.

Veteran Modus

On Veteran mode, approaching a crep can cause a Nydus to spawn nearby.
Don't blindly run toward the objective. Use disguise.

Bella Mission

Bella is a small dog that spawns randomly somewhere on the map. Recon is particularly well suited to handling the Bella mission because of their mobility, vision, Disguise, and ability to operate independently from the main squad.

Finding Bella

Once Bella spawns, Recon should make finding her a priority.
Search her to begin the mission, then take responsibility for locating the three bones scattered randomly around the map.
Remember where you found Bella, then systematically travel the map. Walk every road and check the docks—you’ll find all three bones 99% of the time if you hit all the routes.
Bella will help you locate the bones:
  • Travel across the map with Bella, checking roads, docks, and other accessible areas.
  • When Bella gets within range of a bone or buried cache, she will bark and point her nose toward it. (She will always give priority to bones, if both a bone and cache are in the same area)
  • Follow the direction Bella is pointing to narrow down the location.
  • If trees, buildings, or other obstacles block your view, use your Flaregun to illuminate the area and locate the item.
  • Once you locate the bone, use your Recon abilities to safely reach it and search it.
  • Repeat the process until all three bones have been found.

Buried Caches

Bella can also bark when she detects buried caches.
These are different from the three bones, but they can contain useful loot.
When Bella starts barking somewhere that isn't indicating a bone, look around the area for the buried cache.

Why Recon Should Handle Bella

Bella is much safer when handled by Recon rather than staying with the main squad.
Bella is:
  • Small.
  • Easy to lose in a crowd.
  • Easily confused with regular infected.
  • Prone to falling behind the squad.
  • Weirdly attracted to dangerous places.
It is surprisingly common for someone in the main squad to accidentally kill Bella, especially when she is standing next to or directly in the middle of a group of infected.
She also has a bad habit of standing next to things the squad is preparing to destroy.
Bella has no concept of personal safety.
Keeping Bella with Recon allows the Recon player to move her around independently while the main squad continues its own objectives. It also dramatically reduces the chance of accidentally killing her and ruining the mission.

Recon's Bella Routine

Find Bella → Search Bella → Locate bones → Follow her barking → Use Flaregun to find the bone → Safely reach and search it → keep searching
Rumor has it Bella was once a bomb-sniffing dog before the outbreak.
Honestly, given her apparent talent for standing directly beside things that are about to explode, the evidence is questionable.

Police & Ambulance Mission

Recon is particularly well suited to this mission.
Your faster interact speed allows you to search:
  • Police cars
  • Ambulances
throughout the map without requiring the entire squad to stop.
If you're already passing one:
Search it.
This is a perfect example of something Recon can accomplish while the rest of the squad continues moving.

Infected Trees

Recon should actively look for infected trees.
When you find one:
Mark it with Roadflare or Flaregun.
Don't simply announce it in chat and assume somebody saw the message.
The physical marker allows the squad and Radio Operator to visually identify the location.

Other Missions and Responsibilities

Recon may also be responsible for helping with:
  • Drone mission
  • Missing Dog Tags
  • Missing equipment
  • Respawned Mini Marine drops
  • Shiva
  • LBA
  • Other randomly located objectives
If the Leader asks:
"Has anyone found X?"
Recon should immediately think:
"That's my job."

7. Important Waves

Recon can spend much of the early game away from the squad.
That changes as the waves become more dangerous.
*for -vet mode, be advised that a random boss will spawn every odd wave, return to the squad until it is dead and then continue on your mission
You should know what is coming before it spawns.

Wave 2 & 3

First major boss/large enemy encounters begin. Abomination spawns, Mutalisks will spawn (w3).
You can generally continue scouting, but start paying more attention to enemy positioning.

Wave 3–4

More dangerous enemies begin appearing.
By Wave 4:
You should ideally have a shotgun.
Dogs and Hellhounds become a threat.
If you don't have a shotgun or appropriate equipment:
Stay closer to the squad.

Wave 4 — Lyganesh

Lyganesh is dangerous but usually isn't a reason to panic.
Don't wander directly into it.
Inform the squad if you encounter it and maintain enough distance to escape.

Wave 5

The Devourers begin spawning and /Mutalisk pressure increases.
This is a good time to start reducing how aggressively you scout.

Wave 6 — ETA

Etaphreven and additional dangerous enemies appear.
This is one of the waves where Recon should generally be closer to the squad.
Use:
  • Roadflare
  • Flaregun
  • Shotgun
  • Drone
to support the team.
If you are inexperienced:
Come home for Wave 6.
There is no prize for dying on the opposite side of the map.

Wave 7 — Major Air Wave

This is one of the most dangerous waves for an isolated Recon.
Multiple Mutalisks and Devourers can appear.
A group of Mutalisks can kill a Recon extremely quickly if they catch them in an open area.
If you aren't extremely confident and well equipped:
Stay with the squad during Wave 7.
Vision is also extremely important here.
Use Roadflare and Flaregun.

Wave 8 — Varuna

Stay close to the squad.
Varuna and the additional air spawns make independent scouting significantly more dangerous.
Recon's job here becomes:
  • Vision
  • Flare placement
  • Drone support
  • Emergency shotgun
  • Helping locate Varuna
  • Avoiding unnecessary deaths
A good Recon can still operate independently during Wave 6 or 8, but newer players should absolutely stay with the squad. Setting a trap with decoy and killing the varuna with a drone is possible, also using flaregun to slow varuna and hitting it with a drone strike can be effective. But you still will need to contend with a massive muta spawn headed your way. You will likely need Fibrin bandages and other medical items.

Wave 9

Wave 9 is another major transition.
Multiple bosses begin moving around the map.
This is also when Shiva drops somewhere randomly on the map.
Recon should be particularly aware of:
  • Boss locations
  • Squad position
  • Evil Marines
  • Shiva
  • Has LBA been picked up?
  • Flare placement
A Recon can potentially use their mobility and Disguise to recover Shiva or other important items that would be dangerous for the entire squad to retrieve.
But:
Don't turn a difficult Shiva retrieval into a dead Recon.

Wave 10+

The squad generally enters the base/turtle phase.
At this point, Recon's job becomes less about roaming the entire map and more about:
  • Vision/ Flare placement
  • Drone support
  • Decoy to absorb damage / draw aggro
  • Emergency combat with shotgun
  • Finding remaining mission items
  • Helping with special objectives
  • Assisting with the rescue of respawns
There is usually less useful independent scouting to do.
Once you have:
  • Searched the important areas
  • Completed missions
  • Recovered important equipment
you can often reskill into another class or simply become a support Recon.

Wave 12 — Drone Mission

If the Drone mission occurs, Recon becomes particularly valuable.
Use:
  • Roadflare
  • Flaregun
  • Disguise
  • Movement speed
to search the mission area quickly.
Find the drone components, carry stims, Disguise and independently bring them to the Raven.

8. Combat & Staying Alive

The most important Recon combat skill is knowing when not to fight.
Most enemies are slower than you.
That means:
You don't need to kill them.
If a group of slow enemies is chasing you:
Run around them.
If you encounter a fast enemy:
Kill it.

Dogs

Dogs are fast and dangerous.
Use:
  • Shotgun
  • MP5/MPZ
  • Under-Barrel Shotgun
Don't allow them to repeatedly hit you.

Mutalisks

A single Mutalisk can often be handled with your normal weapon.
Carrying balltip ammo, MPZ or M29 will absolutely melt mutalisks
Multiple Mutalisks are much more dangerous.
If several Mutalisks approach from the same direction:
Benelli Shotgun + Under-Barrel Shotgun
If they approach from multiple directions:
  1. Sprint.
  2. Get them behind you.
  3. Create distance.
  4. Turn and shotgun them together.
Don't stand still trying to fight them individually.

Devourers

Devourers are one of the most dangerous enemies for an isolated Recon.
An MP5 will barely tickle this armored unit.
Use the shotgun.
A useful technique is:
Shotgun → move away → Shotgun → move away
Don't face-tank the Devourer.
You can also combine:
Benelli → Under-Barrel Shotgun → Benelli
If you don't have a shotgun:
Get back to the squad.

Never Let Yourself Get Surrounded

One of the most important Recon habits is keeping enemies behind you.
If you stay in one location too long, enemies begin approaching from multiple directions.
You want:
One direction of danger → enemies behind you → clear escape route
Not:
Enemies surrounding you → Sprint on cooldown → panic
A good rule:
Don't get greedy.
If you're standing in a building thinking, "I'll just search one more crate," and enemies are approaching:
Leave.

Fractures Are Especially Dangerous

Recon depends heavily on movement speed.
A fracture can dramatically reduce your ability to escape.
Avoid taking unnecessary hits.
The goal isn't to kill everything. The goal is to avoid being hit in the first place.

9. Drone, Flaregun & Decoy Tactics

The modern Drone deserves special attention because it gives Recon meaningful combat utility.

Drone + Decoy

One particularly useful combination is:
Decoy → Boss attacks Decoy → Drone Strike
The Decoy prevents the boss from constantly moving toward you, making it much easier to land Drone Strike.
This can be particularly useful when operating away from the squad.

Drone + Flaregun

Flaregun can help slow/stun enemies.
Against fast targets, this can make Drone Strike much easier to land. (without the stun Drone will often miss its strikes unless it is close to its target)

Drone vs. bosses

Drone can be used against:
  • Devourers
  • Roaches
  • Abominations
  • Lyganesh
  • ETA
  • Varuna
  • Other large/high-priority enemies
However:
Don't waste Drone Strike just because a large enemy exists.
Think about whether the damage will actually save the squad resources or prevent danger.

Drone Safety

This deserves its own warning:
Drone Strike can teamkill.
Before firing:
STOP.
Look at:
  1. The target.
  2. The squad.
  3. The rocket trajectory/impact area.
  4. Other enemies that could become the designated target.
If the target is near the squad:
Don't fire.

10. Inventory & Item Priorities

Recon has excellent carrying capacity.
Use it strategically.

High-priority items

Generally prioritize:
Critical class equipment (if needed)
  • Firebat kit
  • Demolition kit
  • Heavy Support kit
  • Medic kit
  • Marksman kit
  • Radio Operator kit
  • Other needed class equipment
Important unique equipment
  • M15
  • Shiva
  • LBA
  • Vahana
  • RBA
  • M29
  • Benelli/shotguns
Important supplies
  • Fibrin Bandages
  • Medkits
  • Road Flares
  • Energy Cells
  • Armor
  • Shotgun shells
  • ammunition
The exact priority changes depending on what the squad already has.

Don't blindly follow an item priority list

For example:
If the squad already has three shotguns but desperately needs Fibrin:
Take the Fibrin.
If the Firebat has no RBA and one is sitting in a building:
The RBA is extremely high priority.
Recon should be thinking:
"What does the squad need?"
not:
"What does my predetermined item list say?"

Ammo

Recon can carry a large amount of ammunition without occupying normal inventory space.
Use this advantage.
Eat ammunition when possible instead of filling your inventory with magazines.
This keeps your inventory available for:
  • Class equipment
  • Medical supplies
  • Weapons
  • Armor
  • Mission items
  • Important loot

Drop Items Onto the Road

You don't have to bring every item directly to base.
If the squad is going to pass through the area:
Move the item onto their route.
This is particularly useful for:
  • Explosives
  • Energy Cells
  • Class kits
  • Armor
  • Weapons
You can then continue scouting instead of making a long trip back.

11. When Should Recon Return to the Squad?

This is one of the most important judgment calls.
Return when:
  • A dangerous wave is approaching.
  • You don't have the equipment to survive alone.
  • Your health is low.
  • You are running low on Antivenom/Fibrin.
  • Your shotgun ammunition is running low.
  • The squad needs vision.
  • You have important items to deliver.
  • You don't know where the squad is going.
  • The Leader is fighting a major boss encounter.
  • You have finished the useful areas to scout.

Regular Survival

An experienced Recon can sometimes be on the opposite side of the map and still be completely safe.
That doesn't mean you should always be there.
If you are inexperienced:
Stay slightly ahead of the squad.
This gives you room to learn without putting yourself into situations where you cannot return.

12. Learning Recon Without Being Annoying

This is worth saying directly because it is one of the biggest problems with the class.
A squad full of players choosing Recon and then running around with the team accomplishes very little.
If you're learning:

Start simple

  1. Learn the squad's route.
  2. Run slightly ahead.
  3. Search the sides of the route.
  4. Search buildings the squad won't enter.
  5. Move useful items onto the road.
  6. Learn the crep locations.
  7. Learn the ambulance/police mission.
  8. Learn the dangerous areas.
  9. Learn when the dangerous waves occur.
  10. Gradually increase how far you operate from the squad.
Don't try to immediately become the guy who is three screens away from the team during Wave 8.
Learn the map first.

13. Common Recon Mistakes

❌ Playing Recon but never reconning

If you're following the squad around all game, you're not providing much Recon value.

❌ Exploring without a purpose

Don't run into random buildings just because they're there.
Have a reason.

❌ Ignoring missions

Especially Crep.
If you are playing Recon and the squad is doing Crep while you are looting random buildings, you're missing one of the class's most important jobs.

❌ Taking unnecessary fights

You can outrun most enemies.
Run.

❌ Exploring too far without an escape plan

Before going somewhere, know:
"If something dangerous spawns here, how do I get out?"

❌ Forgetting the wave timer

Wave 7 is not the same as Wave 3.
Wave 8 is not the same as Wave 3.
Always know what is about to spawn.

❌ Running out of Antivenom

This can turn a manageable situation into a death sentence.

❌ Wasting shotgun ammunition

Don't shoot slow enemies that you can outrun.
Save the shotgun for:
  • Dogs
  • Devourers
  • Mutalisks
  • Emergency situations

❌ Carrying junk

Your inventory is valuable.
Don't fill it with:
  • Several Ball-tip ammunitions
  • Unnecessary defuse kits
  • Random low-value equipment
while leaving important supplies behind.

❌ Leaving important items hidden

If you can't carry something important:
Move it onto the squad's route.

❌ Marking trees only in chat “i see a tree here”

Physically mark them with Roadflare/Flaregun.

❌ Using Drone Strike near the squad

This is how Recon accidentally kills everyone.

❌ Using Decoy without considering boss movement

A Decoy can redirect a boss.
Don't accidentally create an entrance into the base.

❌ Being greedy

This is probably the most common Recon death.
You're one crate away from finishing an area.
Enemies are approaching.
You think:
"I'll just search this one."
Don't. Leave.

14. Quick Reference

If you only remember a few things:
Recon is an advanced class. Have some medals to increase your stats and understand the game before playing it.
Your job is to make the squad's job easier, not simply collect loot.
Stay ahead of the squad and search the areas they won't.
Learn the Leader's route so you know where the squad will be.
Prioritize Confidential Reports (Crep).
Search police cars and ambulances for missions.
Mark infected trees with Roadflare or Flaregun.
Move important items onto the squad's route if you can't carry them.
Always carry Antivenom.
Have armor and a weapon by the early waves.
Shotgun is your emergency weapon. Save the ammunition for dangerous enemies.
You don't have to kill slow enemies. Run past them.
Devourers, Dogs, Mutalisks and other fast enemies deserve your attention.
Don't get surrounded. Keep enemies behind you.
Know what wave is coming before you wander away from the squad.
Come back for dangerous waves if you're inexperienced or poorly equipped.
Use Roadflare to provide vision—not just for yourself, but for the squad.
Use Disguise to safely complete distant missions and objectives.
Use Decoy deliberately; it can redirect bosses.
Drone + Decoy or Drone + Flaregun can be a powerful attack against bosses.
Never use Drone Strike near the squad unless you are absolutely certain it is safe.
Once you've searched the important areas and completed the missions, consider switching to another class.

The Recon Mindset

Recon is difficult because there isn't always a single correct action.
A good Recon constantly thinks ahead:
Where is the squad going?
 What haven't they searched?
 What mission needs to be completed?
 What does the squad need?
 What is about to spawn?
 Can I safely accomplish this?
 How do I get back if something goes wrong?
The best Recons aren't necessarily the players who collect the most items.
They're the players who anticipate problems before the squad encounters them.
They find the Crep before the squad has to search for it.
They find the missing equipment before someone asks for it.
They mark the infected tree before the squad walks past it.
They put explosives on the road before Demo reaches the area.
They flare the dark corner before the Mutalisks arrive.
They recover the important item that would have required the entire squad to detour.
And when nothing needs to be done:
They know when to stop reconning and help the squad.
A great Recon is essentially a one-person support team operating ahead of the squad.
Once you understand the map, the missions, the waves, the Leader's route, and your own limits, Recon becomes incredibly powerful.
You aren't just scouting the map. You're making the map easier for everyone else.

1. Heavy Support Overview

Heavy Support is one of the most important classes in the squad.
The Minigunner is probably one of the most straightforward classes to play: stand in the right place, shoot the right targets, manage your ammunition, and don't die.
That simplicity can be misleading.
On higher difficulties, Heavy Support and Medic are two of the pillars holding the squad together. When the squad is being overwhelmed by Mutalisks, bosses, or large groups of enemies, a well-positioned Minigunner using Suppressing Fire can provide an enormous amount of sustained DPS and prevent the situation from spiraling out of control.
The Minigunner's job isn't just to get the most kills.
Your job is to provide the squad with:
DPS + Damage Aura + Covering Fire + Ammo Carrying capacity

Heavy Support Skills

Primary:
  • Critical Strike — Passive
  • Suppressing Fire — E
  • HEI — D
  • Ammunition Link — Passive
  • Concentration — Passive
Secondary:
  • Reload — Actions Menu

Your priorities

  1. Stay with the squad.
  2. Position yourself where you can continuously fire.
  3. Keep as many teammates as possible inside your damage aura.
  4. Maintain the squad's ammunition supply.
  5. Use Suppressing Fire when the squad needs overwhelming DPS.
  6. Reload before dangerous waves—not during them.
  7. Carry Anti-Venom and good armor.
  8. Avoid wasting ammunition on targets that don't need it.
A good Minigunner kills enemies. A great Minigunner makes the entire squad stronger.

2. Positioning — The Most Important Part of Heavy Support

Positioning is arguably the most important strategy a Minigunner can learn.
Your Minigun has enormous sustained firepower, but there is one very important limitation:
When you're moving or interacting, you're not shooting.
Every unnecessary movement or action is lost DPS.
This means you should avoid constantly wandering around the map, repositioning unnecessarily, or interacting with anything.
Your default position should be:
With the squad, preferably near the center of the group.
This accomplishes several things simultaneously:
  • Keeps teammates inside your damage aura.
  • Gives you more targets to shoot.
  • Allows you to provide covering fire.
  • Makes it easier to respond to threats attacking teammates.
  • Reduces the chance of getting isolated and killed.

Don't be Rambo

This is one of the most frustrating Heavy Support mistakes for a squad leader.
Do not run off alone looking for kills.
A Minigunner who hangs behind the squad and plays Rambo may feel like they are doing more damage, but they are often providing significantly less value to the team.
If you're using the team damage aura, this becomes even more important.
The team aura is only useful if you're actually standing with the team.
Running ahead, falling behind, searching buildings, interacting with objects, or wandering away from the squad can all leave your teammates without one of their most important buffs.
It can also create another problem:
If you get killed, the squad may have to risk their own lives trying to recover you.

Where positioning matters most

Pay particular attention to your positioning during:
  • Wave 9 boss stacking
  • Wave 12 Drone Mission
  • Wave 13 Muta Rush
  • Wave 14
  • Wave 15 Evacuation
During these situations, a strong Minigunner should generally be in the middle of the squad, not off fighting somewhere else.

3. Damage Aura

Heavy Support provides a powerful damage aura that can significantly increase the squad's overall firepower.
There are two ways to approach the aura:

Individual Damage Aura

The individual aura increases your own damage.
This is generally preferable when there are only a few Marines nearby.
As a general rule:
Fewer than 3 Marines benefiting from the team aura → Individual aura is usually better.
This makes individual aura particularly attractive in smaller games such as 3- and 4-player squads.

Team Damage Aura

The team aura increases the damage of nearby teammates.
Once enough Marines are benefiting from it, the combined squad DPS can exceed what you would gain from buffing yourself.
As a general rule:
3+ Marines benefiting from the team aura → Team aura is usually better.
The exact break-even point can vary depending on Marine types, buffs, ranks, and the situation, so treat this as a practical guideline rather than an absolute rule.

Two Minigunners

Two Heavy Supports can be extremely powerful on higher difficulties.
A common setup is:
Minigunner #1
Team Aura
Stay in the middle of the squad and maximize the number of teammates receiving the buff.
Minigunner #2
Individual Aura
Focus more heavily on personal DPS.
This allows the squad to benefit from the team aura while still having a Minigunner dedicated to maximizing their own firepower.

4. Suppressing Fire — E

Suppressing Fire is one of the Minigunner's most important abilities.
When activated, Suppress dramatically increases your sustained firepower at the cost of additional ammunition and energy.
This is your answer to:
"Everything is going to hell, the shit is hitting the fan."
When the squad is being overwhelmed, activate Suppress and put as many bullets downrange as possible.

Use Suppress when:

  • Large numbers of enemies are overwhelming the squad.
  • Mutalisks are flooding the area.
  • A dangerous heavy unit needs to die quickly.
  • A boss is attacking a teammate.
  • The squad needs a massive increase in DPS.
  • The situation is rapidly deteriorating.
When everything starts going to hell, Suppress.

Varuna

Varuna is one of the best examples of when Heavy Support can provide exceptional boss DPS.
During Wave 8, one or two Varunas can often be handled extremely effectively with Suppressing Fire.
If Varuna gets through the defensive line:
Suppress and burn it down.

Don't waste Suppress

Suppress consumes additional ammunition and energy, so don't leave it running constantly.
Normal enemies that the squad can comfortably kill do not always justify the additional ammunition expenditure.
Think of Suppress as your emergency and high-DPS tool, not your default firing mode.

5. HEI — D

HEI is an explosive ammunition ability with several important utility applications.
It can be used to:
  • Destroy corpses.
  • Destroy Nyduses.
  • Clear visual obstructions.
  • Detonate bombs.
  • Finish severely weakened heavy units.
  • Help clear trees when necessary.

Nydus

One of the most useful applications of HEI is destroying Nyduses.
HEI can quickly destroy a Nydus without necessarily destroying the valuable items underneath it.
This is particularly useful during Wave 14, when Nyduses can become a major problem and corpses can begin accumulating inside the base.

HEI can teamkill

Be extremely careful with HEI around teammates.
HEI is explosive and can kill your own squad.
For example:
Boss attacks Flamethrower → don't HEI the boss.
HEI may kill the Flamethrower instead.
In this situation:
Suppress the boss.
Suppress is much safer when you need to rapidly kill a boss that is actively attacking a teammate.

Don't cut down the entire forest

HEI can also remove trees and other visual obstructions.
This is useful when an obstruction is actually affecting the squad.
It is not useful to spend hundreds of rounds removing every tree you see.
Clear what matters. Don't waste half your ammo turning the forest into a parking lot.

6. Ammunition Management

Managing ammunition is one of your primary responsibilities.
You aren't just carrying ammunition for yourself.
You're helping maintain the squad's ammunition reserve.
During the early and mid game, your primary goal is:
Stock ammunition in base.

How much should you carry?

As a general guideline:
Early game
Don't go below ~10 magazines.
Mid game
Try to maintain ~20+ magazines.
This allows you to contribute ammunition to the base while still maintaining enough for yourself.
The exact amount will vary depending on difficulty, squad size, and ammunition consumption.

After Wave 9

Once the squad has established the base:
60+ magazines after Wave 9 = you're in good shape.
If you already have a large reserve, don't greedily carry every ammunition drop.
Leave ammunition for the rest of the squad.

Know the squad's ammunition situation

During base phase, you should know:
  • How much ammunition is stored in base.
  • How much you're carrying.
  • Whether the squad is running low.
  • Whether the next major wave will require additional ammunition.
This becomes particularly important during the Wave 12 Drone Mission.
The leader has to choose between a supply drop containing:
  • Ammunition
  • Medical supplies
  • Explosives
If the squad is low on ammunition:
Tell the leader.
The Radio Operator can also help call ammunition drops when necessary.

7. Reloading

Reload timing is one of the easiest ways to end the game early.
The Minigun has a long reload.
Getting caught in a major wave with an empty weapon can leave the squad without one of its primary sources of sustained DPS.
Reload before the danger—not during it.

How to reload

Reloading is now performed through the Actions menu.
This was changed in a 2026 update because Minigunners were accidentally triggering reload with the R key.

Start of the wave

If you have not invested heavily in Ammunition Link:
Reload at the beginning of every wave.
You want to enter the wave fully loaded.

Base phase

After approximately Wave 10, reload timing becomes particularly important.
You want to spend the peak of the wave shooting, not standing still during a reload.
A good reload window is:
Wave is dying down → enemies are fizzling out → reload → Demolition heads out → Minigun is ready to provide covering fire.
Try to finish your reload before Demolition leaves the base to place road flares.
You want the Minigunner ready to shoot while the squad is moving out.

Critical reloads

9. Ammunition Link

Ammunition Link increases the amount of ammunition you can load and carry.
This is extremely useful because it provides two benefits:
  1. You can carry more ammunition into battle.
  2. You can transport more ammunition back to base.
The downside:
Ammunition Link reduces your movement speed.

When should you upgrade it?

There are two reasonable approaches.
Experienced / high-ranking players
If you have substantial movement-speed bonuses from medals and other stats:
Upgrade Ammunition Link earlier.
I generally recommend reaching Level 3 and eventually considering Level 5 for late-game ammunition capacity.
Newer / low-ranking players
If you don't have many movement-speed bonuses:
0–2 points early is perfectly reasonable.
You can delay Ammunition Link until the squad enters the base phase.
Save your skill points and invest in it once the additional ammunition capacity becomes more valuable than the movement speed.

General rule

Early game: prioritize damage and mobility.
Late game: ammunition capacity becomes increasingly valuable.
If you choose not to invest heavily in Ammunition Link:
Make sure you reload at the beginning of every wave.

13. Common Heavy Support Mistakes

❌ Playing Rambo

Running off alone to get kills removes your aura and covering fire from the squad.
Stay with the team.

❌ Moving constantly

Every second spent moving is a second spent not firing.

❌ Reloading during a major wave

A long reload during a Muta Rush can leave the squad without critical DPS.
Reload before the danger.

❌ Reloading both Miniguns simultaneously

With two Heavy Supports, simultaneous reloads can leave the squad without Minigun firepower.
Stagger your reloads.

❌ Wasting ammunition

Don't dump ammunition into enemies that the squad can easily kill without you.
Your ammunition is a squad resource.

❌ Using Suppress constantly

Suppress is powerful, but it consumes additional ammunition and energy.
Save it for moments when the extra DPS actually matters.

❌ Using HEI carelessly

HEI can teamkill.
If a boss is standing on a teammate, Suppress it instead.

❌ Ignoring Nyduses

Wave 14 can become much more difficult if Nyduses are allowed to generate large corpse piles inside the base.
Use HEI when appropriate.

❌ Ignoring Anti-Venom

A Venomed Minigunner can miss critical shots and lose a significant amount of effectiveness.
Always carry Anti-Venom.

❌ Carrying ammunition greedily

Once you have a healthy reserve, additional ammunition is often more useful in the base or on another player.
Manage the squad's ammunition, not just your own.

Your priorities are simple:

  6. Hold the base entrance and don't die.
  7. Fight elite units and bosses.

1. Marksman Overview

Marksman is a high-impact class that quietly makes a difference from a distance.
Marksman is not a core class. Your core classes should be filled first, and a squad can complete most games without a Marksman. However, Marksman becomes increasingly valuable on higher difficulties and game modifiers, where additional bosses and elite enemies make long-range precision damage much more important.
Marksman is like a silent switch: flipped correctly, it changes everything; left unflipped, nothing changes.
Marksman is the cannon on a well-oiled machine. When functioning correctly and targeting the right enemies opportunistically, a good Marksman can devastate enemy bosses and elite units from a distance, often without the rest of the squad even realizing how much work they are doing. They quietly pluck high-value targets from the battlefield before those enemies ever become a serious problem.
The Marksman's primary jobs are to:
  • Kill heavy and elite enemies from a distance.
  • Kill zombies permanently so they cannot get back up.
  • Provide long-range overwatch for the squad.
  • Identify and ping dangerous enemies before they reach the team.
  • Help Demolition conserve explosives by killing targets with your rifle.
  • Provide vision and scouting with Scope.
  • Destroy Nyduses and concentrated corpse piles when appropriate.
Marksman deals tremendous damage, but the class has a very slow rate of fire and can only attack one target at a time. This makes Marksman extremely vulnerable to groups of weak enemies.
Your greatest strength is also your greatest weakness:
You can kill almost anything at extreme range, but you are terrible at dealing with a swarm standing on top of you.
For this reason, Marksman should generally stay with the squad, preferably toward the center where other players can protect them from swarms. Do not mistake your long range for survivability

Canister also has a relatively small target area, making it difficult to hit small or fast-moving targets. Lyganesh is particularly deceptive because, despite its large appearance, its actual collision area is relatively small.

Nydus

  • Level 4 Canister: Can kill a Nydus after it has completely emerged.
  • Level 5 Canister: Can kill a Nydus during earlier stages of emergence.
This makes Canister particularly valuable during Wave 14, and on inferno mode.

Scope — V

Scope uses energy to greatly increase Marksman's ability to see and attack distant targets.
While scoped:
  • Your vision becomes concentrated around the cursor.
  • Your normal surrounding vision is greatly reduced.
  • You can see and attack targets from extreme range.
  • Vision remains briefly after moving the cursor.
Scope is useful for:
  • Finding distant enemies.
  • Finding important items.
  • Finding Wave 12 drone modules.
  • Scouting the sides of the route while moving with the squad.
  • Identifying bosses before they reach the squad.
  • Covering teammates while they search dark areas.

Scope Safety

Do not scope while standing somewhere unsafe.
Scoping drastically reduces your awareness of your immediate surroundings and makes Marksman much easier to kill.
When scoping:
Be in the center of the squad and make sure someone is covering you.
Before scoping, you can use Disable Movement (X+D) to prevent accidentally moving your Marksman while your camera is focused elsewhere. You can also enable Disable Auto Focus from the Actions menu to prevent your camera from automatically returning to your Marine when Scope is interrupted.

Marksmanship — Passive

Marksmanship increases your rifle's damage and attack speed.
Its levels provide:
  1. +20 damage
  2. +45 damage
  3. +70 damage
  4. +95 damage
  5. +120 damage
Marksmanship also allows you to trade some damage for increased rate of fire.
This can be useful if you find yourself separated from the squad and need to kill weaker enemies more quickly.

3. Preferred Build  

 Marksman's rifle is already extremely powerful, so there isn't much need for complicated builds. Your skill points mainly determine whether you want to focus on scouting and vision or boss killing and utility.

Build 1: Scouting & Vision

Useful when the squad does not have a Recon.
Focus on:
  • Eagle Eye
  • Marksmanship
  • Scope
Max Scope to scout the map, find important items, track enemy movement, and provide vision for the squad. Especially if your squad does not have a Recon.
Shooting a high-value target while scoped also reveals it to your teammates.
Scope a Fiend → shoot → ping. Your squad now knows exactly where a fiend is.
With this build, it helps to have knowledge of boss waves, spawn timings, common boss routes, and important events.
Remember: Marksman's basic rifle is already strong enough to kill heavy units without investing heavily into Canister or Recoil.

Build 2: Combat Marksman — Recommended for difficult game modes with many bosses spawning periodically

This is also my preferred build:
  • Max Eagle Eye
  • Max Marksmanship
  • Canister → Level 4
  • Max Recoil
Start with 1 point in Canister and 1 point in Recoil, then focus on Eagle Eye and Marksmanship.
Level 4 Canister is important for destroying Nyduses, especially during Wave 14. Recoil provides the boss-killing power that makes Marksman especially useful against heavy and elite enemies.
I will occasionally reskill into Scope when needed. For example, on Veteran, I may sacrifice some Recoil to help snipe Banelings before they reach explosives. On Wave 12, Marksman should also use Scope to help locate drone modules, especially when no Radio Operator is available.
Build for what the squad needs. You can always reskill (take class off and put it back on).
Canister can be difficult to land on fast-moving bosses, but that's part of what makes it fun. Successfully sniping something like Varuna with Canister is difficult, but extremely satisfying.

4. What Should Marksman Shoot?  

Everything.
Marksman is the only class whose weapon permanently kills every zombie it shoots. Zombies killed by Marksman do not get back up, making every shot valuable—even against ordinary enemies.
Prioritize dangerous targets when possible, but don't be afraid to shoot anything in your sights. Removing a zombie permanently means one less enemy the squad has to deal with.
Marksman's long range also allows you to kill enemies that your teammates may not even know are there.

High-priority targets

Prioritize:
  • Roaches
  • Banelings
  • Devourers
  • Hellhounds
  • Dogs
  • Mutalisks
  • Fiends
  • Slashers
  • Hesiod
  • Mrit
  • Varuna
  • Sindir
  • Power Rangers / colorful dogs
  • Other elite and heavy units
Your goal is to remove dangerous enemies before they reach the squad.
For example:
A Roach is approaching the Demolition → shoot the Roach.
A Baneling is moving toward the squad → shoot the Baneling before it reaches the team.
A boss is approaching while Demolition is preparing explosives → damage the boss and give Demolition more time.
If you can shoot it, kill it. Every zombie you remove is one less zombie the squad has to fight.

5. Marksman & Demolition  

This section is primarily for the harder game modes.
One of the most important ways Marksman contributes to the squad is by saving explosives.
Demolition has limited explosives and needs them for the enemies that Marksman cannot efficiently kill.
If Marksman can kill an elite or boss with their rifle or Recoil:
Let Marksman do it.
A strong Marksman can eliminate many heavy targets before Demolition needs to spend explosives on them.
This is especially valuable on difficult modes where the squad has more bosses.

Marksman as a finisher

If Demolition has already damaged a boss:
Marksman can finish it instead of forcing Demolition to spend another bomb.
Likewise, if Demolition is preparing a bomb stack and you can safely kill an approaching elite before it reaches them, do it.

6. Bosses & Overwatch  

Marksman should know which bosses spawn during each wave and where they are likely to come from.
This becomes especially important during Wave 9 and the later base-defense waves.

When you see a boss

If you locate a dangerous boss:
  1. Shoot it to reveal it to the squad.
  2. Ping its location.
  3. Tell the Squad Leader if the boss is ahead of the squad.
  4. Do not start killing bosses simply because you see them.
During Wave 9, for example, the squad may need to avoid moving into a boss encounter.
Information is often more valuable than damage.

7. Base Defense  

During the base phase, Marksman becomes extremely effective.
Your primary jobs are:
  • Kill incoming bosses.
  • Snipe Banelings.
  • Kill Roaches and other dangerous units.
  • Protect Demolition.
  • Watch the sides of the base and routes of approach
  • Destroy Nyduses.
  • Provide long-range overwatch.

Protect Demolition

Demolition frequently has to leave the safety of the base to place explosives.
If a dangerous enemy approaches them:
Shoot it. Or recoil shoot it.
A Marksman can often save Demolition simply by removing one dangerous enemy before it reaches them.

Two Marksmen

Two Marksmen can provide extremely strong base defense.
On Wave 14, If both Marksmen have Level 4+ Canister, they can alternate killing Nyduses:
Marksman 1 → Nydus
Marksman 2 → Nydus
Marksman 1 → Nydus
Marksman 2 → Nydus
This can prevent Nyduses from continuously producing enemies and building large corpse piles inside the base.
Two Marksmen can also divide responsibility around the base:
One watches the north side. One watches the south side.
This allows each player to focus on a smaller area while maintaining overwatch over the routes leading into the base.

8. Wave 12 — Drone Modules  

Marksman is exceptionally good at Wave 12's drone mission.
Use Scope to search for the drone modules.
Once you locate a module:
Ping it and tell the squad where it is.
Your long-range vision makes this one of the clearest situations where Marksman can perform a task that would otherwise take considerable time.

9. Wave 14 — Nydus  

Wave 14 is one of the most important waves for Canister.
Before Nyduses appear:
Know where the important items in your base are.
Canister is a concentrated explosive and can:
  • Kill teammates.
  • Destroy important items.
  • Clear corpses.
  • Kill Nyduses.
Keep the base organized so you can safely place your Canister shots.
If you have two Marksmen with Level 4+ Canister, alternate your Nydus kills to maintain continuous coverage.

10. Wave 13 & Hellspawn  

Marksman is extremely valuable during the late-game boss waves, but there are some enemies you cannot simply solo from a distance.
If you see Lyganesh or Hellspawn approaching:
  1. Ping the boss.
  2. Alert the Squad Leader.
  3. Damage it from range.
  4. Give Demolition time to prepare the required explosives.
Your job is not necessarily to kill every boss yourself.
Your job is to make sure the boss reaches the squad as weak as possible.

11. Inventory  

Marksman should build their inventory around their role as a heavy-unit killer.

Always carry

  • Armor
  • Ballistic ammunition — preferred
  • Anti-Venom
Your remaining slots should generally be used to transport useful items back to base.
Once the squad reaches the base phase, you can use your remaining inventory space for:
  • Medical supplies
  • Important equipment (vahana, shiva)
  • Other items the squad needs

Anti-Venom

Anti-Venom is particularly important for Marksman.
Venom can:
  • Reduce vision.
  • Reduce movement speed.
  • Affect accuracy.
A Marksman who cannot accurately hit targets is losing a large portion of their value.
Cure Venom immediately.

Why Ballistic?

Ballistic's +25% attack speed is particularly valuable on Marksman because the class's biggest weakness is its slow firing rate.
Marksman also naturally prevents killed zombies from getting back up, so the corpse-destruction component of AP provides much less additional value.

Uranium

Use Uranium if Ballistic is unavailable.
Its 15% chance to deal 150% damage provides additional burst potential, which is particularly useful once Marksman begins focusing heavily on bosses.

13. Marksman Positioning  

This is one of the most important parts of playing the class correctly.

Stay with the squad.

Marksman has:
  • Extremely high damage.
  • Extremely long range.
  • Extremely poor crowd control.
  • Slow attack speed.
  • Cannot use a sidearm.
That means wandering away from the squad is dangerous.
If several zombies reach you:
You can die surprisingly quickly.

Good positioning

Ideally:
  • Stay near the center of the squad.
  • Stay near Heavy Support.
  • Keep enough distance from enemies to use your rifle safely.
  • Maintain line of sight to important targets.
  • Avoid standing near walls where enemies can surround you.
  • Do not scope unless your immediate area is secure.
Your rifle gives you range. Use that range without giving up the protection of the squad.

14. Common Mistakes  

❌ Shooting every zombie

Kill everything but….
Prioritize heavy units, elites, and bosses.

❌ Wandering away from the squad

Your rifle does not make you immune to swarms.
Stay with the team.

❌ Scoping in an unsafe location

Scope dramatically reduces your awareness.
Position first. Scope second.

❌ Using Canister near teammates

Canister is an explosive.
It can teamkill and destroy items.

❌ Shooting bosses without thinking

Sometimes the squad needs to know where the boss is more than they need the boss damaged.
Ping and communicate before committing to a kill.

❌ Wasting Recoil

Don't use Recoil on weak enemies.
Save it for bosses and dangerous elites.

❌ Ignoring Venom

Reduced vision and accuracy are particularly damaging to Marksman.
Carry Anti-Venom and cure yourself immediately.

❌ Walking while scoped

It is easy to accidentally click somewhere and move your Marine while your camera is focused elsewhere.
Use Disable Movement Command (X+D) when necessary.

15. Quick Reference 

If you only remember one section, remember this:
Marksman is the squad's long-range heavy-unit killer.
Core classes come first.
Stay with the squad.
Prioritize shooting heavy units, elites, and bosses.
Every zombie killed by Marksman stays dead.
Use your rifle to save Demolition explosives.
Ping bosses and important enemies so the squad knows where they are.
Use Scope to scout and provide vision, especially for Wave 12 drone modules.
Use Recoil on bosses and dangerous elites.
Use Canister for corpses, trees, bosses and Nyduses, but never near teammates or important items.
Level 4 Canister is an important breakpoint for Wave 14 Nyduses.
Carry Armor + Ballistic Ammo + Anti-Venom.
Ballistic is the preferred ammo modifier; Uranium is a good alternative.
Do not scope while exposed.
Protect Demolition when they leave the base.
During Wave 14, alternate Canister shots with another Marksman if possible.
On the hardest waves, your job is to kill dangerous enemies before they reach the squad.
The Marksman is the squad's silent cannon. You see the enemy before they see you, put a round through the things that matter, provide vision when the squad needs it, and quietly remove the biggest threats on the battlefield. You may not always get noticed—but if you're doing your job right, the squad will feel the difference.
